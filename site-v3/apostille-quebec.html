How to get an apostille in Quebec (2026 guide)

Guide · Published 26 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

An apostille in Quebec is issued by the Ministère de la Justice du Québec for documents issued in Quebec, and requests go in by mail. As of 26 November 2026 the fee is CA$66.50 per document. Quebec issues its apostilles in French only.

Key points

  • Quebec apostilles only documents issued in Quebec. Federal, territorial and other provincial documents go elsewhere.
  • A private document needs an official certificate from a Quebec lawyer or notary, plus a signature authentication from their professional order.
  • As of 26 November 2026, Quebec charges CA$66.50 per document and takes requests by mail to its Montreal office.
  • Anyone can check a Quebec apostille issued on or after January 11, 2024 in the online apostille register.

Who issues apostilles in Quebec?

The Ministère de la Justice du Québec issues apostilles for documents issued in Quebec, through its Délivrance des apostilles office in Montreal. Quebec's apostille register describes the apostilles it checks as those issued by the ministry, and fees are paid to the ministry's Register Fund. Quebec's application page also links to the province's Act respecting Apostilles.

Quebec says it doesn't issue apostilles for documents from a federal authority, a territory or another province. Global Affairs Canada adds that in Quebec, the competent authority can authenticate a notarized document only if the original was issued in the province. Our guide to getting an apostille in Canada covers the other authorities, and apostille vs notarization explains how the two steps differ.

The Apostille Convention entered into force for Canada on January 11, 2024, according to the Hague Conference status table, which listed 130 contracting parties at its June 30, 2026 update. Quebec also issues apostilles for countries outside the Convention, but says those countries may still require legalization.

Which documents can be apostilled in Quebec?

Quebec apostilles originals, or copies certified true by the person or body that produced them, of four kinds of document: public body documents, court documents, authentic acts such as notarized documents, and documents from a private person or body with an establishment in Quebec. An apostille confirms the document's origin and the signature or seal on it. It doesn't certify the content.

Quebec's page on bodies that issue public documents says you must get each document from the body authorized to issue it. This table summarizes that page:

Some documents can't be apostilled because of their format. Quebec gives a driver's licence, a social insurance number and a passport as examples, and returns them by regular mail without an apostille.

When is a lawyer's or notary's official certificate needed?

A document from a person or body that isn't a public body must be officially certified by a Quebec lawyer or notary before Quebec will apostille it. Quebec gives a single status affidavit, a consent letter for children travelling abroad, a baptismal certificate and a power of attorney as examples.

The notarized document or the official certificate must come with a signature authentication from the professional order, which is the Barreau du Québec for lawyers and the Chambre des notaires du Québec for notaries. Quebec also says commissioners for oaths can't make copies or administer oaths for affidavits related to documents that need an apostille.

A document in a language other than French or English also needs a lawyer's or notary's official certificate. The lawyer or notary must declare that they understand the document, or that they obtained a translation from a member of the Ordre des traducteurs, terminologues et interprètes agréés du Québec (OTTIAQ).

How do you get the signature authentication?

For a lawyer, the Barreau du Québec Registry Office authenticates the signature. You email a copy of the signed document, and the office checks the signature against the sample in the lawyer's professional record and confirms the lawyer is or was on the Roll. As of 26 November 2026, the Barreau charges CA$50 plus taxes and sends the original certificate electronically within five business days of receiving the request and payment.

For a notarial act, Quebec says to get a certified true copy from the notary who made the original act. You then get an authenticity certificate from the Chambre des notaires du Québec confirming that the notary is a member and signed the document.

How do I apply for an apostille in Quebec?

You fill out Quebec's online apostille request form, print it and mail it with your documents. Before you start, Quebec asks you to have the recipient country, your contact details, a payment method and the documents on hand, and to check that each document's issue date meets the recipient's requirements.

  1. Confirm each document is issued in Quebec and qualifies, using the table above.
  2. Get any lawyer's or notary's certificate and the matching signature authentication.
  3. Ask the recipient whether a translation must also be apostilled. If it must, the translation needs a lawyer's or notary's official certificate too.
  4. Fill out the form and choose credit card, money order or bank draft.
  5. Print the form, without stapling it to any document.
  6. Keep the documents for each apostille together, and use an envelope large enough to avoid damage.
  7. Mail everything to Délivrance des apostilles, 276, rue Saint-Jacques, bureau 301, Montréal (Québec) H2Y 1N3.

Quebec cancels any application whose documents aren't received within 3 months, and you then need a new application. A federal document with a translation by an OTTIAQ member is apostilled by Global Affairs Canada.

How much does it cost and how long does it take?

As of 26 November 2026, Quebec charges CA$66.50 for each document, so a request with three documents costs CA$199.50. You can pay online by credit card, or by money order or bank draft payable to the Register Fund of the Ministère de la Justice. Quebec doesn't accept personal cheques.

Quebec publishes the receipt date of the applications it is working on. On its apostille page updated November 24, 2026, it was processing applications received on November 24, 2026, and it says times vary with volume. Processing starts once all required documents reach the office, so mail time comes on top.

Apostilled documents come back by regular mail without a tracking number. For a courier or Canada Post expedited return, include a prepaid shipping label and an envelope of at least 8.5 x 11 inches at your own expense.

How do I verify a Quebec apostille?

Quebec's apostille register checks apostilles issued by a Quebec competent authority on or after January 11, 2024. You enter the apostille's issue date and number, both of which appear on the apostille issued by the Ministère de la Justice.

Quebec lists these reasons for refusing a request:

  • the document was issued too long ago to be validated;
  • the document is from outside Quebec;
  • the document has been altered;
  • the document is glued or laminated;
  • the document isn't an original or a true copy.

Quebec corporate documents

Certificates from the Registraire des entreprises du Québec, such as a certificate of regularity, appear on Quebec's list of documents it can apostille. Company records you prepare yourself, like resolutions or a power of attorney, are private documents and need a lawyer's or notary's official certificate first.

If a Quebec document is notarized in Ontario, Global Affairs Canada says it goes to Ontario's competent authority, wherever it was issued. Our guide to apostilling corporate documents covers what foreign banks and regulators ask for. Our apostille and notarization service coordinates corporate documents only, with notarization by a licensed Ontario partner notary. A certified true copy is a separate step that our guide explains.

Apostille in Quebec: common questions

How do I get a document apostilled in Quebec?

Get the document from the Quebec body that issued it. If it is a private document, add a Quebec lawyer's or notary's official certificate plus a signature authentication from the Barreau du Québec or the Chambre des notaires du Québec (a notarial act needs a certified true copy and a Chambre des notaires authenticity certificate). Then fill out Quebec's online form and mail the printed form with the documents and payment to the Délivrance des apostilles office in Montreal.

Where is the Quebec apostille request form?

Quebec's application page links to an online form at apostille.justice.gouv.qc.ca. You fill it out, choose a payment method, print it and mail it with your documents.

How much is an apostille in Quebec?

As of 26 November 2026, CA$66.50 per document, payable by credit card online, money order or bank draft. Quebec doesn't accept personal cheques.

Can Quebec apostille a federal or Ontario document?

No. Quebec says it doesn't apostille documents issued by a federal authority, a territory or another province. Federal documents go to Global Affairs Canada, and Ontario documents to Ontario.

Which countries accept a Quebec apostille?

Countries where the Apostille Convention is in force. The Hague Conference listed 130 contracting parties at its June 30, 2026 update, including Thailand, where the Convention takes effect on February 28, 2027. Quebec also issues apostilles for other countries, which may still require legalization.
