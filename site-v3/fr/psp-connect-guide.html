PSP Connect: a practical guide for payment service providers

Guide · Published 3 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

PSP Connect is the Bank of Canada's online portal where payment service providers register, file their RPAA reports and notices, and receive messages from supervisors. As of 3 November 2026, this guide explains who can use your account, how messages and the primary contact work, what you file there and by when, and where to go when the portal misbehaves.

Points clés

  • The regulations require the application, annual report, incident notices, significant change notices and registration information changes to go through PSP Connect.
  • Organization administrators add and remove users. Bank staff said a PSP can have more than one, and the primary contact is changed through the Service Desk.
  • Every authorized user can see the account's messages, but automated messages still go to the primary contact, so keep that person current.
  • Use the Service Desk for technical problems and your usual regulatory channel for policy questions, and check the sign-in banner first.

What is PSP Connect used for?

PSP Connect is the electronic system the Bank of Canada provides for payment service providers (PSPs) to apply for registration and meet their reporting duties under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). It sits at rps.bankofcanada.ca, and the Bank's supervisory framework describes it as the PSP's main channel for communicating with the Bank.

The Retail Payment Activities Regulations (RPAR) require these filings to go through the electronic system the Bank provides:

  • the registration application (RPAR s. 24(1));
  • incident notices to the Bank (RPAR s. 11(1));
  • the annual report (RPAR s. 18(2));
  • notices of significant change or new activity (RPAR s. 20(1)(b));
  • notices of change in registration information (RPAR s. 35(b)).

The Bank's June 29, 2026 reminder repeats that all reports and notifications go through the portal.

Who can access a PSP Connect account?

Any individual the PSP authorizes can use its account, including employees and third-party contractors, once they register and accept the terms of use. The person who creates the account is treated as the organization's administrator, according to the Bank's registration step-by-step guide, and other people are added from "User Profile" under "My account".

The Bank's authorized user access bulletin (November 2024) sets out five roles:

  • Org admin. Can do everything in the account and can add a user in any role.
  • Registration. Submits the application, changes application or registration information, and requests a Governor's review of a refusal or revocation.
  • Risk. Submits annual reports, incident notices and significant change notices, and handles assessment communications.
  • Enforcement. Requests a Governor's review of a notice of violation or notice of default and reports progress under a compliance agreement.
  • Authorized payment. Submits payment details for an administrative monetary penalty and signs up for pre-authorized debit.

Bank staff said at the Bank's 2 November 2026 supervision update that adding and removing users is handled by the PSP's own organization administrator, and that a PSP can have more than one. The PSP Connect terms of use require you to deactivate a user who is no longer authorized. They also make the PSP responsible for what its users do in the account.

Who is the primary contact, and how do you change it?

The primary contact is the person who receives the Bank's automated messages from PSP Connect. Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 session that you change the primary contact by contacting the Service Desk, adding that "it's a quick change on our end."

Email delivery also matters for enforcement notices. Section 51 of the RPAR lets the Bank serve a notice under Part 5 of the Act, such as a notice of violation, through its electronic system. It posts the notice there and emails the PSP's last known email address. The notice is deemed served on the day that email is delivered. Review requests are due within 30 days, so a mailbox nobody reads can eat into that window.

Change the primary contact as soon as that person leaves or changes role, and add the sender named in the registration guide, rps-spd@em5388.rps-spd.bank-banque-canada.ca, to your approved senders. Incident notices have their own contact: the incident notice guide says each one names a primary contact picked from your PSP Connect contacts, and only an administrator can add new contact details.

Who can see messages in the Message Centre?

All authorized users on a PSP's account can see its portal messages, and any of them can reply through the Message Centre. The Bank's November 2024 bulletin already said every authorized user can view all information in the account, including communications from the Bank. At the 2 November 2026 session, Bank staff described broader access to portal messages as a recent change.

Staff noted one exception: the portal's automated notifications reach the primary contact alone. During an assessment, staff said, follow-up questions may come as written messages in the Message Centre, and reminders about the annual report also arrive as portal messages.

The bulletin says users act in the account without notifying the other users on it. Decide in writing who answers Bank messages and who covers when that person is away.

In PSP Connect you file the registration application, registration information changes, significant change notices, incident notices, the annual report and prescribed review requests, and a billing section is on the way. When should you contact the Service Desk instead of your supervisors?

Contact the Service Desk when the portal itself isn't working, and use your usual regulatory channel when your question is about the rules. That is how Bank staff divided the two at the 2 November 2026 session. They said the Service Desk's details and business hours are on the PSP Connect sign-in page. Policy or guidance questions should keep going to your usual Bank contact.

  • Service Desk: sign-in or access problems, error messages, forms that won't submit or save, and changing the primary contact.
  • Your organization administrator: adding a user, removing one or changing someone's role.
  • Regulatory channels: whether a change is significant, what a guideline means, or how to answer a request for information.

The Bank's general inquiries page has an online form for technical issues with PSP Connect, and the terms of use direct questions about the portal to rps-compliance@bank-banque-canada.ca. Every user can also write through "Contact us" inside the portal. If a portal problem threatens a deadline, keep the ticket number and screenshots, because the deadline in the regulations still applies.

What known PSP Connect issues should you watch for?

Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 session that the bug blocking registration information change submissions is fixed, and that a fix for cloning previously submitted information is coming. For anything else, check the announcement banner, which appears when you sign in. It carries planned downtime, known issues and workarounds, and if something isn't working, "it's worth checking the announcement banner."

Staff said the submission bug had affected some PSPs for a few months. The form now shows the specific changes you're making before you submit, instead of your whole registration record. Cloning, which copies in everything you submitted before, doesn't yet work for all PSPs.

Staff added that the metrics screens for the next annual report are being reworked to cut manual entry, and that internal processes were streamlined after a support backlog earlier in 2026. As of 3 November 2026, none of these items appears in a published Bank notice. Until the cloning fix lands, check every pre-filled field against your own records before you submit a change.

How should a PSP set up PSP Connect before the 2027 annual report?

Fix access problems now, before the March 31, 2027 deadline:

  • Name at least two organization administrators, so one absence doesn't lock you out of user changes.
  • Give each person only the role they need, using the five roles in the Bank's bulletin.
  • Confirm the primary contact is a current employee who reads Bank messages, and ask the Service Desk to change it if not.
  • Make sure whoever will prepare the annual report has the Risk role well before the form opens.
  • Add a step to your offboarding process to deactivate portal users on their last day.
  • Put every deadline from the table above into one compliance calendar, with an owner for each.

If you haven't registered yet, our RPAA registration guide covers the application itself. Our RPAA compliance program includes a reporting calendar and change-notice procedures that map to these filings.

PSP Connect guide: common questions

How do I add a user to PSP Connect?

An organization administrator adds the user from "User Profile" under "My account". The Bank's November 2024 access bulletin says an Org Admin can add an authorized user in any role, and the new user must register and accept the terms of use.

Can a PSP have more than one PSP Connect administrator?

Yes. Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 supervision update that a PSP can have more than one organization administrator.

How do I change the primary contact in PSP Connect?

Contact the PSP Connect Service Desk. Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 session that the Service Desk makes this change, and that automated messages from the portal go only to the primary contact until it's updated.

Who do I contact about a PSP Connect technical problem?

The Service Desk, whose details and hours are on the PSP Connect sign-in page, according to Bank staff. Policy questions go through your usual regulatory channel instead.

Can a consultant file in PSP Connect for my company?

A consultant can be an authorized user, but the account must be the PSP's own. The Bank's FAQ says PSPs create their own account and can list consultants in Delegate Profiles, and the terms of use make the PSP responsible for what its users do.

Le rapport annuel des fournisseurs de services de paiement en vertu de la LAPD

Tout fournisseur de services de paiement qui exerce des activités associées aux paiements de détail au cours d’une année civile doit déposer un rapport annuel auprès de la Banque du Canada au plus tard le 31 mars de l’année suivante. Le rapport porte sur votre cadre de gestion des risques et d’intervention en cas d’incident, la protection des fonds, les indicateurs d’activité et les résultats financiers.

Lire le guide LAPD

Déclaration des incidents en vertu de la LAPD et règle des 48 heures

Lorsqu’un fournisseur de services de paiement prend connaissance d’un incident ayant une incidence importante, il doit en aviser sans délai la Banque du Canada ainsi que les utilisateurs finaux, les fournisseurs de services de paiement et les chambres de compensation touchés. La Banque s’attend à recevoir le premier avis au plus tard 48 heures après que le fournisseur a déterminé que l’incident est important.

Lire le guide LAPD

Qu’est-ce que la LAPD?

La Loi sur les activités associées aux paiements de détail est la loi fédérale en vertu de laquelle la Banque du Canada supervise les fournisseurs de services de paiement. Elle fixe des règles sur le risque opérationnel, les incidents et la protection des fonds des utilisateurs finaux.
