RPAA incident reporting and the 48-hour rule

Guide · Published 21 October 2026 · 5 min read · AML Canada

When a payment service provider becomes aware of an incident with a material impact, it must notify the Bank of Canada and the affected end users, payment service providers and clearing houses without delay. The Bank expects the first notice no later than 48 hours after the provider decides the incident is material.

Key points

  • Only incidents with a material impact are reported, but every incident must be investigated and documented.
  • The 48 hours run from when you determine an incident is material, not from when it started.
  • File an initial notice and a final notice in PSP Connect, with interim notices as things develop.
  • Notify affected end users directly. A social media post alone isn't enough.

What the law requires

Section 18 of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) says that if a payment service provider (PSP) becomes aware of an incident that has a material impact on an end user, another PSP or a designated clearing house, it must, without delay, notify that individual or entity and the Bank of Canada. The other PSP doesn't have to be one the RPAA applies to. Sections 11 and 12 of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations set out the form and content of the notices. The Bank can also order a PSP to give follow-up notices (RPAA section 19).

The Bank explains how it expects PSPs to comply in its Incident notification guideline (October 2024) and its step-by-step guide to the notice form (May 2025).

What counts as an incident

The Act defines an incident as an unplanned event, or series of related events, that results in, or could reasonably be expected to result in, the reduction, deterioration or breakdown of any retail payment activity the PSP performs. The Bank reads this to include any negative impact on the confidentiality, integrity or availability of your retail payment activities, or of the systems, data and information behind them.

Incidents at your agents, mandataries and third-party service providers count, as do incidents they detect. If one of them has a material impact, you report it.

When an incident has a material impact

Only material incidents are reportable under section 18. The guideline leaves the judgment to you, based on your business model, the services you offer and other relevant factors. It gives examples, which it says aren't exhaustive:

  • Lost funds. Any amount of an end user's held funds becomes unrecoverably lost or permanently unavailable, for example through theft or a system flaw at the PSP, or because the institution holding the funds fails. Losses caused by the end user's own actions, such as authorized fraud, are not included.
  • Outages. An outage that materially affects the availability of your retail payment activities, such as one caused by a technology failure, loss of a data centre, hosting or third party, or a cyber attack.
  • Insolvency. The PSP becomes subject to an insolvency event listed in the regulations.
  • Confidentiality breaches. Unauthorized access to or disclosure of confidential information that creates a real risk of significant harm, such as financial loss, identity theft, damage to reputation or bodily harm. This covers more than personal information. Weigh how sensitive the information is and how likely it is to be misused.
  • Integrity failures. A compromised ledger or transaction records, a payee receiving a different amount than the payer sent, funds routed to the wrong account, misdirected instructions, or errors in clearing or settlement.

Incidents that aren't material don't need a section 18 notice. You still have to investigate, respond to and document every incident, and the Bank may ask about them. Your annual report also counts all incidents by type. See our guide to the RPAA annual report.

The 48-hour clock

The guideline sets the deadline as without delay, but no later than 48 hours after the PSP determines the incident is material. The Bank's June 2026 reminder repeats it. If an incident starts out below the threshold and gets worse, the 48 hours run from the point you determine it has become material.

The notice form asks separately when the incident started, when it was detected and when you became aware it was material. Record all three as they happen. Build your escalation process so that someone with authority makes the materiality decision promptly, since the duty is to notify without delay.

Notifying the Bank: initial, interim and final notices

Notices go to the Bank through PSP Connect, under Reporting, then Incident. The guideline describes three kinds:

  • Initial notice, within the 48 hours, for every material incident.
  • Interim notices, when there are significant developments or useful updates. You can file more than one.
  • Final notice, once the incident is resolved and the root cause is known.

If the incident is resolved and you have everything, including the root cause, within the 48 hours, one notice can serve as both initial and final. If your systems are so degraded that you can't file online, the Bank accepts notices by phone in exceptional circumstances.

Under section 11 of the regulations, the notice must give your name and a contact person, a description of the incident and its material impact, and the measures you've taken. The guideline expects dates and times for start, detection, the materiality decision and resolution; how the incident was detected; which retail payment activities were affected; the actual or estimated impact, such as the number of people affected; the impact on systems and data; and the root cause analysis.

Notifying end users, other PSPs and clearing houses

Section 12 of the regulations requires you to notify each materially affected individual or entity using the most recent contact information they gave you. If you don't have contact details for everyone affected, you post the notice on your website. The guideline expects these notices within the same 48 hours.

  • Email, text message or your app are suitable. A post on social media isn't, although you can use social media as well.
  • The notice gives your name, a description of the incident with when it began, its impact on the person notified, and any steps they can take, such as changing a password.
  • If an incident at PSP A affects PSP B, A notifies B and its own affected users, and B then notifies its users if the incident is material for them.
  • If notifying users within 48 hours could increase the risk of harm or the incident, or isn't feasible, tell the Bank through the incident template. The Bank expects this to be rare.

Privacy law and clearing house rules

A breach that is reportable under privacy law is still reportable to the Bank under the RPAA. You can send affected people a single notice if it meets the requirements of every law that applies. Members of clearing and settlement systems may also have reporting duties under the system's rules. Meeting one set of obligations doesn't satisfy the other, and all material incidents still go to the Bank.

After the notice

The Bank can order follow-up notices, setting who receives them, when, and what they say. It can also send an information request. Normally you have 15 days to answer, but for an ongoing incident that could have a significant adverse impact on end users, other PSPs or certain clearing houses, the regulations allow only 24 hours (section 43).

The notices depend on your risk management and incident response framework, which must allocate roles for detecting, responding to and recovering from incidents. Our RPAA compliance program includes an incident response framework with escalation steps, a materiality assessment and notice templates. If you hold end-user funds, read it with our safeguarding guide, since lost or inaccessible funds are a material incident.

RPAA incident reporting: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

How long does a PSP have to report an incident to the Bank of Canada?

Without delay, and no later than 48 hours after it determines that the incident has a material impact on an end user, another PSP or a designated clearing house.

Do all incidents have to be reported under the RPAA?

No. Only incidents with a material impact need a section 18 notice. Every incident must still be investigated, responded to and documented, and all incidents are counted in the annual report.

How do you submit an RPAA incident notice?

Through PSP Connect, using the Bank's incident notice form. You file an initial notice, interim notices as needed, and a final notice once the incident is resolved and the root cause is known.

Does a PSP have to tell customers about an incident?

Yes, if they're materially affected. Notify them directly using their latest contact details, or post a notice on your website if you don't have contact details for everyone.

Do incidents at a third-party provider count?

Yes. Incidents that occur at or are detected by your agents, mandataries or third-party service providers are reportable if they have a material impact.

The RPAA annual report for payment service providers

Every payment service provider that performs retail payment activities in a calendar year must file an annual report with the Bank of Canada by March 31 of the following year. It covers your risk management and incident response framework, safeguarding, activity metrics and financial results.

RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds

Since September 8, 2025, a payment service provider that holds funds for its users must safeguard them, either in trust in a trust account or in a segregated account backed by insurance or a guarantee. It also needs a written safeguarding framework and an independent review at least every three years.
