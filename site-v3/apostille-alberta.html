How to get an apostille in Alberta

Guide · Published 26 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

To get an apostille in Alberta, you mail or courier your documents to Official Documents and Appointments in Edmonton with a request form and an online payment receipt. As of 26 November 2026, Alberta charges CA$25 per document, offers no walk-in service and asks you to allow at least 7 to 10 business days.

Key points

  • Alberta's Official Documents and Appointments office apostilles documents issued in Alberta and any document notarized in Alberta.
  • Corporate records, powers of attorney and similar documents need a notarized copy from an Alberta notary public.
  • As of 26 November 2026, the fee is CA$25 per document, paid online through Alberta eServices before you mail anything.
  • The front counter is closed, and rush, walk-in and while-you-wait services aren't available.

Who issues apostilles in Alberta?

Apostilles in Alberta are issued by Official Documents and Appointments, an office of Alberta's Ministry of Justice in Edmonton, as the province's Apostille Request Form shows. Which authority handles your document depends on where it was issued and where it was notarized. Global Affairs Canada's requirements set these rules:

Your documentWho apostilles it
Issued in Alberta, with an original signature or sealAlberta
Notarized by an Alberta notary, wherever it was issuedAlberta
Notarized in Ontario or Saskatchewan, even if issued in AlbertaThat province
Issued by the Government of Canada and not notarizedGlobal Affairs Canada
RCMP criminal record checkGlobal Affairs Canada, according to Alberta's page

An apostille certifies the public official's signature, the capacity in which they acted and, where relevant, the seal or stamp on the document, as Alberta's document authentication page explains. It doesn't validate what the document says. The Apostille Convention entered into force for Canada on January 11, 2024, according to the Hague Conference status table. Our guide to apostille vs notarization explains how the two steps differ.

Which documents can Alberta apostille?

Alberta lists birth, marriage and death certificates, education records, notarized documents, export records and corporate records among the public documents it can apostille. Each type has a condition it must meet first. As of 26 November 2026, Alberta's page sets these:

DocumentWhat Alberta needs before it will apostille
Alberta birth certificateAn original on polymer paper, issued in Alberta after February 1, 2008
Alberta marriage or death certificateAn original issued in Alberta after January 1, 1999; no laminated or wallet-size versions
Certificate of DivorceThe original signature of the clerk of the court and an impressed court seal
Education recordsCertified by an official of the institution as an official record or true copy, with a wet signature and embossed seal
Criminal record check not issued by the RCMPNotarized, then sent to Alberta
Power of attorney, certificate of life, export or corporate recordsA notarized copy from an Alberta notary public only, signed, sealed and dated

If a Vital Statistics document is laminated or wallet size, Alberta says to order an updated original from a registry agent. Documents over 10 pages must be double-sided. Our guide to apostilling Canadian corporate documents covers which company records a foreign bank or regulator usually asks for.

Who can notarize a document for an Alberta apostille?

Alberta asks for corporate records, powers of attorney and similar documents to be notarized by an Alberta notary public only. Under the Notaries and Commissioners Act, every Alberta notary can certify and attest a true copy of a document and witness the execution of a document, according to Alberta's notaries public page. Judges, lawyers and students-at-law are notaries because of their office.

An Alberta notary's seal must show the notary's name, the words "Notary Public" and the words "Province of Alberta". A commissioner for oaths can't act as a notary public in Alberta, according to the province's commissioners for oaths page. Our guide to commissioners of oaths and notaries explains the difference, and our guide to certified true copies covers who can certify a copy.

Where a document notarized outside Alberta goes depends on the province. Global Affairs Canada says a document notarized in Ontario or Saskatchewan goes to that province, one notarized in Manitoba, the Atlantic provinces or the territories goes to Global Affairs Canada, and in British Columbia and Quebec the competent authority authenticates a notarized document only if the original was issued in that province. Our guide to British Columbia covers the B.C. rules.

Can I walk in or get an apostille online?

Neither, as of 26 November 2026. Alberta's front counter is closed and the office doesn't accept documents for authentication in person. Rush, walk-in and while-you-wait services aren't available, so every request goes by mail or courier.

The only online step is payment. Alberta's Certificates of Apostille eServices page takes the fee, and you must sign in with an Alberta.ca Account or an Alberta.ca Account for Organizations to use it. The paper documents still have to reach Edmonton.

How much does an apostille cost in Alberta?

As of 26 November 2026, an Alberta apostille costs CA$25 per document. You make one payment per submission, so five documents cost CA$125. Alberta says not to send cash. It emails a receipt once the payment is processed, and you print it and include it with your documents as proof of payment.

The eServices rules say a missing receipt will delay the request, and an overpayment is refunded in the form of payment you used. Global Affairs Canada charges no fee for the documents it handles, as its requirements page states. Notary, courier and translation costs are separate.

How to send an Alberta apostille request

Alberta's process has three steps: gather your documents, pay online, and mail everything with the receipt. This checklist puts every instruction from Alberta's page and the request form in order:

  1. Check each document against Alberta's requirements, and have any notarized copy made by an Alberta notary.
  2. Complete the Apostille Request Form as your cover sheet. It asks for your contact details, a shipping address if different, each document type with the number of documents and the country of use, and your eServices payment order number and date paid.
  3. Pay CA$25 per document online and print the emailed receipt.
  4. Arrange the return. For a courier return, include a prepaid electronic waybill. For Canada Post, include a prepaid Priority Courier or Xpresspost envelope with the return address filled in legibly and in full.
  5. Keep any tracking number, then mail or courier the form, documents and receipt to Official Documents and Appointments, #111, 9833 109 Street, Edmonton, Alberta T5K 2E8.

Alberta doesn't accept handwritten waybills, and it returns those packages by Canada Post without notice. A return to an address outside Canada needs a prepaid electronic waybill. If you make your own cover sheet, include your name, telephone number, email address, return address and the country where the document will be used. The office's phone number is 780-427-5069.

How long does an Alberta apostille take?

As of 26 November 2026, Alberta asks you to allow at least 7 to 10 business days for processing. Documents are processed in the order the office receives them. Because of high volumes, Alberta says it can't provide status updates, and Global Affairs Canada only gives updates on requests sent to its own office.

If Alberta can't apostille a document, it returns the document and explains why in writing. Unless you included a prepaid envelope or courier waybill, documents come back by Canada Post. A recipient abroad can check an Alberta apostille with Global Affairs Canada's verification tool, which since June 20, 2025 covers apostilles issued by Global Affairs Canada, Alberta, Saskatchewan and British Columbia, according to its notices page.

Countries outside the Convention and translations

Alberta issues an apostille for all documents, even when the destination country hasn't signed the Apostille Convention. A document going to a non-member country may still need legalization by that country's foreign representative office, and Alberta tells applicants to contact the country's representative in Canada. Alberta's page doesn't set translation rules, so ask the receiving party whether it needs a translation and whether that translation needs an apostille too.

Our national guide to getting an apostille in Canada compares every competent authority. For corporate documents, our apostille and notarization service coordinates the process, with notarization by a licensed Ontario partner notary. Because that notary is in Ontario, Global Affairs Canada's rule sends those documents to Ontario's competent authority. Personal documents such as birth certificates and degrees are outside that service.

Apostille in Alberta: common questions

Can I walk in to get an apostille in Alberta?

No. As of 26 November 2026, Alberta's front counter is closed, and rush, walk-in and while-you-wait services aren't available. Documents must be sent by mail or courier.

Can I do my Alberta apostille online?

Only the payment. You pay through Alberta eServices, which needs an Alberta.ca Account. The request form, documents and printed receipt still have to be mailed or couriered to Edmonton.

How much does an apostille cost in Alberta?

As of 26 November 2026, CA$25 per document, paid in one online payment per submission. Five documents cost CA$125.

How long does an Alberta apostille take?

Alberta asks you to allow at least 7 to 10 business days for processing. It processes documents in the order received and can't provide status updates.

Can Alberta apostille a document notarized in another province?

Alberta asks for notarized copies from an Alberta notary public only. Global Affairs Canada says a document notarized in Ontario or Saskatchewan goes to that province's competent authority.
