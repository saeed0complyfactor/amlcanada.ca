Together they cover the policies, procedures, risk assessment, training and review planning behind the five required parts of a FINTRAC compliance program, plus your risk appetite and fraud risk management.

The five parts of a compliance program

FINTRAC's guidance sets out five required elements:

  1. A compliance officer, appointed to be responsible for implementing the program.
  2. Written policies and procedures, kept up to date and, for an entity, approved by a senior officer.
  3. A risk assessment of the business's money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risks, documented.
  4. A written, ongoing compliance training program, with a plan for delivering it, if you have employees, agents or others authorized to act for you.
  5. A review of effectiveness of the policies and procedures, risk assessment and training program, at least every two years.

Our guide to FINTRAC compliance program requirements explains what FINTRAC expects in each part.

What your policies and procedures cover

  • knowing your client: verifying identity, beneficial ownership, and politically exposed persons and heads of international organizations;
  • third-party determinations and business relationships;
  • ongoing monitoring, and the enhanced measures you apply to high-risk clients;
  • sanctions and listed person screening;
  • reporting to FINTRAC, including suspicious transactions, and the 24-hour rule;
  • record keeping;
  • ministerial directives, and how you'll apply new ones;
  • travel rule information for funds and virtual currency transfers, where it applies.

The exact list depends on the kind of business you are and the services you offer, which is why we start with how you operate.

Your risk assessment

The rest of your program depends on the risk assessment. It looks at your money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risks: your clients and business relationships, your products, services and delivery channels, the places you operate in and deal with, new developments and technologies, and the risks for your sector set out in Canada's latest National Risk Assessment of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing. Higher-risk clients and products get closer controls.

If your bank or payment partner has asked for your program

Banks and payment partners usually want to see that your program exists, fits your business, has a named compliance officer, and is followed day to day. If you've been asked and don't have one, or aren't sure yours will stand up, book a consultation. We'll tell you what's missing and how quickly it can be put right.

Keeping it current

A program written once and left on a shelf goes out of date as your business and the rules change. We can keep it current through outsourced compliance officer hours, and test it in your two-year effectiveness review.

Compliance programs: questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

What does an AML compliance program include in Canada?

Five parts: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan if you have employees, agents or others acting for you, and an effectiveness review at least every two years.

How much does a compliance program cost?

A fixed US$3,500 for 14 PCMLTFA compliance program documents, from the AML/ATF Compliance Policy to the Fraud Risk Management Policy.

Do you use templates?

We use a consistent structure, and the content is written for your products, customers and team. A program that doesn't describe how your business works won't hold up in an examination.

Does the fee include the effectiveness review?

No. The program includes a plan for your review. The review itself is a separate, scoped piece of work.

Can you keep the program up to date afterwards?

Yes, through outsourced compliance officer hours at US$1,500 per 10 hours.

Our bank has asked for our AML program. What now?

Book a consultation. We'll look at what you have, tell you what's missing and agree a written scope to close the gaps.

Virtual currency

Ontario vs B.C. MSB registration: which province suits a crypto startup?

Today there's no MSB difference between the two provinces. A money services business in Ontario or British Columbia registers with FINTRAC and nobody else, because B.C.'s Money Services Businesses Act has been passed but isn't in force. That should change: B.C. plans to start provincial registration, and Ontario has no equivalent law.

The travel rule and ministerial directives: what Canadian MSBs must do

Canada's travel rule requires money services businesses to send originator and beneficiary information with funds and virtual currency transfers, and to check it arrives with transfers they receive. Ministerial directives add stricter measures for transactions connected to Russia, Iran and North Korea.
