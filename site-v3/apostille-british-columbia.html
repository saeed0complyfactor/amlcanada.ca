Apostille in BC: how to get a British Columbia apostille

Guide · Published 26 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

An apostille in BC comes from the B.C. Authentication Program in Victoria, which authenticates British Columbia public documents for use outside Canada. As of 26 November 2026 the fee is CA$20 per document, requests go by mail or courier only, and the published processing time is 2 to 4 weeks plus mail time.

Key points

  • B.C. apostilles B.C. public documents. It won't apostille a document issued in another province, even if it was notarized in B.C.
  • A notarized document must be signed and sealed by a B.C. lawyer or notary already on file with the B.C. Authentication Program.
  • There is no walk-in or rush service. The request form is online, the fee can be paid online by card or mailed with the documents, and the paper documents go by mail or courier.
  • As of 26 November 2026, B.C. charges CA$20 per document and quotes 2 to 4 weeks, not including mail time.

Who issues apostilles in British Columbia?

The B.C. Authentication Program issues apostilles for British Columbia public documents. It is run by the Order in Council (OIC) Administration Office in the Ministry of Attorney General, where the Registrar or Deputy Registrar, Official Documents, authenticates each document, according to the province's About Authentication page.

The Apostille Convention came into effect in Canada on January 11, 2024. B.C. attaches an apostille certificate to every document it has authenticated since that date. It confirms the signature, seal and signer's authority, and B.C. says it doesn't certify the contents.

Our guide to getting an apostille in Canada shows which of the six Canadian authorities handles documents from each province.

Which documents can be apostilled in BC?

B.C. authenticates B.C. public documents and certain federal documents notarized in B.C., for use outside Canada. It won't authenticate a Canadian document issued in another province or territory, even if it was notarized in B.C. The program can't authenticate foreign documents or documents for use in Canada, according to its eligibility rules.

Does a document need a BC notary first?

Only some documents need notarizing, and vital statistics certificates must not be. Where notarization is needed, the notary must be a B.C. notary public who is already on file with the program. All B.C. lawyers are notaries public, and B.C. also has non-lawyer notaries, according to the notarized documents page.

The notary must sign, date and seal the document as a notary. B.C. won't authenticate a document signed or sealed as a commissioner for taking affidavits. See our guide to commissioners of oaths and notaries. The province publishes a monthly list of lawyers and notaries on file.

If your notary isn't on file

The province's page for B.C. lawyers and notaries gives three options:

  • the notary applies to go on file, which can take a few weeks;
  • for B.C. lawyers only, the Law Society of British Columbia attaches its own certificate first, which can take 2 to 3 weeks;
  • the document is notarized again by a lawyer or notary already on file.

Federal documents and notaries outside B.C.

B.C. accepts certain federal documents notarized in B.C., such as an RCMP criminal record check and Canada Pension Plan or Canada Revenue Agency documents. As a temporary courtesy it accepts any federal document notarized in B.C., and warns this may change. B.C. verifies only the notary, so if the destination wants the federal signature verified, the original goes to Global Affairs Canada.

Global Affairs Canada says B.C. can authenticate a notarized document only if the original was issued in B.C., so check before sending a federal document. It also says a document notarized in Alberta, Ontario or Saskatchewan goes to that province, wherever it was issued.

How do you get an apostille in BC?

You complete an online request form, then mail or courier it with the paper documents to Victoria. The province's submission instructions set out these steps:

  1. Ask the destination country's embassy or consulate what it needs.
  2. Prepare each document as its type requires, stapled or clipped separately.
  3. Complete the B.C. Document Authentication Request form on a desktop or laptop. List each document's type, the name on it and its destination country, which can't be Canada.
  4. Pay online by card, or choose to mail the fee with the documents.
  5. Print, sign and date the form, and keep its order number.
  6. Send the form, the documents and any payment by mail or courier to the Ministry of Attorney General, OIC Administration Office, Attention: BC Authentication Program, 1001 Douglas Street, Victoria, BC V8W 2C5.

One form covers up to 20 documents. B.C. offers no rush, walk-in, drop-off or while-you-wait service. Only the form and payment are online.

How much does an apostille cost in BC?

As of 26 November 2026, B.C. charges a standard CA$20 for each document authenticated, according to its fee page. The total is the number of documents times CA$20.

  • A single CA$20 payment for a stack of pages means one multi-page document with one certificate.
  • The fee includes return by regular Canada Post mail, with no tracking.
  • B.C. accepts cards online, or a money order, bank draft, or personal or business cheque in CAD or USD payable to the "Minister of Finance."
  • Documents sent without payment are returned unprocessed.

Fees differ by province: compare Alberta and Ontario.

How long does a BC apostille take?

As of 26 November 2026, the B.C. authentication guide gives a current processing time of 2 to 4 weeks, not including mail time. Its processing times page says the office aims for 14 business days when at full capacity. A blank email to BCAuthentication@gov.bc.ca returns the date of the requests now being processed.

Missing information, disorganized documents or a signatory not on file slow a request down. B.C.'s FAQ says to mark an urgent request under the special return mail instructions on the form.

B.C. warns that Canada Post delivery may be delayed and suggests a courier. Its return mail page accepts prepaid electronic UPS, FedEx or Purolator labels, and returns documents by regular mail if you send a handwritten waybill.

Do you need a translation for a BC apostille?

Usually, if any part of the document isn't in English. B.C. lists exceptions, such as official bilingual English-French documents from a Canadian government agency and Latin or Greek diplomas from recognized Canadian institutions. B.C. requires it to be translated into English and then notarized as a set with the original by a B.C. notary on file. It refuses unnotarized translations, according to its translation requirements.

  • The translator must be certified through the Society of Translators and Interpreters of British Columbia or a member society of the Canadian Translators, Terminologists and Interpreters Council.
  • Or a B.C. notary on file who is fluent in both languages translates it and signs a declaration.
  • If the destination wants the translation kept apart from the original, say so on the request form.

What happens after BC issues the apostille?

B.C. rivets the apostille to the front of the document with a hole punch. If the certificate is removed, the province says the authentication is null and void. Whether an apostille expires is for the destination to decide, according to the B.C. FAQ.

Since June 20, 2025, a Global Affairs Canada tool lets recipients verify apostilles issued by British Columbia online. A country outside the Convention may still need legalization at its consulate or embassy. Reasons B.C. lists for returning a document include:

  • missing payment or no signed request form;
  • a signatory or notary not on file, or a missing notarization date, signature or seal;
  • a photocopy, or a document over 40 pages;
  • a document issued outside B.C.

Apostille in British Columbia: common questions

Can I walk in to get an apostille in BC?

No. The B.C. Authentication Program has no walk-in, drop-off or while-you-wait service. Requests must arrive by mail or courier at its office in Victoria.

Can I get a BC apostille online?

Partly. The request form is online and you can pay online by card, but the signed form, the documents and any mailed payment must go by mail or courier.

Can a BC commissioner for taking affidavits sign a document for an apostille?

No. B.C. says it won't authenticate a document signed or sealed by a commissioner for taking affidavits. The document must be notarized by a B.C. lawyer or notary on file.

Will BC apostille a document from another province?

No. B.C. refuses documents issued in another province, territory or country. Global Affairs Canada handles Canadian documents from other provinces and territories that don't issue their own apostilles.
