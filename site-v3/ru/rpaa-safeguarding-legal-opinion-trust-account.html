The RPAA trust account legal opinion: what the Bank of Canada asks for

Guide · Published 3 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

The RPAA trust account legal opinion is a written opinion the Bank of Canada asks for when it assesses a payment service provider (PSP) that holds end-user funds in trust. As of 3 November 2026, the Bank's FAQ and self-assessment tool say it must show a valid express trust under common law or the Civil Code of Québec, set out the risks to that trust, and deal with any interest, dividends or capital gains the PSP receives. A lawyer must prepare it, and this guide is not legal advice.

Ключевые моменты

  • The Bank asks for a written legal opinion during its assessments of PSPs that safeguard end-user funds in trust.
  • It covers the express trust's validity under common law or the Civil Code of Québec, the risks to it, and interest, dividends or capital gains the PSP receives.
  • At the 2 November 2026 supervision update, Bank staff said the opinion should be written with the Bank as its intended reader.
  • Keep each account's agreement and set-off acknowledgement with the trust documents. Only a lawyer can prepare the opinion.

When does the Bank of Canada ask for the trust account legal opinion?

The Bank asks for the opinion during its periodic assessments of whether a PSP meets the safeguarding requirements in the RPAA and the Retail Payment Activities Regulations (RPAR). Its retail payments supervision FAQ, dated August 14, 2026, says that as part of the assessment process the Bank will request a written legal opinion from a PSP that safeguards funds in trust.

The request is tied to the first safeguarding option in subsection 20(1) of the Retail Payment Activities Act: holding end-user funds in trust in a trust account that is not used for any other purpose. If you aren't sure you hold end-user funds, start with who must register as a PSP.

Statement B14 of the Bank's safeguarding self-assessment tool (version October 2026) says the PSP can provide the opinion on request. At its 2 November 2026 supervision update, Bank staff told trust-account PSPs to "make sure your documentation supports the arrangement" and to have the trust documents and opinion ready.

Paragraph 2.21 of the Bank's safeguarding guideline (December 2024) already expects a PSP to seek legal advice on the validity of its trust, and the safeguarding requirements have applied since September 8, 2025. The FAQ adds that the Bank will want that advice in writing.

What must the legal opinion cover?

As of 3 November 2026, the Bank's FAQ and self-assessment tool name three core points. The opinion shows that the arrangement amounts to a valid express trust, under common law or, for Quebec arrangements, the Civil Code of Québec. It also assesses the risks or challenges to that trust and how the PSP deals with them, and it covers interest, dividends or capital gains the PSP receives from trust assets.

At the 2 November 2026 session, Bank staff added that the opinion should identify assumptions that may affect the trust's validity, as well as risks. The FAQ and tool themselves refer to risks and challenges. None of the three documents sets a template or format for the opinion (checked 3 November 2026).

What does the Bank say a valid express trust needs?

The Bank's safeguarding guideline lists characteristics it expects a PSP's trust to have, though paragraph 2.21 says it gives no legal advice on how to set one up. The opinion is where a lawyer confirms whether your documents meet them, so give counsel the Bank's own wording.

  • Outside Quebec, paragraph 2.22 says the arrangement must satisfy the three certainties of trust: intention, subject matter and objects. The PSP must intend to create a trust, and end users should intend that their funds be held in trust. Footnote 3 asks the PSP to get legal advice on how best to show end users' intent, such as signed agreements or clear acknowledgements.
  • In Quebec, paragraph 2.23 says the arrangement must qualify as a trust as defined in the Civil Code of Québec, formed by contract between a settlor and a trustee. The alternative is the simple administration regime, where the PSP holds and administers the funds as administrator of end users' property, appointed by them.
  • Under paragraph 2.20, the PSP can be the trustee or use a third party.

Our guide to safeguarding end-user funds covers the other safeguarding options.

Should the opinion be written specifically for the Bank?

Yes, according to Bank staff. At the 2 November 2026 supervision update they said the opinion should be "prepared for the express purpose of sharing with the bank". The published FAQ does not use those words (checked 3 November 2026), so treat this as a session remark.

If your existing trust advice was written for another reader or purpose, ask your lawyer whether it covers each point in the table above and whether it can be given to the Bank. If it can't, commission an opinion written for the assessment.

The FAQ says information obtained during an assessment is treated under section 62 of the RPAA. Subsection 62(1) makes it confidential, subject to the disclosures that subsections 62(2) and (3) allow.

Can a PSP keep interest earned on a trust account?

Possibly. The Bank's FAQ says a PSP holding funds in trust must make sure that keeping interest earned on end-user funds does not compromise or invalidate the trust. The Bank expects the PSP to confirm this with legal advisors who have appropriate expertise.

Tool statement B14 asks the opinion to address any interest, dividends or capital gains the PSP receives from trust assets, and the legal basis for receiving them. Bank staff used the same example at the session.

The guideline asks for legal input on three related uses of trust money:

  • If you hold trust funds in secure and liquid assets other than cash, paragraph 3.21 requires legal due diligence showing the trust is not compromised. The market value of those assets must always equal or exceed the funds you must safeguard.
  • If you intend to pay administrative expenses from the trust account, paragraph 2.28 recommends legal advice on what qualifies, and the trust arrangement should say how those expenses are shared among end users.
  • As a best practice, paragraph 2.27 says to pay no other expenses from the trust account and to settle obligations to a financial institution or payment network from a separate account.

How the opinion fits with RPAA s. 20, the RPAR and set-off evidence

The opinion covers the trust itself under paragraph 20(1)(a) of the RPAA, and other documents evidence the rest of the arrangement. Under the schedule to the Retail Payment Activities Regulations, a breach of subsection 20(1) of the Act is a very serious violation.

  • Set-off, account by account. Under RPAA subsection 20(3), whoever maintains a safeguarding account cannot assert set-off or compensation against its funds, and guideline paragraph 2.16 expects the account provider to acknowledge this in writing. At the 2 November 2026 session, Bank staff asked PSPs that use more than one safeguarding account to hold set-off evidence for every account.
  • The account agreement. Under paragraphs 2.29 and 2.30, you tell the account provider the funds are held in trust, and the agreement identifies them as held in trust for your end users.
  • The account provider. RPAR section 13 limits providers to listed Canadian institutions and comparably regulated foreign institutions.
  • The framework. RPAR subsection 15(3) requires your safeguarding framework to identify legal and operational risks, having regard to jurisdictions, account providers and the terms of your trust arrangements. The risks in the opinion feed that analysis.
  • Review and reporting. RPAR section 17 requires an independent review of compliance with subsection 20(1) at least once every three years, and paragraph 19(3)(a) asks your annual report to describe the trust arrangement.

What should a PSP prepare before engaging counsel?

Gather your trust documents, every account agreement and a plain description of how money moves through each trust account before you brief a lawyer. The trust statements in the self-assessment tool (B11 to B20) list most of the evidence the Bank expects:

  • The trust instrument and related documents, such as a trust agreement or declaration, a service agreement with trust provisions, the form of end-user acknowledgement and any third-party trustee agreement (B11).
  • For Quebec arrangements, the Civil Code trust agreement or the simple administration documents, with any French translations (B12).
  • For each safeguarding account: the provider, its jurisdiction, the account agreement, the written set-off acknowledgement and the notice that the funds are held in trust.
  • Where your end users are located (RPAR paragraph 15(3)(a)).
  • What flows in and out of each account, including any expenses, fees or settlement payments.
  • Who receives any interest, dividends or capital gains from trust assets, on what terms, and which assets other than cash you hold.
  • Any unaffiliated PSP you rely on to reach an account.
  • How end users show their intent that their funds be held in trust.
  • Your ledger description, the legal and operational risk analysis in your framework, and your trust account controls, such as signing authority and periodic verification (guideline para. 3.35).

Map each item to the tool's statements. The tool is voluntary, and the Bank states that filling it in is not, by itself, a demonstration of compliance. Our RPAA compliance program builds the safeguarding framework that surrounds the opinion, which only a lawyer can prepare.

Trust account legal opinion: common questions

Is a formal written legal opinion required for an RPAA trust account?

Yes, if the PSP safeguards end-user funds in trust. The Bank's FAQ, dated August 14, 2026, says the Bank will request a written legal opinion as part of its assessment process.

Can a PSP keep the interest earned on a safeguarding trust account?

Only if doing so does not compromise or invalidate the trust. The Bank's FAQ expects the PSP to confirm this with legal advisors, and its October 2026 self-assessment tool asks the legal opinion to address the legal basis for any interest, dividends or capital gains the PSP receives.

Does a PSP that uses insurance or a guarantee need a trust legal opinion?

The Bank's published request applies to funds held in trust. A PSP relying on insurance or a guarantee must meet RPAR section 14 instead. Any PSP in an indirect arrangement must still get legal advice under guideline paragraph 6.4.

Will the Bank of Canada keep a PSP's legal opinion confidential?

Yes, subject to limits. Subsection 62(1) of the RPAA makes information the Bank obtains under the Act confidential, apart from the disclosures that subsections 62(2) and (3) allow.

Who should prepare the RPAA trust account legal opinion?

A lawyer. The Bank's FAQ expects advice from legal advisors with appropriate expertise, and its safeguarding guideline says it does not provide legal advice on how to set up a valid express trust.

RPAA

Защита средств конечных пользователей по RPAA

С 8 сентября 2025 г. поставщик платёжных услуг, хранящий средства своих пользователей, обязан обеспечивать их защиту — либо в доверительном управлении на трастовом счёте, либо на обособленном счёте, обеспеченном страхованием или гарантией. Ему также нужна письменная система защиты средств и независимая проверка не реже одного раза в три года.

Годовой отчёт по RPAA для поставщиков платёжных услуг

Каждый поставщик платёжных услуг, осуществлявший розничные платёжные операции в календарном году, должен подать в Банк Канады годовой отчёт до 31 марта следующего года. Он охватывает вашу систему управления рисками и реагирования на инциденты, защиту средств, показатели деятельности и финансовые результаты.

Кто должен регистрироваться как PSP по RPAA?

Вы должны зарегистрироваться в Банке Канады, если выполняете платёжную функцию как самостоятельный вид деятельности, для электронных переводов в канадской или иностранной валюте, при наличии связи с Канадой и если не применяется ни одно исключение. Это руководство показывает, как Банк применяет каждую часть этого теста к реальным бизнес-моделям.
