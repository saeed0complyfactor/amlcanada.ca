How to get an apostille in Ontario

Guide · Published 26 November 2026 · 5 min read · AML Canada

An Ontario apostille is issued by Official Documents Services (ODS), the provincial office that authenticates documents for use outside Canada. ODS handles documents issued in Ontario and documents notarized by an Ontario notary public. Original federal documents go to Global Affairs Canada.

Key points

  • Official Documents Services issues apostilles in Ontario, in person at five offices or by mail to Toronto.
  • Ontario birth certificates, business registry documents since 1991, Superior Court or Ontario Court of Justice documents since July 10, 2023, and public post-secondary records since 2019 can go to ODS without notarization.
  • Any document notarized by an Ontario notary goes to Ontario, even a copy of a federal document.
  • As of 26 November 2026, ODS charges CA$16 per notarized document and CA$32 per Ontario government document, and processes mail-in requests within 15 business days.

Who issues apostilles in Ontario?

Official Documents Services (ODS) is the authority that issues apostilles and certificates of authentication in Ontario, according to Ontario's authentication page. ODS checks that the signature and seal on a document match its records. It doesn't validate what the document says.

Canada joined the Hague Apostille Convention on January 11, 2024. Since then, ODS issues an apostille for a document going to a country that belongs to the Convention, and a certificate of authentication for a country that doesn't. The Hague Conference status table lists member countries. A certificate of authentication may still need legalization at the destination country's embassy or consulate. If you're unsure which of these steps your document needs, our guide to apostille vs notarization explains the chain.

Does ServiceOntario do apostilles?

Only at five locations. Ontario accepts in-person requests at the ODS office in ServiceOntario Toronto College Park, 777 Bay Street, Lower Level, and at ServiceOntario centres in Ottawa (Ottawa Courthouse), Sault Ste. Marie, Windsor (City Hall) and Thunder Bay. Other ServiceOntario centres aren't listed for authentication.

All five locations open Monday to Friday, except statutory holidays. Since November 1, 2023, you can book an authentication appointment before you visit. ODS in Toronto is the only office that processes mail-in requests.

Which documents Ontario apostilles

ODS authenticates Ontario-issued public documents and documents notarized by an Ontario notary public. Global Affairs Canada says a document notarized in Ontario goes to Ontario's competent authority, wherever the original was issued. Original federal documents go to Global Affairs Canada, which charges no fee. As of 26 November 2026, Ontario and Global Affairs Canada list these routes:

DocumentNotarize first?Who apostilles it

Business registry documents issued since 1991 by the Business and Personal Property Branch or the Ontario Business RegistryNoODS
Superior Court of Justice or Ontario Court of Justice documents issued since July 10, 2023NoODS
Ontario court documents issued before July 10, 2023Yes (a certificate of divorce can be reissued instead)ODS
Ontario public post-secondary degrees, diplomas and transcripts issued since January 1, 2019NoODS
Private documents and copies, including copies of federal documents, notarized by an Ontario notaryAlready notarizedODS
Original Government of Canada documents not notarized in OntarioDepends on the document; Global Affairs Canada says whichGlobal Affairs Canada
Documents issued outside CanadaNot eligible, except a signature witnessed by an Ontario notaryThe issuing country

Ontario won't authenticate documents commissioned by a commissioner for taking affidavits, religious documents, documents already legalized by a consulate or embassy, or pseudo-legal documents. Check each document on the document authentication portal, which also says whether it needs notarizing.

When you need an Ontario notary first

A document outside the categories above, such as a power of attorney or a copy, is eligible at ODS once an Ontario notary public has notarized it. ODS doesn't have an in-house notary. Under section 3 of the Notaries Act, a notary may witness the signing of a document and certify a true copy, according to the Ministry of the Attorney General.

  • The notary must be physically present with the signer. Remote notarization isn't permitted, and ODS won't apostille a remotely notarized document.
  • The full signature and seal must appear on the same page, and match the specimen ODS holds on file.
  • Lawyers and paralegals aren't notaries by right; they must apply and be appointed.
  • A notary who isn't a lawyer or paralegal may notarize only documents tied to the business or organization named in their appointment.

A commissioner for taking affidavits can't stand in for a notary here, because ODS doesn't process commissioned documents. Our guide to commissioners of oaths and notaries explains the difference.

How can I get an apostille on a document in Ontario?

Ontario sets out five steps on its official page:

  1. Confirm the requirements with the embassy, consulate or receiving party. ODS can't advise on what they want.
  2. Check eligibility on the document authentication portal.
  3. Have the document notarized by an Ontario notary, if the portal says it's needed.
  4. Prepare a hard copy. For documents thicker than one-quarter inch (7 mm), punch 2 holes in the upper-left corner.
  5. Submit a service request online through the portal, or complete the service request form for mail-in or in-person submission.

An online request gives you a service request number by email. You still deliver that number, the documents and payment by mail or in person. Mail goes to Official Documents Services, 777 Bay Street, Lower Level, Toronto, ON M7A 2J8. ODS returns mail-in documents by regular mail; include a prepaid return envelope or a computer-generated return waybill if you want tracking.

How much does an apostille cost in Ontario, and how long does it take?

As of 26 November 2026, Ontario charges these fees per document, in Canadian dollars and exempt from tax:

Document typeFee
Notarized legal documentCA$16
Ontario government official documentCA$32
Ontario court-issued documentCA$32
Ontario public post-secondary degree, diploma or transcriptCA$32
Filing with a foreign consulate or embassyCA$32
Status of good standing for a notary publicCA$16

Two notarized documents cost CA$32. In person, ODS takes cash, Visa, Mastercard, debit, certified cheque or money order. By mail, it takes a certified cheque or money order payable to the Minister of Finance, or Visa or Mastercard through the mail-in form.

ODS issues mail-in apostilles within 15 business days of receiving the documents. The standard in-person wait is about 30 minutes if the request is complete, and longer for several items. Court-issued documents can take up to 10 business days in person.

Documents in other languages

Ontario only apostilles a document in a language other than English or French if it was issued in Canada, notarized by an Ontario notary public and accompanied by a translation. The translation can come from a certified translator who belongs to a recognized provincial association, listed by the Canadian Translators, Terminologists and Interpreters Council. An Ontario notary who speaks both languages can attest it instead, stating that they speak English or French and the original language and certifying the translation is accurate.

Checklist before you send, and verifying an Ontario apostille

ODS says it will only authenticate a document if the request meets its conditions. Before you submit, check that:

  • the receiving party has confirmed it wants an apostille or certificate of authentication;
  • the portal shows the document is eligible at ODS, and not at Global Affairs Canada;
  • the document is a paper original or a notarized paper copy, never an electronic file;
  • any notarization was done in person by an Ontario notary, not commissioned;
  • the notary's signature and seal are on the same page;
  • translations meet Ontario's two options;
  • the service request form or number and the fee for each document are included.

A recipient can check an Ontario apostille on the ODS verification page with the apostille number and issue date. The tool covers apostilles issued after January 11, 2024; certificates of authentication are verified by contacting ODS.

Apostille in Ontario: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Does ServiceOntario do apostilles?

Some locations do. Official Documents Services takes in-person requests at ServiceOntario Toronto College Park and at ServiceOntario centres in Ottawa, Sault Ste. Marie, Windsor and Thunder Bay. Mail-in requests go only to ODS in Toronto.

How long does an apostille take in Ontario?

As of 26 November 2026, ODS issues mail-in apostilles within 15 business days of receiving the documents. In person, the standard wait is about 30 minutes for a complete request, and court-issued documents can take up to 10 business days.

How much does an apostille cost in Ontario?

As of 26 November 2026, CA$16 per notarized document and CA$32 per Ontario government, court-issued or public post-secondary document. Fees apply to each document.

Can Ontario apostille a federal document?

Not an original. Original Government of Canada documents go to Global Affairs Canada. Global Affairs Canada says a document notarized in Ontario goes to Ontario, so a notarized copy of a federal document is apostilled by ODS.

Can I apostille a document notarized online in Ontario?

No. The Notaries Act doesn't permit remote notarization, and the Ministry of the Attorney General says ODS won't authenticate or apostille documents notarized remotely.
