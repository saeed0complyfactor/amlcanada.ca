How to check FINTRAC MSB registration

Guide · Published 11 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

To check FINTRAC MSB registration, search the business's legal name, operating name or registration number in FINTRAC's Money Services Business Registry and confirm the status reads Registered. Then match the name, number, website and services on the entry to the business you are dealing with. A listing confirms registration only, because FINTRAC does not license or endorse money services businesses.

Key points

  • Search FINTRAC's registry by name or number, then check that the name, number and status match the business in front of you.
  • Only Registered means registered today. A past expiry date with Registered status means a renewal was filed on time and is still being processed.
  • The whole registry downloads as XLS or CSV, and FINTRAC keeps a separate list of revoked registrations.
  • Registration is not a licence, an endorsement or a compliance finding, and FINTRAC can't tell you more than the registry shows.

How do I check FINTRAC MSB registration?

Open FINTRAC's Money Services Business Registry and search the business's legal name, operating name or registration number. FINTRAC says all fields are searchable. Then confirm that the name, the registration number and the status on the entry match what the business told you, which is the check FINTRAC itself recommends.

The registry is the public record of MSB registration. Section 54.1(1) of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) makes FINTRAC responsible for keeping a registry of the information businesses submit when they register. Section 54.1(3) requires FINTRAC to make the identifying part of it public. Under section 11.15, a business is registered once FINTRAC adds it to that registry.

FINTRAC says it can't give any further information on a business beyond what the registry shows. As of 11 November 2026, its registry page (modified 4 November 2026) says the registry is updated monthly.

What does the FINTRAC MSB registry show?

The registry's search results show 14 fields for each business. Section 2 of the PCMLTFA Registration Regulations defines the public "identifying information" as the details in Parts A and C of Schedule 1, plus the dates a registration is revoked or expires and the date a business ceases an activity. The fields are:

  • organization names, legal and operating;
  • business address and website;
  • services offered;
  • MSB registration status and MSB registration number;
  • initial registration date, expiry date, cessation date and revocation date;
  • jurisdiction of incorporation, incorporation number and incorporation date;
  • foreign MSB indicator.

The foreign MSB indicator separates the two kinds of registrant in section 5 of the Act. An MSB under paragraph 5(h) has a place of business in Canada. A foreign MSB under paragraph 5(h.1) has no place of business in Canada but directs its services at people in Canada and provides them to clients in Canada.

What do Registered, Expired, Ceased and Revoked mean?

Every business in the registry carries one of four statuses, and FINTRAC defines each one on its registry page. Only Registered means the business is registered today:

  • Registered. The business is currently registered with FINTRAC.
  • Expired. The business did not renew its registration after two years. Section 11.19 of the Act requires renewal every two years.
  • Ceased. The business told FINTRAC it no longer offers the services. Section 11.2 requires that notice within 30 days of stopping an activity.
  • Revoked. The business is no longer registered because it didn't answer a clarification request, didn't provide assistance to FINTRAC or was found ineligible.

Read the expiry date with the status. FINTRAC says a business that submitted its renewal before the expiry date stays Registered until the renewal is processed, even after that date passes. A business that did not submit one in time is treated as Expired. Our guide to denied, revoked and expired registrations explains what each outcome means for the business itself.

How can I check an MSB registration number in Canada?

Search the number in FINTRAC's registry and check that it returns the same legal or operating name the business uses with you. FINTRAC's registry page and its guidance on conducting transactions with an MSB both say to verify the name, number and status of a business together.

A number on its own proves little, because anyone can quote a number copied from a registered business. Compare the website and business address on the entry with the contact details you were given.

FINTRAC sends a business notice of its registration (section 11.15), but it says it does not issue licences or certificates of registration. Rely on the registry entry, and treat any "certificate" or "licence" a business shows you as its own document.

Can you provide me with a list of MSBs registered with FINTRAC?

Yes. FINTRAC publishes the registry as a download on its registry page, in XLS (1.1 MB) and CSV (2.6 MB) versions as of 11 November 2026. Check the status column of each row before you treat it as a current registration.

FINTRAC also lists the money services businesses registry among its datasets on the Open Government portal. Its separate list of businesses with revoked registration uses the same fields except Website, and FINTRAC points users back to the main registry for the most recent status.

The revoked list page sets out when FINTRAC can deny or revoke a registration. The grounds are a business that isn't eligible, doesn't answer a clarification request within 30 days, doesn't respond in time to demands for information, doesn't tell FINTRAC about updates to its name or address, or fails to provide assistance. A business can ask for a review within 30 days of a denial or revocation.

What a registry entry tells a bank, partner or buyer, and what it doesn't prove

A registry entry confirms a legal fact about registration and little else. FINTRAC says registration "indicates only that the business has satisfied the legal requirements to register." Read each field for what it can carry:

How each group uses the table:

  • Banks and credit unions. Section 9.31 of the Act bars banks, credit unions, caisses populaires, trust and loan companies and other prescribed entities from opening or keeping an account for a foreign MSB, or having a correspondent banking relationship with one, unless it is registered with FINTRAC. Save the entry at onboarding and again at each review.
  • Partners and payment providers. Match the listed services to the flows you will run with the MSB, and compare the expiry date with your contract term.
  • Buyers. Our MSB due-diligence checklist covers the rest, from examination history to the eligibility of new owners.
  • Consumers. FINTRAC says it can't assess an MSB's business practices or help customers recover funds, so treat the registry as the start of your research.

What if a business isn't in the FINTRAC registry?

A business missing from the registry may be listed under another name, may not be required to register as an MSB, or may be operating without registration. Search its legal name, operating names and registration number before you draw a conclusion.

Section 11.1 of the Act requires registration from MSBs, foreign MSBs, government departments and agents under paragraph 5(l) that issue or sell money orders to, or redeem them from, the public, and any other prescribed section 5 entity, except as the regulations provide otherwise. Banks and credit unions are reporting entities under paragraphs 5(a) and (b). A payment company may instead, or also, be registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, which keeps its own registry. Our guide to checking Bank of Canada PSP registration covers that search.

If the business still looks unregistered, FINTRAC's consumer guidance says you may submit voluntary information to FINTRAC anonymously. FINTRAC says operating an unregistered MSB is a violation and an offence under the Act. Section 77.4 makes it an offence for a business that must register to knowingly carry on an activity it isn't registered for.

Check MSB registration: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Is FINTRAC MSB registration a licence?

No. FINTRAC says registration does not mean it endorses or licenses the business, and it does not issue licences or certificates of registration. People search for an "MSB licence", but what section 11.1 of the PCMLTFA requires is registration with FINTRAC.

How often is the FINTRAC MSB registry updated?

FINTRAC's registry page, modified 4 November 2026, says the registry is updated monthly. A very recent change may not show yet, so save a dated copy of each search and repeat it before you sign or pay.

Can FINTRAC tell me more about an MSB than the registry shows?

No. FINTRAC says it can't provide any further information on a business beyond what the registry makes public. It also says it can't freeze funds, help recover funds or assist consumers in disputes with an MSB.

Why does a registered MSB show an expiry date that has passed?

FINTRAC says a business that submitted its renewal before the expiry date stays Registered until the renewal is processed. A business that missed the date has the status Expired instead.

Does the FINTRAC registry show Bank of Canada PSP registration?

No. None of the registry's 14 fields covers RPAA registration. Search the Bank of Canada's PSP registry separately, as our PSP registration guide explains.
