Effectiveness review or AML audit?

They are usually the same thing. Businesses and banks often call it an AML audit or an independent AML review. FINTRAC's term is the two-year effectiveness review: an evaluation that tests whether your compliance program works, and whether it has gaps or weaknesses that could stop you detecting and preventing money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion.

How often is an effectiveness review required?

At least every two years. FINTRAC requires every reporting entity to review the effectiveness of its compliance program, covering its policies and procedures, risk assessment and ongoing training program and plan, at a minimum once every two years. The review looks at how the program runs day to day as well as what the documents say.

The timing rule is stricter than many people expect. FINTRAC's guidance says you must start each review no later than 24 months from the start of your previous review, and you must have completed the previous review before you start the next one.

The review plan you need to document

FINTRAC requires you to institute and document a plan for the review, not just carry it out. The plan must include every element of your compliance program, and FINTRAC expects it to set out:

  • the scope of the review, and the reasons behind the areas you focus on;
  • the time period the review covers;
  • the evaluation methods you expect to use, such as staff interviews, record sampling and document review;
  • the sample sizes.

How deep the review goes on each element can vary with the complexity of your business, your transaction volumes, the findings of earlier reviews and your current money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risks.

Who can carry out the review

FINTRAC's guidance says the review is carried out, and its results documented, by an internal or external auditor or, if the business has no auditor, by the business itself. Whoever does it should know the requirements of the PCMLTFA and its regulations. As a best practice, FINTRAC says the reviewer should not be someone directly involved in your compliance program, so the review stays impartial. An external review gives you that independence. Whoever carries it out, making sure it happens on time remains your responsibility.

What a review tests

A review checks whether what your team does matches what your documents say, and whether you meet the PCMLTFA. The examples in FINTRAC's guidance include:

  • interviews with staff who handle transactions, to test their knowledge of your policies on client identification, record keeping and reporting;
  • a sample of client records, to see whether identification procedures were followed and the risk assessment was applied;
  • whether ongoing monitoring happens as often as each client's risk level requires, and whether high-risk clients received enhanced measures;
  • a review of transactions, to see whether suspicious transactions were reported to FINTRAC;
  • whether large cash transactions and electronic funds transfers were reported accurately and on time;
  • agreements with agents or mandataries, and a sample of the information they used to verify identity;
  • whether your risk assessment reflects your current operations, including clients, products, delivery channels, new technology and geography;
  • whether your policies and procedures are up to date with current law and your business practices.

Documenting the results and reporting to a senior officer

FINTRAC says the review should record the date it was done, the period it covered, who performed it, the results of the tests, and the conclusions, including any deficiencies, recommendations and action plans.

If you are an entity, you must also report in writing to a senior officer within 30 days of completing the review. The report covers the review's findings, any updates made to your policies and procedures during the period that were not made because of the review, and the status of implementing those updates.

How we run a review

  1. Scoping call. We learn your products, clients, volumes and earlier findings, and agree the scope in writing.
  2. Written review plan. Scope, rationale, review period, methods and sample sizes, as FINTRAC expects.
  3. Document review. Your policies and procedures, risk assessment, and training program and plan.
  4. Interviews and sampling. We talk to the people who do the work and test samples of client files, monitoring and reports.
  5. Findings. A written report with deficiencies, recommendations and an action plan, in order of priority, ready for the 30-day report to your senior officer.

Effectiveness review or FINTRAC examination?

An effectiveness review is your own check of your program. A FINTRAC examination is FINTRAC's assessment of your compliance. A recent, honest review, with its findings being fixed, is one of the best ways to prepare for an examination.

Is an AML audit the same as a FINTRAC effectiveness review?

Usually, yes. AML audit is the common name; FINTRAC calls it the two-year effectiveness review. It tests your policies and procedures, risk assessment, and training program and plan.

How often is an AML effectiveness review required in Canada?

At least every two years. Each review must start no later than 24 months from the start of the previous one, and the previous review must be finished first.

Who receives the results?

If you are an entity, you must report the findings in writing to a senior officer within 30 days of completing the review.

Can we do the review ourselves?

FINTRAC's guidance allows a self-review if you have no internal or external auditor, provided whoever does it knows the requirements. An external review adds independence.

What do we receive?

Written findings and recommendations, in order of priority, so you know what to fix first.

FINTRAC

FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) explained

FINTRAC can impose administrative monetary penalties for breaches of the anti-money laundering law. Amendments in force since March 26, 2026 raised the maximum amounts up to 40 times and added compliance agreements and compliance orders.

Read the guide Compliance programs

FINTRAC compliance program requirements

Every business FINTRAC regulates must establish and implement a compliance program. It has five parts: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan, and a review of the program at least every two years.

Read the guide Examination

Preparing for a FINTRAC examination

A FINTRAC examination usually starts with a call to your compliance officer and a letter asking for documents. Examinations can be done remotely or on site, and findings can lead to follow-up or a penalty.
