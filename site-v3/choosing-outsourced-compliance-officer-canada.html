How to choose an outsourced compliance officer in Canada

Guide · Published 19 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

How to choose an outsourced compliance officer comes down to three checks: what the provider will do for your FINTRAC program, what the written agreement gives them in authority and access, and whether anything they claim conflicts with FINTRAC's rules. This checklist is for a FINTRAC-regulated business that has already decided to outsource the role.

Key points

  • You can appoint an outside person as compliance officer under PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(a), but your business stays responsible for meeting its compliance program requirements.
  • Before you sign, get written answers on reports, onboarding reviews, policy upkeep, training, hours, access, escalation, sign-off and how decisions are recorded.
  • The agreement should grant access to senior management and information, name who approves what, keep every record in your systems, and set out a handover on exit.
  • Treat four claims as warning signs: an offer to do your effectiveness review while running your program, "FINTRAC-approved" wording, MSB registration called a licence, and a promise that your legal responsibility moves to the provider.

Can a compliance officer be outsourced?

A compliance officer can be outsourced: PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(a) requires you to appoint "a person" responsible for implementing your compliance program, and FINTRAC's compliance program guidance says the officer can be you or another individual. Neither text says the person must be your employee.

FINTRAC's guidance sets two limits on any outsourced arrangement. The officer may delegate certain duties but remains responsible for implementing the program, and meeting the program requirements is the reporting entity's responsibility.

Who can legally hold the role is covered in our guide to MSB compliance officer requirements. If you're still weighing cost and the in-house option, start with outsourced vs in-house compliance officer.

What questions should you ask an outsourced compliance officer?

Before you sign, ask an outsourced compliance officer ten questions: which FINTRAC reports they file, which onboarding files they review, who keeps your policies and risk assessment current, who trains staff, how hours work, what access they need, how escalation works, who signs off, how decisions are recorded, and whether they would review their own work.

The table below is our outsourced CAMLO checklist, checked against the law and FINTRAC's guidance as of 19 November 2026.

What should an outsourced compliance officer agreement include?

An outsourced compliance officer agreement should record the appointment and the officer's authority, their access to senior management, information and resources, who signs off on what, who owns the records, and how the role is handed over when the engagement ends.

  • Record the formal appointment and the officer's authority. FINTRAC's compliance assessment manual says examiners may review documents showing you formally appointed the officer, their job description and documents describing their authority.
  • Grant access in writing. FINTRAC's guidance says the officer needs the authority and access to resources to implement an effective program and, as a best practice, the ability to communicate directly with senior management or the board.
  • Name who approves. For an entity, PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(b) requires a senior officer to approve the policies and procedures. Section 1(2) defines a senior officer to include a director who is a full-time employee, the CEO, and other officers who report directly to the board, CEO or COO.
  • Keep the records yours. Your business must keep its records for at least five years (PCMLTFR s. 148(1)), keep a copy of every report it sends to FINTRAC (s. 144), and be able to provide records within 30 days of a request (s. 149). Under s. 148(2), a person under contract isn't required to keep records that belong to the business after the contract ends, so the agreement should keep every record in your systems.
  • Plan the handover. An MSB's registration includes the compliance officer's name, address, phone number and email (Registration Regulations, Schedule 1, Part B, item 11), and PCMLTFA s. 11.13(1) requires you to notify FINTRAC of a change within 30 days after you become aware of it. In our view, the agreement should set a notice period, list what is handed back (open files, pending reports, policies, training records) and say who covers the role in between.

What are the warning signs when choosing a provider?

Four warning signs follow from the law and from FINTRAC's own guidance and public statements: an offer to run your program and also review it, a claim to be FINTRAC-approved or certified, MSB registration called a licence, and a promise that your legal responsibility moves to the provider.

They offer to review their own work

PCMLTFR s. 156(3) requires the two-year review to be carried out and documented by an internal or external auditor, or by you if you have no auditor. FINTRAC's best practice is that the reviewer isn't directly involved in your compliance program activities, and a provider running your program is. In our engagements, we prepare the documents and records, and the client appoints the independent reviewer.

They say they are FINTRAC-approved or certified

FINTRAC says it "does not endorse any templates or services provided by businesses or industry associations" (FINTRAC compliance program video), and as of 19 November 2026 we found no FINTRAC program that approves or certifies compliance consultants. FINTRAC's training guidance puts the check on you: with an external service provider, you may need to determine whether their services and content suit your business. Ask what the claim refers to, and look for it on FINTRAC's own website.

They call MSB registration a licence

FINTRAC's MSB registry page says registration doesn't indicate that FINTRAC endorses or licenses the business, and that FINTRAC doesn't issue licences or certificates of registration. Its consumer page says FINTRAC doesn't have the authority to issue licences to the businesses it regulates.

They say the responsibility moves to them

FINTRAC's guidance says the compliance officer stays responsible for implementing the program when duties are delegated, and that meeting the program requirements is the reporting entity's responsibility. An agreement can divide the work, and your obligations under the PCMLTFA stay with you.

What does a well-scoped engagement include?

In our engagements, a well-scoped outsourced compliance officer arrangement is written as a split of who does what: we act as compliance officer and file your FINTRAC reports, and your team escalates complete files, handles all customer contact and runs day-to-day monitoring.

Our side covers FINTRAC reports and other filings, such as MSB registration changes; the policies, procedures and risk assessment; decisions on escalated onboarding files and transactions; help designing onboarding controls and monitoring rules; and staff training. The client's side covers escalating through one shared channel, sending information requests to customers, building and integrating systems, giving us access to folders and platforms, and appointing the independent effectiveness reviewer. Our role is advisory, and following that advice in practice is the client's responsibility.

The day-to-day workflow after you sign is in how an outsourced compliance officer works, and the service and fee are on our outsourced compliance officer page.

What are the 7 pillars of compliance, and what does FINTRAC require?

The PCMLTFA and its regulations don't use a seven-pillar list. For a FINTRAC-regulated business, PCMLTFR s. 156(1) sets out the program in six paragraphs, (a) to (f), which FINTRAC groups as five required elements because it treats the training program and training plan as one. A provider's scope should show which ones it covers.

  1. A compliance officer (s. 156(1)(a)): the provider, with authority set out in writing.
  2. Written policies and procedures (s. 156(1)(b)): drafted by the provider, approved by your senior officer if you're an entity.
  3. A documented risk assessment (s. 156(1)(c)): maintained by the provider with information from your team.
  4. A written, ongoing training program (s. 156(1)(d), part of FINTRAC's single training element): delivered by the provider if training is in scope.
  5. A training plan and delivery of the training (s. 156(1)(e), the same training element): with records you keep.
  6. A plan for the effectiveness review (s. 156(1)(f)): carried out every two years under s. 156(3), and, as FINTRAC's best practice, by someone not involved in running the program.

Each element is explained in our guide to FINTRAC compliance program requirements.

Choosing an outsourced CO: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Can a compliance officer be outsourced?

Yes. PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(a) requires you to appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program, and FINTRAC's guidance doesn't require that person to be an employee. The officer can delegate duties but stays responsible, and your business stays responsible for meeting its program requirements.

What are the 7 pillars of compliance?

Canada's AML law doesn't use a seven-pillar list. PCMLTFR s. 156(1) sets out the compliance program in six paragraphs, which FINTRAC groups as five elements: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan, and a two-year effectiveness review and plan.

Can my outsourced compliance officer also do our effectiveness review?

PCMLTFR s. 156(3) says the review is done by an internal or external auditor, or by you if you have no auditor. FINTRAC's best practice is that the reviewer isn't directly involved in your compliance program activities, so the provider running your program shouldn't also review it.

Is there a list of FINTRAC-approved compliance consultants?

FINTRAC says it "does not endorse any templates or services provided by businesses or industry associations", and as of 19 November 2026 we found no FINTRAC list of approved or certified consultants. FINTRAC's guidance says that if you use an external training provider, you may need to determine whether their services suit your business.

Do I have to tell FINTRAC if our outsourced compliance officer changes?

If you're an MSB, yes. The compliance officer's contact details are part of your registration information, and PCMLTFA s. 11.13(1) requires you to notify FINTRAC of a change within 30 days after you become aware of it.
