How buying a registered MSB works

  1. Consultation. Your plans: the services you'll offer, your customers and your timeline.
  2. Matching. A company registered for the money services you need.
  3. Due diligence. Corporate records, registration status and history, and the eligibility of the new owners and directors, reviewed before you commit.
  4. Bank of Canada. If the company is also registered under the Retail Payment Activities Act, re-registration before the purchase closes.
  5. Change of ownership. The transfer of the company's shares to you.
  6. FINTRAC updates. Changes to the registration information, such as owners, directors and compliance officer, reported within 30 days.

What to check before you buy

  • that the registration is active and covers the services you plan to offer;
  • when the registration is next due for renewal;
  • the company's corporate records, ownership history and any past compliance issues;
  • that the new owners and directors are eligible. Under PCMLTFA s.11.11, a business can't be registered if, for example, a director, the chief executive officer, the president or a 20% owner has certain criminal convictions, or if a FINTRAC penalty remains unpaid;
  • that the chief executive officer, the president, the directors and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more can provide criminal record checks, with a certified translation for any that aren't in English or French;
  • whether the company is also registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider;
  • whether provincial requirements apply where you'll operate. Quebec, for example, licenses money services businesses separately.

How to check an MSB on FINTRAC's registry

  1. Open FINTRAC's MSB registry. It's updated monthly, and a full CSV download is available.
  2. Search by the company's legal name or its MSB registration number.
  3. Check that the status is Registered, and check the registered services and the expiry date.
  4. Make sure the legal name and incorporation number match the share purchase agreement.

A newly registered company can take up to a month to appear, because the registry is updated monthly. In that case, ask the seller for FINTRAC's registration approval email.

Rules buyers get wrong

  1. It's a registration, not a licence. FINTRAC registers money services businesses under the PCMLTFA. It doesn't issue licences, and a registration isn't an endorsement. Quebec is the only province that licenses money services businesses separately. FINTRAC
  2. You buy the company, not the registration. The registration belongs to the registered business, so it continues when the company's shares are sold. It can't be moved to another company. FINTRAC
  3. Changes go to FINTRAC within 30 days. New owners, directors and other changes to the registration information must be reported within 30 days. FINTRAC
  4. New owners must be eligible. A business can't be registered if, for example, a director, the chief executive officer, the president or a 20% owner has certain criminal convictions (PCMLTFA s.11.11). FINTRAC
  5. Bank of Canada registration is separate. If the company is registered as a payment service provider under the RPAA, buying control needs Bank of Canada re-registration before the purchase closes. Bank of Canada

If the company is also registered with the Bank of Canada

Some MSBs are also registered with the Bank of Canada as payment service providers under the Retail Payment Activities Act. Buying control of such a company is an acquisition of control, so the company must apply again and be re-registered by the Bank before the purchase closes (RPAA s.24). The changes are then reported to FINTRAC within 30 days.

After the purchase

The obligations come with the company. The business needs a compliance officer and a compliance program that reflect its new owners and services, and it must report to FINTRAC, keep records and renew its registration every two years. FINTRAC registration doesn't guarantee a bank account or payment partners, which make their own decisions.

MSB for sale: questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

How much does a registered MSB cost?

Acquisitions start from €28,000. The price depends on the company, the services it's registered for and its history.

Does the FINTRAC registration stay with the company?

The registration belongs to the registered business, so the purchase is of the company's shares; a new entity would need its own registration. After the purchase, FINTRAC must be told about changes to the registration information, such as new owners and directors, within 30 days.

Do the new owners need criminal record checks?

FINTRAC asks for criminal record checks for the chief executive officer, the president, the directors and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more of the business. Any check that isn't in English or French needs a translation attested by a certified translator.

What if the company is registered with the Bank of Canada too?

Then it must apply again and be re-registered by the Bank before the purchase closes. We check this during due diligence.

Does it come with a bank account?

FINTRAC registration doesn't guarantee banking or payment partners. We discuss what each company has in place before you commit.

Can a buyer outside Canada acquire an MSB?

Yes. Foreign buyers can acquire a registered Canadian company, subject to the same FINTRAC requirements.

Which MSBs are available right now?

The companies listed on this page: one Ontario and two British Columbia companies, all registered with FINTRAC for foreign exchange, money transferring and virtual currency, with no trading history. Names and registration numbers are shared under NDA.

How can I verify a company before I pay?

Ask for its legal name and MSB registration number, then search FINTRAC's public MSB registry and check that the status is Registered and the services and expiry date match what you've been told.
