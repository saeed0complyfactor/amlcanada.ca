Crypto custody in Canada: the rules and custody models compared

Guide · Published 27 October 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

Canada has no single crypto custody licence. A platform that holds client crypto is expected to register with securities regulators. Under CSA pre-registration undertakings, which broadly match the terms registered platforms work under, client crypto is held in trust, mostly with an acceptable third-party custodian. Exchanging or transferring crypto for clients means FINTRAC registration. The Bank of Canada comes in where fiat payments are involved and, once the Stablecoin Act is in force, for stablecoin issuers.

Key points

  • A platform that keeps client crypto instead of delivering it is under securities law and is expected to register as an investment dealer.
  • Under CSA pre-registration undertakings and registered platforms' terms, client crypto is held in trust, mostly with an acceptable third-party custodian.
  • Exchanging or transferring crypto for clients is an MSB service, so register with FINTRAC and apply the travel rule.
  • Crypto-only custody is outside the RPAA. A spending card or other fiat payment feature can bring you in.

Who regulates crypto custody in Canada

The rules that apply turn on who controls the private keys, whether clients can take their crypto away without relying on you, and whether you also move fiat money. Four regimes can overlap:

  • Provincial and territorial securities regulators, working together as the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA), when a platform holds crypto for clients rather than delivering it to them.
  • FINTRAC, when you exchange or transfer virtual currency for clients, which makes you a money services business (MSB).
  • The Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA), when you also perform fiat payment functions that aren't incidental to your crypto business.
  • The Bank of Canada again for stablecoin issuers, once the Stablecoin Act is in force.

FINTRAC registration, reporting and record keeping for crypto businesses are covered in registering a crypto business with FINTRAC.

When holding client crypto becomes a securities matter

CSA Staff Notice 21-327, published January 16, 2020, sets the test. A platform generally falls outside securities legislation only if the crypto asset isn't a security or derivative and the platform must deliver it immediately, and routinely does. CSA staff generally treat delivery as complete when the user gets ownership, possession and control, can deal with the asset without relying on the platform, and isn't left exposed to the platform's insolvency, fraud, performance or proficiency risk.

If the platform keeps the crypto and moves it to a user-controlled wallet only on request, with the purchase recorded on its own books, securities legislation applies. The notice says "a mere book entry does not constitute delivery."

A custodial platform is therefore expected to register. On August 6, 2024, the CSA and the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization (CIRO) said they expect crypto trading platforms to register as investment dealers and become CIRO members. The CSA no longer accepts new pre-registration undertakings, the Ontario Securities Commission says, so a new platform applies directly to CIRO.

Joint CSA/IIROC Staff Notice 21-329 lists measures platforms with custody must take, including:

  • segregating client assets and private keys from their own;
  • keeping records that confirm client holdings at all times;
  • having policies for cold storage and for creating and storing private keys;
  • holding enough financial resources and insurance, or other risk mitigation;
  • doing due diligence before retaining a custodian and monitoring it afterwards; and
  • getting independent assurance over the custodian's controls, such as a SOC 2 report.

What an acceptable third-party custodian is

CSA Staff Notice 21-332, published February 22, 2023, tightened the undertakings unregistered platforms gave while seeking registration. Client assets had to be held apart from the platform's own property and in trust for the client. Crypto went into a designated trust account, or an account designated for clients with an "Acceptable Third-party Custodian". The standard undertaking already committed platforms to keep at least 80% of the total value of client crypto with third-party custodians, and never to pledge, re-hypothecate or otherwise use it.

The notice defines an acceptable third-party custodian as an entity that:

  • is a Canadian custodian or Canadian financial institution, a custodian qualified under section 6.2 of National Instrument 81-102, a custodian that meets CIRO's definition of an acceptable securities location, or a foreign or other custodian the platform's regulators have approved in writing;
  • is functionally independent of the platform, as National Instrument 31-103 uses that term;
  • has audited financial statements from the last 12 months with an unqualified opinion, showing what it owes custody clients and the assets it holds against that; and
  • has a SOC 2 Type 1 or Type 2 report from the last 12 months, or a comparable report its regulators accept.

The platform also lets CSA staff get client account information straight from the custodian. Registered platforms have further custody terms in their own exemptive relief decisions. If you're looking at a Canadian trust company as custodian, check it against these definitions with your principal regulator before you sign.

Private keys count as virtual currency under the PCMLTF Regulations

The PCMLTF Regulations define virtual currency to include a private key that gives access to a digital representation of value. FINTRAC's MSB page says dealing in virtual currency covers exchange services, meaning funds for crypto, crypto for funds or one crypto for another, and transfer services, meaning moving virtual currency at a client's request or receiving it to remit to a beneficiary. A business doing either registers as an MSB, or as a foreign MSB if it serves Canada from abroad.

FINTRAC doesn't list custody as a separate service, but a custodian that sends crypto out when a client asks is doing what FINTRAC describes as a transfer. When you have to keep a record of a virtual currency transfer, section 124.1 of the Regulations requires you to send the requester and beneficiary information with it and take reasonable measures to check that incoming transfers include it. Our travel rule guide has the details.

The RPAA rarely reaches crypto-only custody

The RPAA covers payment functions for electronic funds transfers in the currency of Canada or another country, or in a unit that meets criteria prescribed by regulation. The Retail Payment Activities Regulations, current to September 30, 2026, prescribe no such unit. The Bank's crypto case scenarios from October 2, 2024 show where the line falls:

  • An exchange where users keep crypto in its wallets and trade crypto for crypto, with no fiat, isn't a payment service provider (PSP).
  • An exchange that pools client fiat for trading is under securities legislation and its payment functions are incidental, so it doesn't register.
  • A virtual currency exchange whose prepaid cards let users spend the value of their crypto is a PSP and must register, if it meets the other criteria.

Crypto-only custody stays outside, but a spending card or fiat wallet can bring you in. The federal stablecoin framework adds that the Bank will supervise PSPs performing payment functions in a fiat-backed stablecoin, once that stablecoin is prescribed in regulation. See who must register as a PSP.

Stablecoin reserves and qualified custodians

The Stablecoin Act received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026 and isn't in force yet. Once it is, an issuer must place its reserve assets with one or more qualified custodians, meaning a financial institution or another person the regulations provide for. The Department of Finance says reserves will be cash or high-quality cash-like assets, segregated from the issuer's and the custodian's own assets, and expects the framework in force in 2027. Until then, CSA Staff Notice 21-333 expects the reserve behind a stablecoin traded on a platform to sit with a qualified custodian, apart from the issuer's assets.

Structuring custody lawfully

Securities regulators judge custody by what a product does with client assets, so a product described as non-custodial is still treated as custodial if you keep the keys.

  • To stay outside securities registration, deliver crypto to a wallet the client alone controls, immediately and every time, and keep no security interest in it.
  • To hold client crypto, register, keep most of it with an acceptable third-party custodian, hold it in trust and apart from your own assets, and never lend or pledge it.
  • To offer crypto without running custody, partner with a registered platform and get advice on whether your own role needs registration.
  • Under any model, register with FINTRAC before you exchange or transfer crypto, and build travel rule and sanctions screening into the transfer flow.

Crypto custody: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Do I need to register to hold client crypto in Canada?

Usually with more than one regulator. A platform that keeps client crypto instead of delivering it immediately is subject to securities law and is expected to register as an investment dealer and join CIRO. If you exchange or transfer crypto for clients, you also register with FINTRAC as an MSB.

What is an acceptable third-party custodian?

Under CSA Staff Notice 21-332, it's a Canadian custodian or financial institution, another qualified custodian, or one your regulators approve in writing. It must be functionally independent of the platform and have audited financial statements and a SOC 2 Type 1 or Type 2 report from the last 12 months.

Does a non-custodial wallet need FINTRAC registration?

It depends on what the business does. FINTRAC's MSB definition covers exchanging and transferring virtual currency for clients. If users control their own keys and you provide neither service, get a written analysis, or ask FINTRAC, before launch.

Does the RPAA apply to crypto custody?

Not to crypto-only custody. The Bank of Canada's case scenarios say a crypto-for-crypto exchange that handles no fiat isn't a PSP, while a crypto exchange whose prepaid cards let users spend the value of their crypto is, if it meets the other criteria.

Is the Stablecoin Act in force?

No. It received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026, and the Department of Finance expects the framework to come into force in 2027. Issuers will then have to hold reserve assets with qualified custodians.
