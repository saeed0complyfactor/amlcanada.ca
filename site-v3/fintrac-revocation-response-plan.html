FINTRAC revoked your MSB registration: a response plan

Guide · Published 27 October 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

A revoked registration means the business can't offer the money services it was registered for. You have 30 days from receiving the decision to ask FINTRAC's Director to review it. Stop the registered services straight away, because the Act doesn't pause a revocation while a review runs.

Key points

  • Becoming ineligible revokes a registration on the day it happens, before any notice arrives.
  • Stop the registered services at every location. The Act doesn't pause a revocation during a review.
  • Apply in writing to FINTRAC's Director within 30 days of receiving the decision.
  • Prevention comes down to 30-day deadlines, two-year renewals, screening owners and directors, and cooperating with examinations.

The short answer

FINTRAC registers money services businesses (MSBs). It doesn't license them, though many people call the registration an MSB licence. When FINTRAC revokes it, the business is no longer registered, and section 77.4 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act makes it an offence to knowingly carry on an activity you aren't registered for.

For registry statuses, denied applications and expiry, see our guide to denied, revoked and expired registrations. This is general information, not legal advice. Talk to a lawyer about your own case.

What triggers a revocation

The Act gives FINTRAC four routes to revoke an MSB or foreign MSB registration:

TriggerSectionFINTRAC's role
The business becomes ineligible11.11(2)Revoked automatically as of the day the ineligibility arises. FINTRAC must send written notice once it becomes aware.
A clarification request goes unanswered for 30 days11.17May revoke
No reasonable assistance to an examiner, or documents demanded by an examination notice not provided11.171May revoke
A foreign MSB doesn't report a change to its representative for service's name or address within 30 days11.13(2)Must revoke

Ineligibility covers people and entities under certain sanctions, listed terrorist entities and those convicted of listed offences. Under section 3 of the Registration Regulations, a corporation is also ineligible if its CEO, president, a director or a 20% owner falls into most of those groups. FINTRAC's eligibility page lists the offences.

Two Acts that received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026 changed the list. Neither change waits for an order in council, so both took effect that day and appear in the consolidated Act. The Strengthening Canada's Immigration System and Borders Act (S.C. 2026, c. 4) rewrote the unpaid-penalty ground. A person or entity that still hasn't paid a FINTRAC penalty and interest 30 days after all proceedings on the violation have ended is ineligible, and so is anyone acting with them to evade payment. The Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1 (S.C. 2026, c. 3) reworded the sanctions ground to refer to prohibitions under Part 1 of the Special Economic Measures Act.

Every decision to revoke must be in writing with reasons (section 11.18). FINTRAC says it usually sends clarification requests by email to the contact person or representative for service on file, so an unmonitored inbox can start the 30-day clock without anyone noticing. For a foreign MSB, a notice served on its representative for service counts as served on the business (section 72.1).

The first 48 hours

The 48-hour window is our recommendation. The first deadline in the Act is the 30-day review. Treat the business as unregistered from the date in the notice. For an ineligibility revocation, that is the day the ineligibility arose, which may be earlier than the notice. Neither section 11.3 nor section 11.4 says that asking for a review or appealing restores or suspends the registration. If transactions are already in progress, ask your lawyer how to deal with them before you complete any.

Your agents and branches don't register separately because they appear on your list (section 11.12(2)), so the revocation reaches every location that offers services under your registration. Tell them in writing to stop. Banks and payment partners can see the change on FINTRAC's public registry, so tell them directly and give them a contact for questions. A foreign MSB should know that banks and other listed financial entities can't open or maintain an account for a foreign MSB that isn't registered (section 9.31). Customers need a plain notice that services are paused and how to reach you about funds already in progress.

If the business is also a payment service provider registered with the Bank of Canada, section 53.6 requires FINTRAC to tell the Bank about the revocation as soon as feasible, and about any review or appeal and its outcome.

Records you keep

Revocation doesn't end your record keeping. The Regulations require most records to be kept for at least five years (section 148), and the criminal record documents filed with an application or change notice must be kept for five years after they were submitted (Registration Regulations, section 6.03). Keep your full registration file, every clarification request and reply, and your examination correspondence. Your review request will rely on them.

What a strong review request contains

The application goes to FINTRAC's Review and Appeals Unit by mail, or through a secure link you get by emailing rev-app@fintrac-canafe.gc.ca. Don't put sensitive information in that first email. FINTRAC's reviews page says the request must be in writing with an explanation and any supporting information, and that a review officer first checks it was filed within 30 days. If a lawyer files it, include a letter of authorization.

The Director can confirm the decision or substitute their own, and must consider any information they deem relevant. Answer the ground FINTRAC actually gave:

  • Unanswered clarification request. Give the complete answers now, and show when and where the request was sent if it reached an outdated contact.
  • Examination assistance or documents. Provide the documents the notice demanded, with a log of what you provided earlier and when.
  • Ineligibility. Show who holds each role and share, with criminal record documents issued by a competent authority. For an unpaid penalty, show payment with interest.
  • Representative for service. Provide the current name and address for service and the date of the change.

Correct anything else in your registration information that is out of date, and say in the request what you corrected.

Public registry status and reapplying

FINTRAC's registry shows the business as Revoked, and the revocation date is part of the identifying information the Registration Regulations make public (section 2). FINTRAC also keeps a separate list of revoked registrations. Its registry page says FINTRAC updates the registry "on a monthly basis". FINTRAC keeps the information submitted for registration for 10 years after a business stops being registered (section 54.1(6)).

The Act sets no waiting period before a new application, but the cause has to be fixed first. The application asks whether you have applied before (Schedule 1, Part B, item 18 of the Registration Regulations), and criminal record documents must be issued no more than six months before you submit (section 6.03(1)). Our registration guide covers the full process.

Prevention checklist

  • Renew every two years (section 11.19), before the expiry date. FINTRAC says a renewal submitted before expiry keeps you Registered while it's processed. Virtual currency dealers and foreign MSBs should check the first-renewal date set by section 6.1 of the Registration Regulations.
  • Report changes within 30 days (section 11.13), including contact details and, for a foreign MSB, the representative for service.
  • Answer clarification requests within 30 days, and put the contact address FINTRAC has on file on a shared inbox that someone checks daily.
  • Give examiners reasonable assistance and meet the deadlines in any document notice (sections 62(2) and 63.1). Our examination guide explains what to prepare.
  • Check the CEO, president, directors and anyone holding 20% or more against the ineligibility list and sanctions before appointment and on a schedule. Our guide to changes of ownership covers new owners.
  • Pay or resolve any FINTRAC penalty. An unpaid penalty makes the business ineligible 30 days after proceedings end. See FINTRAC penalties.

An outsourced compliance officer can own these deadlines for you, and our MSB registration service handles renewals, change notices and new applications.

Revocation response plan: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Can FINTRAC revoke my MSB licence?

FINTRAC registers MSBs rather than licensing them, and it can revoke a registration. The grounds are ineligibility, an unanswered clarification request, failing to assist an examination or provide demanded documents, and, for a foreign MSB, not reporting a change to its representative for service within 30 days.

How long do I have to challenge a FINTRAC revocation?

30 days after you receive the decision to apply in writing to FINTRAC's Director for a review. If you disagree with the Director's decision, you have 30 days after it's served to appeal to the Federal Court, or a longer period the Court allows.

Can I keep operating while FINTRAC reviews the revocation?

The Act doesn't say a review or appeal restores or suspends the registration, and knowingly carrying on an activity you aren't registered for is an offence under section 77.4 of the Act. Plan on being unregistered and ask a lawyer about your situation.

Will customers and banks see that my registration was revoked?

Yes. FINTRAC's public registry shows the Revoked status and the revocation date, and FINTRAC also publishes a list of businesses with revoked registrations. FINTRAC says it updates the registry monthly.

Does FINTRAC tell the Bank of Canada about a revocation?

If the business is a payment service provider registered with the Bank of Canada, the Act requires FINTRAC to notify the Bank as soon as feasible, and to report any review or appeal and its outcome.
