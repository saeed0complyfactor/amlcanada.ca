OFAC sanctions eight Canadian MSBs: what money services businesses should check

Update · Published 21 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

On 6 November 2026, the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated eight money services businesses based in Canada under its Iran sanctions, and FINTRAC said it coordinated with OFAC and FinCEN on the action. For Canadian MSBs, it is a reason to recheck sanctions screening and how Iran-connected transactions are identified and reported.

Das Wichtigste in Kürze

  • OFAC added eight Canadian MSBs in Ontario and British Columbia to its SDN List under Executive Order 13902, for operating in the digital asset sector of the Iranian economy.
  • The SDN entries list aliases, addresses, Canadian MSB registration numbers and, for four entities, TRX digital currency addresses, so screening on legal names alone can miss a match.
  • FINTRAC says it has revoked 161 MSB registrations since the start of 2026 and imposed penalties totalling more than $198 million on nine MSBs over the past year.
  • Canada's Iran ministerial directive already treats every transaction originating from or bound for Iran as high risk and requires it to be reported to FINTRAC.

What did OFAC and FINTRAC announce on 6 November 2026?

OFAC designated eight Canadian money services businesses under Executive Order 13902 "for operating in the digital asset sector of the Iranian economy," according to the US Treasury press release. The action is part of Treasury's Operation Economic Outcast. Treasury says the businesses facilitate the exchange of Iranian rials and other currencies for digital assets, and that the action was coordinated with FinCEN and FINTRAC.

The same day, FINTRAC published a public statement confirming it had coordinated with OFAC and FinCEN "in the imposition of sanctions against eight Canadian money services businesses." FINTRAC added that, under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, it cannot disclose compliance actions against specific businesses except through a public notice of an administrative monetary penalty.

What does Treasury say these businesses did?

Treasury says digital asset addresses linked to several of the businesses transacted with sanctioned Iranian exchanges, including Nobitex, Wallex, Ramzinex, Bitpin, Aban Tether Exchange and Shelbit, or with addresses associated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

Treasury also says several of the businesses worked with an OFAC-sanctioned Canadian drug trafficker, who used couriers to bring bulk cash to money exchange businesses in the Vancouver and Toronto areas that converted it into digital assets to send abroad. It adds that several of the businesses "have changed their business names and addresses in an attempt to evade detection and enforcement actions."

Treasury's account describes the exchange of rials for virtual currency, cash converted to virtual currency for transfer abroad, and changes of business name and address. These are Treasury's statements. FINTRAC's statement does not describe the conduct of any of the eight businesses, and none of them appears on FINTRAC's public notice list of penalties as of 21 November 2026.

What did FINTRAC say about MSB enforcement?

FINTRAC's statement gave these figures, which go beyond the eight businesses:

  • Since the beginning of 2026, FINTRAC has revoked the registrations of 161 money services businesses, "more than the last three years combined."
  • Over the past year, it imposed administrative monetary penalties on nine MSBs for more than $198 million, for violations that include failing to register and failing to report large virtual currency transactions.
  • Over the past year, it disclosed 28 cases of MSB non-compliance to law enforcement.
  • Since Bill C-12 passed in March 2026, operating without a valid MSB registration can bring, on conviction on indictment, a fine of up to $5,000,000, up to five years in prison, or both.

Our guides on FINTRAC penalties and refused and revoked MSB registrations explain how penalties and revocations work.

Which Canadian rules apply to Iran-connected transactions?

An OFAC designation is a US measure. Your obligations to FINTRAC come from Canadian law. The sanctions evasion and listed property reports below depend on Canadian sanctions laws and lists, so a match on the SDN List alone does not trigger them. The Iran directive and suspicious transaction reporting for money laundering or terrorist financing can still apply.

The ministerial directive on Iran

The directive came into force on July 25, 2020 and was amended on February 15, 2024 and November 15, 2025, according to FINTRAC's Iran directive guidance. For every transaction originating from or bound for Iran, whatever the amount, you must treat it as high risk and verify the identity of the client who requests or benefits from it. Your due diligence must establish the source of the funds or virtual currency, the purpose of the transaction and the beneficial ownership or control of any entity involved. You must also keep a record and report the transaction to FINTRAC. FINTRAC's examples of a connection include virtual currency or currency exchange transactions involving the Iranian rial, and online transactions traced to an IP address geolocated in Iran. Our ministerial directives guide covers the "IR2020" reporting field.

Suspicious transaction reports for sanctions evasion

You must report completed and attempted transactions when there are reasonable grounds to suspect they relate to a sanctions evasion offence, according to FINTRAC's sanctions evasion guidance. FINTRAC defines that offence by reference to orders and regulations under the United Nations Act, the Special Economic Measures Act and the Sergei Magnitsky Law, and points to the Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List and the UN Security Council list. Suspicions of money laundering or terrorist financing are reportable on their own terms; see our FINTRAC reporting guide.

Meldungen von Vermögen gelisteter Personen oder Einrichtungen

If you must disclose property of a listed person or entity under the Criminal Code, the United Nations Act, the Special Economic Measures Act or the Sergei Magnitsky Law, you must also send FINTRAC a Listed Person or Entity Property Report immediately, according to FINTRAC's guidance. Property includes virtual currency.

Why does a US designation matter to a Canadian MSB?

Treasury's press release says US persons are generally prohibited from dealing with designated persons, and that entities owned 50% or more by blocked persons are also blocked. It adds that non-US persons are prohibited from causing US persons to violate US sanctions, and that financial institutions and others "may risk exposure to sanctions" for certain transactions with designated persons. Each SDN entry also notes "secondary sanctions risk" under section 2 of Executive Order 13902.

If your business uses US banks, US dollar settlement or US-based service providers, those providers may be US persons subject to these prohibitions. Whether and how US sanctions apply to a specific business is a legal question, so get advice from a sanctions lawyer if any of your clients or counterparties appear on the SDN List. Which Canadian MSBs did OFAC sanction in November 2026?

On 6 November 2026, OFAC added 1380079 BC Ltd, 2397639 Ontario Ltd, B Galaxy Inc, CCC Inc, Darvish Exchange, Diverse Trade Inc, NIA Nations Currency Ltd and Top PC Technician Inc to its SDN List under Executive Order 13902.

Did FINTRAC take part in the OFAC sanctions?

FINTRAC said in a public statement on 6 November 2026 that it coordinated with OFAC and FinCEN in the imposition of sanctions against eight Canadian money services businesses.

Do Canadian MSBs have to report transactions connected to Iran?

Yes. Under the ministerial directive on Iran, every transaction originating from or bound for Iran must be treated as high risk and reported to FINTRAC, whatever the amount.

Do MSBs have to report suspected sanctions evasion to FINTRAC?

Yes. FINTRAC requires a suspicious transaction report when there are reasonable grounds to suspect a transaction relates to a sanctions evasion offence under Canada's United Nations Act, Special Economic Measures Act or Sergei Magnitsky Law.

How many MSB registrations has FINTRAC revoked in 2026?

FINTRAC said on 6 November 2026 that it had revoked the registrations of 161 money services businesses since the beginning of 2026, more than the previous three years combined.
