Buying an MSB in Canada: what to check

Guide · Updated 25 October 2026 · 4 min read · AML Canada

Reviewed by Muhammad Saeed Abbasi, CAMS, ACMA, CGMA, MLRO and Managing Director, ComplyFactor Limited. Last checked 25 October 2026.

Buying a company that's already registered with FINTRAC as a money services business needs the same care as any acquisition, plus checks specific to FINTRAC and, for payment businesses, the Bank of Canada.

Key points

  • Check the registration on FINTRAC's public registry before you agree a price.
  • New owners and managers must not be ineligible under the PCMLTFA.
  • Tell FINTRAC about changes, like new owners and directors, within 30 days.
  • A PSP registered under the RPAA must re-register before control changes.

Why some businesses buy

Some businesses acquire a Canadian company that's already registered with FINTRAC as a money services business, instead of registering a new one. They start from an established entity with a registration history. It costs more up front, and the due diligence matters as much as the price.

AML Canada sells registered MSBs. Our current companies are listed on MSB for sale. The checks below apply whichever seller you buy from.

Check the public registry

Start with FINTRAC's MSB registry. Search by name or registration number and confirm the status is Registered. The other statuses are Revoked, Expired and Ceased. The listing also shows the services the business is registered for, its addresses, and its registration and expiry dates.

Compare the listed services with what you plan to offer. Adding services later means updating the registration. Note the expiry date too: registration lasts two years and must be renewed before it expires. A Registered listing means the business met the legal requirements to register. FINTRAC says it isn't an endorsement.

Check that the new owners are eligible

Section 11.11 of the PCMLTFA makes some persons and entities ineligible to register. They include:

  • persons and entities subject to sanctions under the United Nations Act, the Special Economic Measures Act or the Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act, or listed under the Criminal Code;
  • persons and entities convicted of money laundering, terrorist financing or PCMLTFA offences, or of certain other Criminal Code, drug, customs and tax offences;
  • corporations whose director, CEO, president or 20%+ owner has been convicted on indictment of an offence under the PCMLTFA or its predecessor Act;
  • since Bill C-12, persons and entities that still haven't paid a FINTRAC penalty, with interest, 30 days after all proceedings on the violation have ended.

If a registered business becomes ineligible, its registration is revoked from that day. If a change of ownership makes the business ineligible, for example under the corporation rule above, the registration you're paying for ends. FINTRAC also needs criminal record checks for the CEO, the president, each director and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more, so plan for these for the incoming people, especially if they live outside Canada.

Shares or assets

As general information, FINTRAC registration belongs to the registered person or entity. In a share purchase, the company keeps its registration and reports the changes to FINTRAC. If you buy the business's assets into a new or different company, that company needs its own registration before it provides money services.

A share purchase also brings the company's history with it: past transactions, records, any examination findings and any unpaid penalties. Ask your lawyer about warranties and indemnities that cover this.

What else to check before you buy

  • Past FINTRAC examinations, findings letters and action plans, and any published penalties.
  • The compliance program, the last two effectiveness reviews and their reports to a senior officer.
  • Whether reports were filed on time, and whether records exist for the past five years.
  • Corporate records: ownership history, directors, and that the company is in good standing.
  • Bank accounts and payment partners, and whether they will continue after the change of control.

Red flags

  • A registry status other than Registered, or an expiry date that has already passed.
  • A seller who won't share FINTRAC correspondence or examination results.
  • Registered services or transaction estimates that don't match what the business has done.
  • No compliance program, or one that was never put into practice.
  • Owners or directors who may fall within the ineligibility rules.

Bank of Canada and Quebec

If the company is also registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider, a planned acquisition of control triggers RPAA section 24. The PSP must submit a new application and be registered again before the acquisition closes. The new application gives the Minister of Finance the chance to carry out a national security review. The Bank's guidance on acquisitions asks PSPs to allow time for both reviews before closing.

In Quebec, money-services businesses also need a licence from Revenu Québec. Revenu Québec decides on a licence only after it receives security clearance reports from the Sûreté du Québec on the business and its associated persons, including directors, officers and anyone who directly or indirectly owns or controls it. New reports are required when a change is likely to affect the licence.

After the purchase

A registered MSB must tell FINTRAC within 30 days when its registration information changes. A change of ownership usually means several changes at once: owners, directors, senior management, and often the compliance officer and address.

The business still needs a compliance program that reflects its new owners, services and risks, a compliance officer with the authority to do the job, reporting to FINTRAC, record keeping, and renewal every two years. Our compliance program documents can be rewritten for the new owners. FINTRAC registration also doesn't guarantee banking or payment partners, which make their own decisions.

Buying an MSB: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Where can I buy a FINTRAC-registered MSB in Canada?

AML Canada lists registered Canadian MSBs for sale on its MSB for sale page, with each company's FINTRAC dates, registered services and status. We also review the company with you and handle the FINTRAC change filings after closing.

Which companies sell ready-made Canadian MSBs?

Consultancies, law firms and brokers sell them. Choose one that gives you the company's FINTRAC registration number before you pay a deposit, so you can check its status and services on FINTRAC's registry yourself.

How do I buy an existing MSB in Canada?

You buy the shares of a company already registered with FINTRAC. Then you report the new owners, directors and compliance officer to FINTRAC within 30 days, and update the compliance program for your business.

Can a FINTRAC MSB registration be transferred to a new owner?

Not on its own. The registration belongs to the company, so it continues when the company's shares are sold. The changes must then be reported to FINTRAC within 30 days.

Can you buy a FINTRAC-registered MSB?

Yes. You can acquire a company that's already registered. Changes to its registration information, such as new owners and directors, must be reported to FINTRAC within 30 days.

Does the registration transfer if I buy the assets?

As general information, registration belongs to the registered entity. In a share purchase the company keeps it. A new or different company that buys the assets needs its own registration.

How much does a ready-made Canadian MSB cost?

AML Canada's registered MSBs start from €28,000. The price depends on the registered services, the company's history and whether it's also registered with the Bank of Canada. Registering a new MSB instead is our fixed fee of US$3,500.

Should I buy an existing MSB or register a new one?

Buy if you need a registered company sooner; register if you want a company with only your history. Both routes carry the same obligations afterwards. Our buy or register page compares them.

What due diligence should I do before buying a Canadian MSB?

Check on FINTRAC's registry that the registration is active, covers your services and when it expires. Then review the corporate records, past compliance issues and FINTRAC correspondence, the new owners' eligibility, and whether the company is registered with the Bank of Canada.

What happens to an RPAA registration when I buy a Canadian MSB or PSP?

Buying control of an RPAA-registered company is an acquisition of control, so the company must apply again and be re-registered by the Bank of Canada before the purchase closes.

Do new owners need criminal record checks?

Yes. FINTRAC requires criminal record checks for the CEO, the president, each director and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more, so plan for them as part of the purchase.

How to register an MSB in Canada

A money services business must register with FINTRAC before it starts operating, and renew every two years. FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee, and it says the majority of complete applications are processed within three months.

How much does MSB registration cost in Canada?

FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee to register a money services business. The costs come from criminal record checks, translations, documents and the compliance program you need to operate.
