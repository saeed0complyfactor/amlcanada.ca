RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds

Guide · Published 16 October 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

Since September 8, 2025, a payment service provider that holds funds for its users must safeguard them, either in trust in a trust account or in a segregated account backed by insurance or a guarantee. It also needs a written safeguarding framework and an independent review at least every three years.

Key points

  • Safeguarding applies only to end-user funds a PSP holds at rest.
  • Place held funds in a safeguarding account on receipt. Unavoidable processing constraints allow until the next business day.
  • Use a trust account, or a segregated account with insurance or a guarantee, or a mix of both.
  • Keep a daily ledger, approve the framework every year and have it reviewed independently every three years.

Who must safeguard end-user funds

One of the payment functions in the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) is holding funds on behalf of an end user until they're withdrawn by the end user or transferred to another person or entity. A payment service provider (PSP) that performs it must safeguard those funds. The Bank of Canada's supervisory framework says the requirement came into force on September 8, 2025.

It only covers funds that are held. The Bank's safeguarding summary explains the difference:

  • You hold funds when they sit at rest in an account you own, available for a later withdrawal or transfer, and you owe them to the end user.
  • Funds are at rest while you have no instruction to transfer them immediately.
  • You stop holding funds when you receive an instruction for immediate withdrawal or transfer, or when a future-dated transfer date arrives.
  • Funds you receive together with an instruction to transfer them immediately aren't held.

For example, if a user tops up a wallet with you and leaves the balance there for a later payment, you're holding those funds from the moment they reach your account. If a user sends funds with an instruction to pay a recipient straight away, those funds aren't held.

The Bank's safeguarding guideline adds some specific cases. Funds a merchant processor reserves specifically to cover chargebacks or fraud aren't held, and neither are your own funds advanced to a merchant before a card transaction settles. If you have a place of business in Canada, the requirement covers funds you hold for end users anywhere. If you don't, it covers only funds you hold for end users in Canada.

There's one exception. A PSP that is itself a member of a provincial deposit insurance program doesn't need to safeguard end-user funds it holds as deposits insured under that program. It doesn't apply just because the credit union or other institution holding your account is a member. If you're not sure whether your business holds funds at all, our guide to who the RPAA covers explains registration.

Segregate funds in a safeguarding account

The Bank gives safeguarding two objectives: end users should have reliable access to their funds without delay, and their funds should be protected against loss if the PSP becomes insolvent.

You must keep end-user funds apart from all other funds, including your own, in an account that isn't used for any other purpose. The Bank calls it the safeguarding account. It must be provided by a Canadian financial institution of the kinds the RPAA lists, or by a foreign institution prudentially regulated to standards comparable to Canadian ones. The Bank also expects a written acknowledgement from the account provider that it has no interest in, recourse to or right against the funds for any sum owed to it.

The Bank's guideline expects funds to go into the safeguarding account on receipt. If processing constraints you can't avoid make that impossible, they must be in the account by the end of the next business day at the latest. In that case you must not use the funds for anything else, be able to explain the delay to the Bank, track the funds and tell end users when their funds might not be protected straight away. The Bank treats funds not safeguarded on receipt as a shortfall, so keep a record of them and report them in your annual report.

Trust account, insurance or guarantee

Subsection 20(1) of the RPAA sets out the ways to protect the funds. You can combine them, for example by holding part of the funds in trust and covering the rest with insurance, or by using more than one provider.

The written safeguarding framework

A PSP that holds end-user funds must establish, implement and maintain a written framework. The Bank's safeguarding guideline sets out what it must contain at a minimum:

  • A ledger that records the funds you hold for each end user, with their name and contact details, and whether the funds are in the safeguarding account. It must be updated each day.
  • Your approach to meeting withdrawal and transfer requests. Funds can be held in cash or in secure and liquid assets, meaning assets that can be turned into cash quickly with little or no loss of value. The Bank's list includes guaranteed investment certificates, government securities and promissory notes rated at least A-, and commercial paper and corporate bonds rated at least AA-. It excludes residential mortgage-backed securities and common shares.
  • Procedures for returning funds to each end user if you become insolvent, written so that an insolvency professional could follow them using your records.
  • An analysis of the legal and operational risks that could stop the framework working, and how you reduce them.
  • A review at least once a year, after any change to how you safeguard funds, and after any change likely to have a material effect on it.

Unless the PSP is an individual, the framework must name a senior officer responsible for overseeing safeguarding and for compliance with the requirements. The senior officer, and your board of directors if you have one, must approve the framework at least once a year. The senior officer also approves it after each material change.

Shortfalls and the three-year independent review

Under the Retail Payment Activities Regulations, you must take ongoing measures to find any shortfall, where the funds in trust or covered by insurance or a guarantee add up to less than the funds you hold. Fix a shortfall in a timely manner, investigate its root cause immediately and, as soon as feasible, take measures to stop it happening again. The Bank suggests checking for shortfalls daily as a best practice.

At least once every three years, an independent review must check whether you comply with the safeguarding requirements. The reviewer can be inside or outside your business, but must be suitably skilled and must have had no role in setting up or running the framework or in finding shortfalls. Report the gaps it finds, and what you did about them, to your senior officer.

Using another PSP's account

The Bank expects you to have a direct relationship with your safeguarding account provider. If you rely on another, unaffiliated PSP for access to an account at a financial institution, the Bank treats that as an indirect arrangement. It won't accept one as compliant unless you can show you've dealt with the trust law, Quebec Civil Code and insolvency problems it creates.

Where safeguarding shows up in your reporting

Your annual report to the Bank includes information on your safeguarding practices if you hold end-user funds. It must also list each shortfall you found, with the amount, the root cause and the measures you took to prevent it recurring. A change expected to materially affect how you safeguard funds is a significant change, and you must notify the Bank at least five business days before you make it, according to its supervisory framework. RPAA safeguarding: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Does every PSP have to safeguard funds under the RPAA?

No. Only PSPs that hold end-user funds at rest, available for later withdrawal or transfer. Funds received with an instruction to transfer them immediately aren't held.

Can a PSP use both a trust account and insurance?

Yes. The Bank says you can safeguard part of the funds in trust and protect the rest with insurance or a guarantee, and you can use more than one provider.

How quickly must funds go into the safeguarding account?

On receipt. If processing constraints you can't avoid make that impossible, by the end of the next business day at the latest, and you must tell end users when that can happen.

How often is the safeguarding framework reviewed?

You review it at least once a year and after any change to how you safeguard funds, and your senior officer, and your board if you have one, approve it at least once a year. An independent review of your compliance is also needed at least once every three years.

Is RPAA safeguarding the same as FINTRAC compliance?

No. Safeguarding is a Bank of Canada requirement under the RPAA. FINTRAC is a separate regulator, and the Bank's registration application asks for any registrations for retail payment activities you hold with FINTRAC.

RPAA

What is the RPAA?

The Retail Payment Activities Act is the federal law under which the Bank of Canada supervises payment service providers. It sets rules on operational risk, incidents and the safeguarding of users' funds.

FINTRAC compliance program requirements

Every business FINTRAC regulates must establish and implement a compliance program. It has five parts: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan, and a review of the program at least every two years.
