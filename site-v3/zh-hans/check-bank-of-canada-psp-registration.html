How to check Bank of Canada PSP registration

Guide · Published 3 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

To check Bank of Canada PSP registration, search the company's legal or trade name in the Bank's Registry of payment service providers, then check its list of applicants, refused or revoked list and enforcement decisions. As of 3 November 2026, the registry listed 1,789 PSPs. A listing confirms registration only, because the Bank does not license or endorse PSPs.

要点

  • Search the Registry of PSPs by legal and trade name, then open the entry to check payment functions, the registration date and violations.
  • "In Review" means the business has applied and has no decision yet. Applicants from 8 September 2025 onward can't operate until they are registered.
  • Some refusals and revocations are technical, such as a business that is out of scope or has stopped operating. Read the authority and reason on each entry.
  • Registration is not a licence or a compliance finding. Report a suspected unregistered PSP through the Bank's general inquiries form, and expect no updates.

How do I check if a payment company is registered with the Bank of Canada?

Search the company's legal name or main trade name in the Bank of Canada's Registry of payment service providers, then open its entry. Section 26 of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires the Bank to keep this registry and publish each registered PSP's name, address and prescribed information. As of 3 November 2026, it listed 1,789 registered PSPs.

The search table shows legal name, main trade name, head office country and a violations flag. Section 23 of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations adds the trade names, registration date, addresses, phone, email, website, payment functions performed and the names of all agents and mandataries.

Contact and PSP details can change, so the Bank asks users to check back periodically. Its retail payments supervision FAQ, dated 14 August 2026, says a registration does not expire and the registry stays public indefinitely, although the Bank can revoke a registration.

What does "In Review" mean on the list of applicants?

"In Review" is the status the Bank's registry data gives a business that has applied to register and has no decision yet. As of 3 November 2026, all 419 entries on the Bank's list of applicants had that status. An applicant is not a registered PSP.

The list shows legal name, main trade name, head office country and date of application. The date decides what the applicant may do:

  • Applied before 8 September 2025. Section 108 of the RPAA lets a PSP that applied during the transition period keep operating until the Bank registers or refuses it. The Bank expects these applicants to meet the operational risk and safeguarding obligations already.
  • Applied on or after 8 September 2025. The Bank says these applicants may not start retail payment activities until it has registered them.

The Bank warns that the list may leave out some businesses in discussion with it. At its 2 November 2026 supervision update, Bank staff said they could not give a review timeline. After the Bank's own review, the Department of Finance coordinates a national security review. They said both lists are updated very frequently.

What does the refused or revoked registrations list show?

The Bank's refused or revoked registrations list names applicants the Bank refused to register and PSPs whose registration it revoked, with reasons. Section 27(1) of the RPAA requires the Bank to keep and publish it. As of 3 November 2026, it held 57 refusals and 15 revocations.

The table shows legal name, main trade name, head office country, status (Refused or Revoked) and violations. Each entry gives the decision date, the legal authority relied on and a written reason, with a summary of facts and analysis where available.

Read the authority line before you judge the business. Some refusals rest on the RPAA not applying, for example because the applicant is excluded from the Act. Some revocations cite section 52(e), which covers a PSP that has stopped performing retail payment activities. Grounds such as false or misleading information, a PCMLTFA conviction or penalty, or a directive from the Minister of Finance after a national security review (RPAA sections 40 and 48) are more serious.

Under section 27(2), a name goes on the list only after the review period has passed or a review has confirmed the decision. The Bank's refusal and revocation policy lets a business apply again at any time, though it may be refused if the earlier reasons still apply.

Where does the Bank publish notices of violation?

The Bank publishes notices of violation (NOVs) on its enforcement decisions page and flags them on the PSP's registry entry. Section 93 of the RPAA requires it to make public the nature of the violation, the PSP's name and any penalty once the violation is established. The Bank says these decisions stay online for five years.

Its public notice policy (17 June 2024) limits publication to NOVs issued against a PSP and adds a notice of default if a PSP breaks a compliance agreement. The 12 June 2026 announcement says an NOV appears once the period for representations has ended. Full enforcement review decisions sit on the prescribed review decisions page.

As of 3 November 2026, the page held 13 NOVs. Each was for breaching RPAA section 23, which bars a PSP from performing retail payment activities before it is registered, and each penalty was $0. The page also carries temporary orders, which the Governor can make under section 94(4). Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 session that warning letters are not published, and that NOVs with monetary penalties will be published once issued.

Is Bank of Canada registration a licence or a compliance certificate?

Bank of Canada registration is neither a licence nor a compliance certificate. The registry page states that the Bank does not endorse or license registered PSPs, and its FAQ says the Bank does not give PSPs a licence. A listing shows the business is in scope of the RPAA, paid the required fees and cleared a national security screening coordinated by the Department of Finance.

At the 2 November 2026 session, Bank staff described an activities-based registration regime: "It's not a licensing regime." They said a PSP does not have to prove compliance to get registered. Supervision and enforcement come afterwards. The lists don't tell you:

  • whether the PSP's risk management or safeguarding works in practice;
  • whether it has had a warning letter, since those stay private;
  • whether it is under assessment or investigation;
  • whether it is registered with FINTRAC or a province;
  • which of its products each listed payment function covers.

How is the Bank's PSP registry different from FINTRAC's MSB registry?

The Bank's registry records RPAA registration for supervision of operational risk, incidents and safeguarding. FINTRAC's money services business registry records registration under the PCMLTFA, Canada's anti-money laundering law. A business may need both, one or neither, as our comparison of RPAA and FINTRAC MSB registration explains.

FINTRAC puts Registered, Revoked, Ceased and Expired businesses in one search, with a registration number, services offered, expiry date and a foreign MSB indicator. MSB registration has to be renewed every two years, while the Bank's FAQ says RPAA registration does not expire. FINTRAC's page, modified 11 October 2026, says it updates the registry monthly. Both regulators state that registration is not an endorsement or a licence. A FINTRAC status of Revoked is covered in our guide to refused and revoked MSB registrations.

What should PSPs do if they see competitors in their vertical not registered as a PSP at the Bank?

Tell the Bank what you have seen by using its online general inquiries form and picking retail payments supervision as the topic. Bank staff gave this answer to an audience question at the 2 November 2026 session, and said the Bank reviews each submission and decides whether further action is warranted.

Run the checklist first. The competitor may be on the list of applicants, act as a listed agent of a registered PSP, or be excluded or out of scope. If it still looks unregistered, give its legal and trade names, website, the payment service it offers and any sign that it serves Canadian end users, with dates.

You won't hear back. Bank staff said "the bank wouldn't be able to provide any updates on its supervisory activities" to the person reporting. Operating without registration breaches RPAA section 23, a very serious violation with a penalty of up to $10,000,000 under section 48 of the regulations.

What happens if PSPs that should be registered don't register?

They breach section 23 of the RPAA, a very serious violation with a penalty of up to $10,000,000. As of 3 November 2026, the Bank had published 13 notices of violation for this breach, each with a $0 penalty. Bank staff said on 2 November 2026 that the Bank has been investigating entities operating without registration.

Can a company on the Bank of Canada list of applicants process payments?

It depends on the application date. A PSP that applied before 8 September 2025 may continue until the Bank registers or refuses it (RPAA section 108). One that applied on or after that date may not operate until the Bank registers it.

Why is a payment company not on any Bank of Canada list?

Banks, credit unions and other institutions in section 9 of the RPAA are excluded, and some businesses are out of scope or act as agents of a registered PSP. A missing name can also mean the business is operating unregistered, so ask for its written scoping analysis.

How often is the Bank of Canada PSP registry updated?

The Bank says it updates the registry and its lists regularly, and Bank staff said on 2 November 2026 that updates are very frequent. FINTRAC updates its MSB registry monthly. Save a dated copy of each search.

Does the Bank of Canada handle complaints about a payment company?

The Bank of Canada does not handle complaints of that kind. Individual disputes, fee complaints and privacy complaints fall outside its retail payments mandate, so contact the PSP and then a provincial or federal consumer protection agency. Information about a business that may be operating unregistered can go to the Bank through its general inquiries form.
