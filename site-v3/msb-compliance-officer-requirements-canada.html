MSB compliance officer requirements in Canada

Guide · Published 11 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

The core MSB compliance officer requirement in Canada is short: you must appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program. Most of what people call requirements, such as seniority, independence and knowledge, comes from FINTRAC's guidance, and this guide marks which is which.

Key points

  • The law requires you to appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program. FINTRAC's guidance sets out the authority and knowledge that person needs.
  • An owner or operator of a small business can be the compliance officer, and a sole proprietor can appoint themselves.
  • The officer's name and contact details go in your MSB registration, so a change must be reported to FINTRAC within 30 days.
  • Delegating or outsourcing the work doesn't move responsibility. The officer stays responsible for implementing the program, and your business stays responsible for meeting the requirements.

What are the MSB compliance officer requirements in Canada?

Every money services business and foreign money services business must appoint a person who is responsible for implementing its compliance program. That is the legal requirement, in paragraph 156(1)(a) of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations. FINTRAC's guidance then describes what the person needs to do the job.

The duty to have a compliance program comes from subsection 9.6(1) of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA), which applies to every person or entity referred to in section 5 of the Act. MSBs and foreign MSBs are both in section 5. Appointing a compliance officer is the first of the program's elements; the others are covered in our guide to FINTRAC compliance program requirements.

FINTRAC says that appointing someone isn't enough on its own. In its compliance program guidance, the appointed officer is responsible for implementing every element of the program.

Required by law, expected by FINTRAC, or best practice

As of 11 November 2026, the rules on compliance officers fall into three groups: what the Act and regulations require, what FINTRAC's guidance says the officer needs and its examiners check, and what FINTRAC calls best practice. This table sorts each point and names the source.

The registration item matters in practice. Because the officer's details are part of your application under the Registration Regulations, replacing your compliance officer is a change you report to FINTRAC within 30 days of becoming aware of it, under PCMLTFA subsection 11.13(1).

Who can be a compliance officer under FINTRAC rules?

FINTRAC says the compliance officer can be you or another individual, depending on the size of your business. In a small business, it can be a senior manager, the owner or the operator. In a large business, FINTRAC's example is someone at a senior level with direct access to senior management and the board of directors.

FINTRAC's glossary defines the compliance officer as the individual, with the necessary authority, that you appoint to be responsible for implementing your compliance program. Keep that role separate in your mind from the senior officer. For an entity, a senior officer approves your written policies and procedures and receives the effectiveness review results.

Can I act as my own compliance officer for my MSB?

Yes, in most small MSBs. FINTRAC's guidance says the owner or operator of a small business can be the compliance officer, and a sole proprietor can appoint themselves. The regulation itself lets a person take responsibility for implementing the program instead of appointing someone else.

Two points from FINTRAC's guidance limit how far this goes as you grow:

  • Handling funds. As a best practice, the compliance officer of a larger business shouldn't be directly involved in the receipt, transfer or payment of funds. An owner who also processes transactions fits a small business better than a larger one.
  • The two-year review. The effectiveness review must be done by an internal or external auditor, or by you if you have no auditor (PCMLTFR s. 156(3)). As a best practice, FINTRAC says the reviewer shouldn't be someone directly involved in your compliance program activities, so an owner who is also the compliance officer is better off with someone else reviewing the program.

Being your own compliance officer also means doing the work. FINTRAC's examiners judge the officer by whether the program is actually put into practice.

What qualifications do you need to be a compliance officer?

Neither section 156 of the regulations nor FINTRAC's compliance program guidance names a certification, degree or minimum years of experience. FINTRAC describes what the officer needs instead. Its guidance says a compliance officer needs to:

  • have the necessary authority and access to resources to implement an effective compliance program and make any desired changes;
  • know your business's functions and structure;
  • know your sector's money laundering, terrorist activity financing and sanctions evasion risks and vulnerabilities, and the related trends and typologies;
  • understand your sector's requirements under the PCMLTFA and its regulations.

FINTRAC's compliance assessment manual says examiners may look at the officer's background and experience, and at the training you have given them, to check that knowledge. FINTRAC's guidance also lists the compliance officer among the people your training plan should cover. An AML credential is one way to show that knowledge; section 156 doesn't require one.

Can a compliance officer be outsourced?

The regulation requires you to appoint "a person" responsible for implementing the program, and FINTRAC's guidance says the officer can be you or another individual. Neither text says the officer must be your employee, and FINTRAC's guidance doesn't address outsourcing directly. Its examples are a senior manager, the owner or operator of a small business, or a senior person with direct access to senior management and the board, and it says a sole proprietor may appoint themselves or "choose to appoint someone else". FINTRAC's expectations of the role are the same whoever fills it.

Two rules in FINTRAC's guidance shape any outsourced arrangement:

  • The compliance officer may delegate certain duties to other employees, but remains responsible for implementing the compliance program.
  • Whoever is appointed, it is the reporting entity's responsibility to meet its compliance program requirements.

FINTRAC's assessment manual says examiners may confirm that the compliance officer has direct access to senior management or the board, and timely access to information from all business lines. An external officer has these only if you grant them. In our view, the appointment letter or agreement should grant both, and say who signs off on reports, policy changes and client decisions. Our guide to outsourced vs in-house compliance officers compares the two options in more detail.

Does a foreign MSB's compliance officer have to live in Canada?

We found no residency rule for compliance officers. Section 156 of the regulations and FINTRAC's compliance program guidance don't require the compliance officer to live in Canada or to be a Canadian citizen. The person a foreign MSB must have in Canada is a different role: someone who resides in Canada and is authorized to accept notices from FINTRAC on the business's behalf.

That second person appears separately in Schedule 1 of the Registration Regulations (Part B, item 8.1), in the same part as the compliance officer's contact details (item 11). Our guide to foreign MSB registration covers the representative for service in more detail.

Where the officer lives doesn't change what FINTRAC expects. They still need to understand Canadian requirements under the PCMLTFA, and they need the authority and access described above.

What FINTRAC checks about your compliance officer

In an examination, FINTRAC verifies four things about the compliance officer: appointment (selection), authority, knowledge and duties. Its compliance assessment manual says the focus is whether the officer is fulfilling their duties to implement a sound compliance program, judged by whether the areas examined are put into practice.

Based on the manual, keep these on file:

  • a document showing you formally appointed the compliance officer;
  • their job description and documents describing their authority;
  • an organizational chart showing their line to senior management or the board;
  • policies and procedures that give the officer enough guidance to meet the legal requirements;
  • a record of their background, experience and the AML training you have given them.

In our view, if your officer is outsourced, also keep the agreement setting out their access and sign-off authority.

Can a compliance officer be outsourced?

The regulation requires you to appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program, and FINTRAC's guidance doesn't say that person must be an employee. The officer can delegate duties but stays responsible, and your MSB stays responsible for meeting its requirements.

What qualifications do you need to be a compliance officer?

FINTRAC's guidance names no certification, degree or minimum experience. It says the officer needs the authority and resources to run the program, knowledge of your business and its money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risks, and an understanding of your PCMLTFA requirements.

Can I act as my own compliance officer for my MSB?

Yes, if your business is small. FINTRAC's guidance says the owner or operator of a small business can be the compliance officer, and a sole proprietor can appoint themselves. In a larger business, FINTRAC's best practice is an officer who isn't directly involved in receiving, transferring or paying funds.

Do I have to tell FINTRAC if my compliance officer changes?

Yes. The compliance officer's name, address, phone number and email are part of an MSB's registration information, and PCMLTFA subsection 11.13(1) requires you to report a change within 30 days after you become aware of it.

Does an MSB compliance officer have to live in Canada?

Section 156 of the regulations and FINTRAC's compliance program guidance set no residency requirement for the compliance officer. A foreign MSB must separately name a person who resides in Canada and is authorized to accept notices from FINTRAC.
