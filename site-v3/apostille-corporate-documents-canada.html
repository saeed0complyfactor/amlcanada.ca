Apostilling Canadian corporate documents for foreign banks and regulators

Guide · Published 21 October 2026 · 4 min read · AML Canada

Foreign banks, payment partners and regulators often want Canadian corporate documents with an apostille. Some government certificates can go straight to the authority. Most corporate records must be notarized first.

Key points

  • Get the receiving party's document list in writing before ordering anything.
  • Global Affairs Canada says business and corporate records must be notarized, apart from Corporations Canada certificates of incorporation with an original signature or seal.
  • Ontario apostilles business registry documents issued since 1991 without notarization.
  • A copy notarized in Ontario goes to Ontario's Official Documents Services, even if the original is a federal document.

Start with the receiving party's list

Each bank, payment partner and regulator sets its own requirements, and the destination country's law decides whether an apostille is needed at all. Global Affairs Canada and Ontario both say they can't advise on what a foreign authority wants. Ask the receiving party to confirm in writing:

  • which documents it needs, by name;
  • how recent each must be;
  • whether it accepts certified or notarized copies;
  • whether translations must be apostilled as well;
  • whether the country is a member of the Apostille Convention.

Our general guide to getting an apostille in Canada covers the steps, authorities and fees for any document.

Federal corporations: Corporations Canada documents

Documents issued by the Government of Canada go to Global Affairs Canada. Its document eligibility tool treats corporate documents differently by type:

  • Certificate of incorporation. A Corporations Canada certificate with an original signature or seal doesn't need notarizing. One without an original signature or seal must be notarized.
  • Certificates of compliance, existence and amendment, and articles of incorporation. The tool says business or corporate records must be notarized.

Where notarization is needed, the notary's province decides the route. Global Affairs Canada says a document notarized in Alberta, Ontario or Saskatchewan goes to that province, wherever it was issued. A federal certificate copied and notarized by an Ontario notary is apostilled by Ontario's Official Documents Services, at CA$16 per notarized document.

Ontario corporations: Ontario Business Registry documents

Ontario's Official Documents Services accepts business registry documents issued since 1991 by the Business and Personal Property Branch or the Ontario Business Registry without notarization. Ontario charges CA$32 to authenticate an Ontario government official document.

The Ontario Business Registry sells a certificate of status online for CA$26, available immediately, and copies of filed documents for CA$3 each. Ontario's guidance says it checks signatures and seals against its records and needs a hard copy; it won't authenticate electronic copies. Confirm with the eligibility portal that the version you order qualifies.

Companies registered in other provinces

British Columbia apostilles original documents from B.C. Registries and Online Services, such as a Notice of Articles, incorporation certificate, certificate of good standing and certified true copy. British Columbia requires notarization if the document has no Registrar's signature, or has no raised seal and you can't provide the online receipt. Alberta asks for corporate records to be notarized by an Alberta notary before it issues an apostille. Quebec apostilles documents issued in Quebec, including authentic acts such as notarized documents.

Minute book extracts, registers and resolutions

Your company's own records, such as register extracts, minute book pages and director or shareholder resolutions, are private documents. Global Affairs Canada's tool says business or corporate records, including corporate bylaws, must be notarized. Corporate powers of attorney fall under private legal documents, which must also be notarized.

Under Ontario's Notaries Act, a notary can witness and attest the signing of a document and certify a true copy, according to the Ministry of the Attorney General. The notary must be physically present, as remote notarization isn't permitted, and Ontario won't apostille a remotely notarized document. Ontario also needs the notary's signature and seal on the same page, and a name and seal that match its records.

Evidence of FINTRAC or Bank of Canada registration

Payment partners often ask an MSB or payment service provider to prove it is registered. FINTRAC's Money Services Business Registry is public and shows each business's registration number, status and dates. FINTRAC says it doesn't issue licences or certificates of registration. The Bank of Canada keeps a public registry of payment service providers and says it doesn't endorse or license them.

Global Affairs Canada's tool says documents from a Government of Canada department or agency that aren't on its list must be notarized. Ask the receiving party what evidence it will accept before anything is prepared. Our guides to registering an MSB and foreign MSB registration explain the registration itself.

Translations

Some countries want documents translated into their language. Depending on the recipient, you may need an apostille on the original only, or on both the original and the translation, as Quebec explains. Ask the receiving party which one it needs. For documents going into the Canadian process, Global Affairs Canada needs a certified translation of any content that isn't in English or French.

Common reasons documents come back

The authorities list these reasons on their official pages:

  • a plain photocopy, with no original signature or seal;
  • an electronic copy (Ontario needs a hard copy, and Global Affairs Canada needs paper);
  • a document commissioned by a commissioner for taking affidavits, which Ontario won't authenticate;
  • a document notarized remotely, in Ontario;
  • a notary's signature and seal on different pages, or a seal that doesn't match the authority's records;
  • glued or laminated documents, which Global Affairs Canada can't authenticate;
  • a document sent to the wrong competent authority;
  • a missing fee or incomplete request form.

Apostille for corporate documents: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Can a certificate of status be apostilled without notarization?

In Ontario, yes. Ontario's Official Documents Services accepts business registry documents issued since 1991 without notarization. Global Affairs Canada says federal certificates of compliance and existence must be notarized.

Do minute book extracts and resolutions need notarizing?

Yes. Global Affairs Canada says business or corporate records must be notarized before authentication.

Does FINTRAC issue a registration certificate?

No. FINTRAC says it doesn't issue licences or certificates of registration. Registration details appear in its public Money Services Business Registry.

Where does a notarized copy of a federal certificate go?

If an Ontario notary notarized it, to Ontario's Official Documents Services. Global Affairs Canada says documents notarized in Alberta, Ontario or Saskatchewan go to that province.

Can corporate documents be notarized remotely in Ontario?

No. Ontario's Notaries Act doesn't permit remote notarization, and Ontario won't apostille documents notarized remotely.
