The RPAA annual report for payment service providers

Guide · Published 21 October 2026 · 5 min read · AML Canada

Every payment service provider that performs retail payment activities in a calendar year must file an annual report with the Bank of Canada by March 31 of the following year. It covers your risk management and incident response framework, safeguarding, activity metrics and financial results.

Key points

  • File the report in PSP Connect by March 31 for the previous calendar year.
  • Financial metrics use your most recent financial year-end. Everything else uses the calendar year.
  • The form asks for monthly figures on held funds and transfers, so collect them as the year goes.
  • A late report costs $500 a day for up to 30 days, then $15,000 to $1,000,000.

Who files, and when

Section 21 of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) requires a payment service provider (PSP) that performs retail payment activities to submit an annual report to the Bank of Canada. Under section 18 of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations, a PSP that performs retail payment activities in a calendar year must submit the report for that year no later than March 31 of the following year. The report for 2026 is due by March 31, 2027.

The Bank's FAQ says the first annual report covered 2025. The Bank makes the form available in PSP Connect ahead of the deadline and, according to its step-by-step guide, notifies PSPs when it's ready to complete. If you aren't yet sure whether you're a PSP at all, start with who must register under the RPAA.

The reporting period

The reporting year is the calendar year (subsection 19(5) of the regulations). Financial metrics are the exception. The Bank's annual reporting policy says you give your most recent figures based on your financial year-end. The guide's example: if your year ends on June 30, you report financial metrics as at June 30, 2026 in the 2026 report, due by March 31, 2027.

Financial metrics are reported in Canadian dollars. If your statements are in another currency, you calculate the Canadian dollar equivalent. You may report unaudited figures if the audit for the year won't be finished before the deadline, or if you don't prepare audited statements for any other purpose.

What the report contains

Section 19 of the regulations lists the information. In summary:

  • Risk management and incident response framework. Changes made during the year and plans for maintaining the framework; its objectives, reliability targets and indicators; your operational risks and their causes; how you classified assets and business processes; the controls in place; the resources and roles assigned; how approvals were obtained; training; the reviews, testing and independent reviews carried out; and a description of any incidents.
  • Safeguarding, if you hold end-user funds. Who provides your safeguarding account and who regulates them; any other PSP through which you use an account; any insurer or guarantor and the terms of cover; how you safeguard funds; your safeguarding framework; each shortfall found during the year with its root cause and the measures taken; and any independent review. Our safeguarding guide covers these requirements.
  • Size and connections. The maximum value of end-user funds held at any time; for each month, the average end-of-day value of funds held and the number and value of transfers, broken down by currency and for end users in Canada; the number of end users; and the number of other PSPs you served. A PSP without a place of business in Canada reports the Canadian figures.
  • Other information. Significant changes made, activities started or stopped, changes in your use of third-party providers and agents, your record-keeping practices, and financial metrics including revenues, gross and operating profit or loss, assets, liabilities and equity.

The current form starts by asking you to confirm or update your registration information. It then has six sections: operational risk and incident management, safeguarding, significant changes and incidents, ubiquity and interconnectedness, financial information, and record-keeping. In the incident section you count every incident in the year by type, including those that weren't material enough to report under section 18.

How it's filed

You file through PSP Connect, under Reporting, then Annual Reporting. You can save your progress and return later, and the Bank sends a confirmation of receipt once you submit. Its policy says the form may change from year to year, with instructions when the questions change, and the Bank may ask for more information after it reviews your answers. Section 61 of the Act prohibits giving the Bank false or misleading information, and that includes the annual report.

Late or missing reports

Missing the deadline is a violation. For a violation of section 21, the regulations (section 48) set the penalty at $500 for each day it continues, for up to 30 days. After 30 days the range is $15,000 to $1,000,000. The Bank's June 2026 reminder says PSPs that don't meet reporting requirements may face enforcement action. On June 12, 2026, the Bank announced it would start publishing notices of violation and noting them on the PSP's entry in its public registry.

Other reports during the year

The annual report sits alongside event-driven filings, all made through PSP Connect:

ReportWhen it's neededTiming
Incident noticeAn incident with a material impact on an end user, another PSP or a designated clearing house (RPAA s.18)Without delay, and no later than 48 hours after you determine it's material
Significant change or new activityA change that could reasonably be expected to have a material impact on operational risks or safeguarding, or a new retail payment activity (RPAA s.22)At least five business days before
Change to registration informationChanges such as your name, address, structure, agents or third-party providers (RPAA s.59)Within 30 days after the change
Change to prescribed informationInformation used for the national security review, such as directors and major shareholders (RPAA s.60)As soon as feasible, and some changes 30 or 60 days before they take effect
Acquisition of controlSomeone plans to acquire control of the PSP (RPAA s.24)A new application, and registration under it, before the acquisition

One change can trigger more than one filing. The Bank's policy on reporting changes to registration information gives the example of a PSP that starts holding end-user funds and engages a new third-party provider: it files a significant change notice and a change of registration information. Our guide to RPAA incident reporting covers incident notices in detail.

Preparing through the year

Most of the work is collecting information you already produce, in the form the Bank asks for. A few habits make March easier:

  • Record held funds and transfer volumes and values each month, split by currency and between end users in Canada and elsewhere.
  • Log every incident, material or not, with its type.
  • Keep a shortfall log if you hold end-user funds, with the root cause and fix for each one.
  • Date your framework approvals. The regulations require your senior officer and board, if you have one, to approve the risk management and incident response framework at least once a year (section 5).
  • Keep the record of each review and test. The regulations also require records sufficient to show you comply (section 40).

Our RPAA compliance program includes a framework for the annual report and change notices, and outsourced compliance officer hours can cover preparing the report itself.

RPAA annual report: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

When is the RPAA annual report due?

By March 31 of the year after the reporting year. The report for 2026 is due by March 31, 2027.

What period does the RPAA annual report cover?

The previous calendar year. Financial metrics are the exception: you report them as at your most recent financial year-end.

Do I have to file if I don't hold end-user funds?

Yes, if you performed retail payment activities during the year. The safeguarding information applies only to PSPs that hold end-user funds.

What happens if a PSP files its annual report late?

It's a violation. The penalty is $500 a day for up to 30 days, then $15,000 to $1,000,000, and the Bank has said it will publish notices of violation.

Do the financial figures have to be audited?

No, not in every case. You may report unaudited figures if your audit won't be finished before the deadline or if you don't prepare audited statements for any other purpose.

RPAA incident reporting and the 48-hour rule

When a payment service provider becomes aware of an incident with a material impact, it must notify the Bank of Canada and the affected end users, payment service providers and clearing houses without delay. The Bank expects the first notice no later than 48 hours after the provider decides the incident is material.

RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds

Since September 8, 2025, a payment service provider that holds funds for its users must safeguard them, either in trust in a trust account or in a segregated account backed by insurance or a guarantee. It also needs a written safeguarding framework and an independent review at least every three years.

What is the RPAA?

The Retail Payment Activities Act is the federal law under which the Bank of Canada supervises payment service providers. It sets rules on operational risk, incidents and the safeguarding of users' funds.
