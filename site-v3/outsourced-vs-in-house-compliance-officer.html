Outsourced or in-house compliance officer?

Guide · Updated 11 October 2026 · 2 min read · AML Canada

Every business FINTRAC regulates needs a compliance officer. You can appoint the owner or a manager, hire someone, or outsource the role, and FINTRAC's expectations are the same in each case.

Key points

  • Every reporting entity must appoint a compliance officer.
  • The officer needs authority and access to resources and decision-makers.
  • Small businesses can appoint the owner or operator.
  • Your business stays responsible, however the role is filled.

On this page

What FINTRAC expects of a compliance officer

Appointing a compliance officer is the first required element of a compliance program. The person must have the necessary authority and access to resources to implement an effective program, and FINTRAC's best practice is that they can communicate directly with the people who make decisions about the business.

Who it should be depends on size. In a small business, it can be the owner or operator. For a larger business, FINTRAC's guidance suggests someone from a senior level with direct access to senior management, and says that as a best practice the officer shouldn't be directly involved in receiving, transferring or paying funds.

Your three options

1. Appoint the owner or an existing manager

Common in small businesses. It works when that person has the time and the knowledge. The risk is that compliance competes with everything else they do.

2. Hire an in-house compliance officer

Suits larger or fast-growing businesses with steady volumes. You get full-time attention and someone who knows the business from the inside. The costs are a senior salary and the challenge of finding someone experienced with Canadian requirements.

3. Use an outsourced compliance officer

An experienced compliance professional carries the day-to-day work, or supports the person you appoint, for the hours you need. They aren't in the business every day, so they rely on the information and access you give them. However it's arranged, your business remains responsible for its obligations, and the arrangement should be set out in writing so it's clear who decides and signs off on what.

What the work involves

Whichever option you choose, the work is the same: second-line review of customer onboarding, advice on cases and products, reporting to FINTRAC on time, keeping policies and the risk assessment current, client risk ratings, training, and preparing for the effectiveness review and any examination.

How to choose

  • If no one in the business has AML experience, an outsourced officer can bring it in while you build your own knowledge.
  • If you're based outside Canada, consider whether anyone on your team knows Canadian requirements well enough to run the program.
  • If volumes are high and steady, an in-house hire, supported by outside reviews, may make sense.
  • Some businesses start outsourced and move in-house as they grow.

Does every MSB need a compliance officer?

Yes. Appointing a compliance officer is a required part of every reporting entity's compliance program.

Can a small business owner be the compliance officer?

Yes. FINTRAC's guidance says that in a small business the owner or operator can be the compliance officer.
