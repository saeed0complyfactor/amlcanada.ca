How does an outsourced compliance officer work?

Guide · Published 19 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

An outsourced compliance officer works through escalation. Your business appoints an outside professional as the person responsible for implementing its compliance program, and your operations team runs onboarding and monitoring day to day, escalating files to that person for a compliance decision. Your business stays the reporting entity throughout, and this guide shows how the work divides.

Key points

  • An outsourced compliance officer makes the compliance decisions on the files and transactions your team escalates. Your team runs operations and all customer contact.
  • PCMLTFA s. 9.6(1) puts the duty to establish and implement a compliance program on your business, and FINTRAC says meeting the requirements stays your responsibility whoever is appointed.
  • The officer needs access to your folders, platforms and decision makers. FINTRAC's examiners may confirm direct access to senior management and timely access to information.
  • Put the split of work, the escalation route, sign-off authority and the effectiveness reviewer in writing before the arrangement starts.

How does an outsourced compliance officer work day to day?

An outsourced compliance officer works through escalation. Your operations team handles customers and systems every day, and brings the officer the onboarding files and transactions that need a compliance decision. The officer reviews each one, decides, and your team carries out the decision.

The legal anchor is paragraph 156(1)(a) of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations (PCMLTFR), which requires you to appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program. FINTRAC's compliance program guidance says that person is responsible for implementing all elements of the program.

A fractional compliance officer in Canada is usually part-time. In our engagements we are not watching your platforms full time, so the arrangement depends on your team knowing what to escalate and when. Our outsourced compliance officer service page covers the fee and how an engagement starts.

How an escalation works

Every escalation follows four steps, and each step has one owner: your team at the start and the end, the officer in the middle. In our engagements all onboarding and transaction escalations go through one shared Slack channel, so every escalation and decision is in one place.

  1. Your point of contact posts the client file or transaction in the shared channel, with the information we need to assess it.
  2. We carry out due diligence: we review the file and run a customer risk assessment under your compliance framework.
  3. We decide to approve, reject or request more information.
  4. Your operations or support team applies the decision and sends any request for information to the customer.

Your team handles all customer contact, so the customer relationship stays with your own staff. For what a complete escalated file should contain, see our MSB onboarding file review checklist.

What your team provides: a point of contact and access

Your team provides two things the officer can't supply alone: a trained point of contact in operations, and access to your records and systems. Without both, the officer can't review files or see the risks they are responsible for managing.

The point of contact escalates files and handles customer requests for information. We train them on what an escalated file needs to contain, so each file arrives with enough information to complete due diligence. Training is also a legal element of the program: if you have employees, agents or mandataries or other authorized persons, PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(d) requires a written, ongoing compliance training program for them.

On access, FINTRAC's compliance assessment manual says examiners may confirm two things about the compliance officer, where applicable:

  • direct access to senior management or the board of directors, or to those who make important decisions about compliance issues;
  • timely access to information from all business lines, so they know of potential compliance risks or concerns.

FINTRAC's guidance also says the officer needs the necessary authority and access to resources to implement an effective program and make any desired changes. An outside officer has that authority and access only if you grant them.

How transaction monitoring is shared

Monitoring is shared between your team and the outside officer. Your team runs the platforms day to day, and the officer reviews the alerts and transactions that need a compliance decision. As a fractional service, we rely on your team to bring us anything that needs that decision.

In our engagements your team turns rules on and off and releases transactions that don't need our review. We help deploy the rules and thresholds, review what you escalate, and decide whether a transaction should be reported. The rules themselves, and what FINTRAC expects of ongoing monitoring, are covered in our guide to FINTRAC ongoing and transaction monitoring.

In our view, a shared model works only if the escalation criteria are written down. Agree in advance which alert types always come to the officer and which your team may close.

Who is responsible when AML compliance is outsourced?

Your business stays responsible. Subsection 9.6(1) of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) says every person or entity referred to in section 5 shall establish and implement a compliance program. Subsection 9.6(1.1) adds that the person or entity shall ensure the program is reasonably designed, risk-based and effective.

The reporting duties sit with the business too. PCMLTFA section 7 requires every person or entity referred to in section 5 to report suspicious transactions to FINTRAC. When a provider files on your behalf, FINTRAC's compliance assessment manual says examiners check that the reports carry your business's identification information, not the provider's.

FINTRAC's guidance sets out how responsibility works inside the arrangement:

  • the compliance officer may delegate certain duties, but remains responsible for implementing the compliance program;
  • while the compliance officer is appointed, it is the reporting entity's responsibility to meet its compliance program requirements.

Senior management keeps its own legal roles. For an entity, a senior officer approves the written policies and procedures (PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(b)) and receives the effectiveness review findings in writing within 30 days after the review is completed (s. 156(4)).

Can a compliance officer be outsourced? FINTRAC's guidance doesn't say the officer must be an employee, and our guide to FINTRAC compliance officer requirements sets out what the rules say on who can hold the role.

What an outsourced compliance officer doesn't do

An outsourced compliance officer doesn't build your systems, doesn't run your effectiveness review, and doesn't operate your business. In our engagements three limits apply, and each one leaves a task with your team or with someone independent of us.

  • Integration is out of scope. We advise on onboarding controls and monitoring rules; building and connecting systems sits with your team or your providers.
  • We support the two-year effectiveness review and don't conduct it. PCMLTFR s. 156(3) requires the review to be done by an internal or external auditor, or by you if you have no auditor, and FINTRAC's best practice is a reviewer who isn't directly involved in your compliance program activities.
  • Our work is advice, review and decisions on what you escalate. We make sure your documentation and processes meet the requirements, and your team carries out that advice in day-to-day operations.

Our effectiveness review checklist shows what the reviewer tests.

What to have in writing

Before the arrangement starts, record who does what, who can decide, and what the officer can see. FINTRAC's compliance assessment manual says examiners may review documents showing you formally appointed the compliance officer, their job description, documents describing their authority, and an organizational chart.

In our view, the written agreement or appointment should cover:

  • the formal appointment of the outside officer as the person responsible for implementing your compliance program;
  • the who-does-what split for reporting, policies, onboarding, monitoring, training and the effectiveness review;
  • the escalation channel, what a complete file contains, and which alerts always come to the officer;
  • who signs off on FINTRAC reports, policy changes and client decisions;
  • the folders and platforms the officer can access, and their line to senior management or the board;
  • the name of your point of contact and who sends requests for information to customers;
  • who appoints the effectiveness reviewer;
  • a statement that your business remains the reporting entity.

How an outsourced CO works: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Can a compliance officer be outsourced?

PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(a) requires you to appoint a person responsible for implementing your compliance program, and FINTRAC's guidance doesn't say that person must be an employee. Our guide to MSB compliance officer requirements covers who can hold the role.

What exactly does a compliance officer do?

FINTRAC's guidance says the compliance officer is responsible for implementing all elements of the compliance program. Under PCMLTFR s. 156(1), those elements are written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, a training program and plan, and a plan for the two-year effectiveness review.

Who is responsible for AML compliance?

The reporting entity. PCMLTFA s. 9.6(1) requires every person or entity referred to in section 5 to establish and implement a compliance program, and FINTRAC's guidance says meeting those requirements is the reporting entity's responsibility even after a compliance officer is appointed.

Does an outsourced compliance officer watch transactions full time?

Not in a fractional arrangement like ours. Your team runs day-to-day monitoring and escalates the alerts and transactions that need a compliance decision, and the officer reviews those and decides whether to report.

Can the outsourced compliance officer do the effectiveness review?

We don't. PCMLTFR s. 156(3) requires the review every two years by an internal or external auditor, or by you if you have no auditor, and FINTRAC's best practice is a reviewer not directly involved in your compliance program activities.
