FINTRAC training requirements: the program, the plan and the records

Guide · Published 11 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

FINTRAC training requirements come from section 156 of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Regulations. If anyone works or acts for you, you need a written, ongoing compliance training program and a documented plan for delivering it. Neither the Regulations nor FINTRAC fix how often you train, so your plan sets the frequency and your records should show you kept to it.

Key points

  • PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(d) and (e) require a written, ongoing training program and a documented plan to deliver it if anyone acts for you.
  • No training frequency is fixed in law. FINTRAC's guidance says your plan should state one, using regular intervals, trigger events or both.
  • Train client-facing staff, agents, anyone who handles funds or virtual currency, and the people who run the program, including the compliance officer.
  • Under the penalty regulations, failing the training requirement is a very serious violation.

Is AML training a legal requirement in Canada?

Yes, for any reporting entity with employees, agents, mandataries or other people authorized to act for it. Section 9.6(1) of the PCMLTFA requires a compliance program, and paragraphs 156(1)(d) and (e) of the Regulations make a written, ongoing training program and a documented plan for delivering it part of that program.

As of 11 November 2026, section 156 of the Regulations requires you to:

  • s. 156(1)(d): develop and maintain a written, ongoing compliance training program for your employees, agents or mandataries and other persons authorized to act on your behalf;
  • s. 156(1)(e): institute and document a plan for that ongoing training program, and deliver the training.

The duty sits with the business. FINTRAC's compliance program guidance lists training as one of the elements every reporting entity must implement, with a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment and a two-year effectiveness review. Our guide to FINTRAC compliance program requirements covers the other elements.

FINTRAC says a sole proprietor with no employees, agents or other individuals authorized to act for them doesn't need a training program or a training plan for themselves.

Who do FINTRAC training requirements cover?

Your training plan should name who gets trained. FINTRAC's guidance says recipients should include anyone who has contact with clients, is involved in client transactions, handles cash, funds or virtual currency for you in any way, or is responsible for implementing or overseeing the compliance program.

  • front-line staff and agents who deal with clients;
  • staff involved in client transaction activities;
  • anyone who handles cash, funds or virtual currency for you;
  • the compliance officer, senior management, information technology staff and internal auditors.

FINTRAC's glossary defines a mandatary as a person who acts for another person or entity under a mandate or agreement. If someone acts for you under a contract, check whether they belong in the plan.

The compliance officer needs training too. FINTRAC's assessment manual says examiners look at the officer's background and experience, and at the training you have given them. They check that the officer knows your business's functions and structure and your sector's money laundering and terrorist financing risks.

What should FINTRAC compliance training cover?

FINTRAC says your training program should explain five things to the people who act for you. Those are your obligations under the Act and Regulations, background on money laundering and terrorist financing, how your business could be vulnerable, your own policies and procedures, and each person's role in detecting and deterring suspicious activity.

  1. Your requirements under the PCMLTFA and its Regulations.
  2. Background, such as the definitions and methods of money laundering and terrorist activity financing.
  3. Vulnerabilities: how your business could be used, with indicators and examples.
  4. Your policies and procedures, including reporting, record keeping and know your client.
  5. Roles and responsibilities in detecting and deterring money laundering and terrorist financing, and when dealing with potentially suspicious activities or transactions.

The program and plan should fit your size, structure and complexity and your exposure to money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion risk. A large business may give general training to some people and specialized training to others according to their roles. FINTRAC says the plan should explain that choice.

The assessment manual says examiners pay close attention to the training you provide on detecting suspicious transactions.

How often is AML training required in Canada?

Canadian law sets no fixed interval. Paragraph 156(1)(d) of the Regulations requires an "ongoing" training program, and neither the Regulations nor FINTRAC's compliance program guidance names a set period. FINTRAC asks you to choose a frequency, write it in your training plan and deliver training on that basis.

FINTRAC's guidance describes three ways to set it:

  • Regular intervals. FINTRAC's examples are monthly, semi-annually and annually.
  • Trigger events. FINTRAC's examples are before a new employee deals with clients and after a procedure is changed.
  • A combination of regular intervals and events.

Base the choice on your risks and how your business runs. One of FINTRAC's own event examples is training a new employee before they deal with clients. If a new product or a change in the law leads you to rewrite a procedure, the people who follow that procedure should be trained on the new version.

The two-year effectiveness review is a separate duty. Under s. 156(1)(f) and (3), a review must test the program, including the training program and plan, every two years. It checks your training and doesn't count as training.

What goes in a FINTRAC training plan? A checklist

A FINTRAC training plan documents how you will implement and deliver your ongoing training program. FINTRAC says it should cover recipients, topics and materials, delivery methods and frequency, and the program should keep a record of training delivered. FINTRAC AML training for MSBs and their agents

Money services businesses meet the same training requirement as every other reporting entity, and it reaches their agents. FINTRAC's recipient list names front-line staff or agents with client contact and anyone who handles cash, funds or virtual currency for you. Every agent and every person who moves funds or virtual currency belongs in an MSB's plan.

Points worth checking in an MSB training plan:

  • Agents are named as recipients, and you keep training records for agent staff as well as your own.
  • Training covers the reports you file and how staff escalate a possible suspicious transaction, since examiners focus on suspicious transaction detection.
  • Training follows your written policies, which FINTRAC expects to cover the travel rule for electronic funds and virtual currency transfers and ministerial directives.

FINTRAC's assessment manual says examiners may interview employees and agents. They confirm that people understand the requirements for their positions, follow your policies and procedures, understand how your business could be vulnerable and have received adequate ongoing training.

How does FINTRAC examine your training program?

During an examination, FINTRAC looks at who receives training, what topics are covered, when and how often training takes place, how you have implemented the program and how it is delivered, according to its assessment manual. It also checks that the program is adequate for your business and put into practice.

To do that, the manual says FINTRAC may:

  • review your policies and procedures for guidance on running an ongoing training program;
  • review your training plan for the steps you take to develop, maintain and deliver training;
  • review your training material to confirm it suits your business and each person's responsibilities;
  • interview employees and agents.

Section 9.6(1.1) of the PCMLTFA, added by S.C. 2026, c. 4, s. 79, requires your program to be reasonably designed, risk-based and effective. Under the penalty regulations, schedule Part 2, item 199, contravening s. 156(1)(d) and (e) is a very serious violation. Section 5(c) of those regulations sets the range for a very serious violation at $1 to $20,000,000, subject to subsections 73.1(2) and (3) of the Act. Our guide to FINTRAC penalties explains how amounts are set.

Your own effectiveness review covers the same ground first. FINTRAC's examples of review methods include interviews with those handling transactions to evaluate their knowledge of your policies and procedures. See preparing for a FINTRAC examination for the documents to have ready.

Training requirements: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Is it a FINTRAC requirement that you complete AML/CTF training?

The requirement is placed on the business. A reporting entity with employees, agents or other authorized persons must keep a written, ongoing training program and deliver it under a documented plan (PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(d) and (e)). The people FINTRAC says should be trained, including the compliance officer and senior management, take part through that program.

Does FINTRAC approve or certify AML training courses?

Section 156 and FINTRAC's compliance program guidance don't name an approved course or certification. FINTRAC says instructors should know the Act and Regulations, and that if you use an external provider you may need to decide whether its services and content suit your business.

Can FINTRAC training be done online?

Yes. FINTRAC lists self-directed learning, including online courses and e-learning materials, among the delivery methods. You can use one method or several, depending on your size and how many people need training.

What should a training record include?

FINTRAC's examples are the date the training took place, a list of the attendees and the topics covered. Records help you schedule the next sessions and show that you deliver training on an ongoing basis.
