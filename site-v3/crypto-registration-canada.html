Registering a crypto business with FINTRAC

Guide · Updated 11 October 2026 · 2 min read · AML Canada

Crypto exchanges and virtual currency dealers register with FINTRAC as money services businesses, whether they're based in Canada or serve Canadians from abroad.

Key points

  • Dealing in virtual currency is a money service: register with FINTRAC first.
  • Foreign crypto businesses serving Canada register as foreign MSBs.
  • Report virtual currency receipts of $10,000+ within 5 working days.
  • Trading platforms also deal with provincial securities regulators.

Crypto businesses register as MSBs

Dealing in virtual currency is one of the money services FINTRAC lists, so crypto exchanges and virtual currency dealers register with FINTRAC as money services businesses before they start. A crypto business outside Canada that directs and provides these services to people in Canada registers as a foreign MSB. The registration steps are the same as for any MSB.

Large virtual currency transaction reports

When you receive virtual currency worth $10,000 or more, you file a large virtual currency transaction report (LVCTR) within 5 working days after the day you receive it. Under the 24-hour rule, this includes several receipts within a 24-hour window that total $10,000 or more, when you know they were made by or for the same person or entity, or for the same beneficiary. See FINTRAC's LVCTR guidance. Suspicious transaction reports apply too, with no minimum amount. Our FINTRAC reporting guide covers both.

The travel rule for virtual currency

When you send a virtual currency transfer, you must include the requester's name, address and account or reference number, and the beneficiary's name, address and account or reference number. When you receive transfers, you must take reasonable measures to make sure that information is included. You can't strip received travel-rule information out of a transfer.

Record keeping

For virtual currency transfers of $1,000 or more, MSBs keep detailed records, including the date, the type and amount of virtual currency, client and beneficiary details, transaction hashes, sending and receiving addresses, and the exchange rates used. Records are generally kept for at least five years.

Provincial rules

FINTRAC registration is federal. Crypto trading platforms are also regulated by provincial securities regulators. A platform operating in Canada is expected to register with them or, while it seeks registration, file a pre-registration undertaking, under CSA Staff Notice 21-332. Platforms that let clients trade stablecoins, which securities regulators call value-referenced crypto assets, are expected to follow the terms in CSA Staff Notice 21-333.

Quebec also licenses money-services businesses through Revenu Québec. British Columbia has passed its own Money Services Businesses Act, which isn't in force yet; see Ontario vs B.C. MSB registration. We'll tell you when you need separate advice on these rules.

Changes in 2025 and 2026

Since October 1, 2025, regulations strengthening the MSB registration framework have been in force, including obligations when MSBs engage agents or mandataries.

Bill C-12, which received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026, raised the maximum administrative monetary penalty for violations after that date to $4 million per violation for a person and $20 million for an entity. It also requires compliance agreements after penalties, adds compliance orders, and requires compliance programs to be reasonably designed, risk-based and effective. FINTRAC lists these on its changes page.

The Budget 2025 Implementation Act, which also received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026, created the Stablecoin Act. It isn't in force yet. Under the federal stablecoin framework, stablecoin issuers will register with the Bank of Canada, which will supervise them, and FINTRAC says they will also register with it as MSBs dealing in virtual currency.

Your compliance program

A crypto MSB needs the same five-part compliance program as any MSB, written for crypto risks: wallet screening, blockchain analytics, travel rule handling and virtual currency reporting. Our 14-document program includes a Travel Rule Policy and Procedure.

Crypto registration: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Do crypto exchanges need to register in Canada?

Yes. Dealing in virtual currency is a money service, so crypto exchanges and dealers register with FINTRAC as MSBs. Trading platforms also deal with provincial securities regulators.

What is an LVCTR?

A large virtual currency transaction report, filed within 5 working days when you receive virtual currency worth $10,000 or more, including linked receipts that the 24-hour rule adds together.

Does the travel rule apply to crypto in Canada?

Yes. MSBs must include requester and beneficiary information when they send virtual currency transfers, and take reasonable measures to check it's included when they receive them.

Is the Stablecoin Act in force?

Not yet. It received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026, and will come into force once its regulations are made.

A money services business must register with FINTRAC before it starts operating, and renew every two years. FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee, and it says the majority of complete applications are processed within three months.
