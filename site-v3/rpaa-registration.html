RPAA registration with the Bank of Canada.

Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) registration for payment service providers. Fixed fee US$3,500, plus the Bank's application fee.

Book a consultation Read on Fixed fee · US$3,500

Payment service providers that perform retail payment activities for people and businesses in Canada must register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA).

Payment processors, digital wallets, money transfer apps and similar businesses fall under the Bank's supervision. A new payment service provider (PSP) must be registered before it starts retail payment activities, so plan your launch around the Bank's review.

We confirm whether the RPAA applies to you, prepare the application with you, and help you answer the Bank's questions during its review.

Our fee is a fixed US$3,500. The Bank of Canada charges its own one-time application fee, which you pay directly to the Bank.

Does the RPAA apply to you?

The Act covers businesses that perform payment functions for end users as a service or business activity, such as holding accounts or funds for end users, initiating, authorizing or transmitting electronic funds transfers, and providing clearing or settlement services. A PSP with a place of business in Canada is covered. A PSP outside Canada is covered when it directs retail payment activities at people or entities in Canada and performs them for end users in Canada (RPAA s.5). Foreign firms often need FINTRAC registration as well; see our foreign MSB registration guide.

Banks and some other institutions are excluded, and some activities fall outside the Act, so we confirm where you stand before preparing anything. Our RPAA guide explains the Act in more detail.

What it costs

Our fee is a fixed US$3,500. The Bank's one-time, non-refundable application fee was CA$2,500 when it was introduced in November 2024 and is indexed to inflation each January, so we confirm the current amount before you apply.

The Bank also plans to charge registered PSPs annual assessment fees to recover its supervision costs. It isn't charging them yet and will start once the cost-recovery regulations are finalized.

How we handle your application

  1. Assessment. Which of your activities are retail payment activities, and whether you need FINTRAC registration as well.
  2. Preparation. Your business, ownership, payment activities, agents, mandataries and third-party providers, gathered and checked.
  3. Application. Submitted to the Bank of Canada, with the application fee.
  4. Review. The Bank shares applications with the Department of Finance and FINTRAC, and the Minister of Finance can order a national security review. We help you answer questions along the way.
  5. Decision. Once registered, your business is listed in the Bank's public registry of PSPs.

In our experience the process often takes 2 to 8 months, though that isn't guaranteed. A new PSP can't start retail payment activities until the Bank registers it, so we plan the timeline with you from the start.

After registration

Registered PSPs need a risk management and incident response framework and, if they hold end-user funds, a safeguarding framework. They also report incidents, changes and an annual report to the Bank. Our RPAA compliance program sets these up for a fixed US$3,500.

RPAA and FINTRAC: do you need both?

Often, yes. A payment business that remits or transmits funds usually needs FINTRAC registration as an MSB as well. The Bank's regime deals with operational risk and the safety of users' funds, and FINTRAC's with money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion. We can prepare both registrations together.

How much does RPAA registration cost?

Our fee is a fixed US$3,500. The Bank of Canada also charges a one-time, non-refundable application fee, which was CA$2,500 when introduced in November 2024 and is indexed to inflation each January. We confirm the current amount before you apply.

Is the Bank of Canada fee included in your price?

No. The application fee is paid directly to the Bank and isn't part of our US$3,500.

Do foreign payment service providers have to register?

Yes, if they direct retail payment activities at people or entities in Canada and perform them for end users in Canada.

Do I also need to register with FINTRAC?

Often. If you also provide money services, such as remitting or transmitting funds, you need FINTRAC registration as an MSB too.

How long does RPAA registration take?

In our experience it often takes 2 to 8 months, including any national security review. That isn't guaranteed, and a new PSP can't start retail payment activities until it's registered.

What happens after registration?

You need risk management, incident response and, if you hold end-user funds, safeguarding frameworks, plus incident notices, change notices and annual reports. Our RPAA compliance program covers these.

RPAA

The RPAA compliance frameworks a PSP needs

Every payment service provider needs a written risk management and incident response framework, and one that holds end-user funds also needs a safeguarding-of-funds framework. Third parties, agents and the senior officer's role are built into those two documents. Record keeping rules cover both, and the annual report, change notices and incident notices draw on them.

Read the guide RPAA

RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration: do you need both?

Payment service providers register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. Money services businesses register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Many payment businesses are both, and the two registrations are linked: the Bank shares your application with FINTRAC and can refuse it based on what FINTRAC reports.

Read the guide RPAA

Who must register as a PSP under the RPAA?

You must register with the Bank of Canada if you perform a payment function as a business in its own right, for electronic transfers in Canadian or foreign currency, with a link to Canada, and no exclusion applies. This guide shows how the Bank applies each part of that test to real business models.
