RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration: do you need both?

Guide · Published 21 October 2026 · 4 min read · AML Canada

Payment service providers register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. Money services businesses register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Many payment businesses are both, and the two registrations are linked: the Bank shares your application with FINTRAC and can refuse it based on what FINTRAC reports.

Key points

  • The RPAA deals with operational risk and safeguarding. The PCMLTFA deals with money laundering and terrorist financing.
  • A remittance business is usually both a PSP and an MSB. A crypto-only exchange or a cash currency counter can be an MSB without being a PSP.
  • The Bank sends your application details to FINTRAC, and FINTRAC reports back within 30 days.
  • If you're an MSB, put the FINTRAC registration in place first or alongside the Bank application.

Two laws, two regulators

The Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) gives the Bank of Canada the job of supervising payment service providers (PSPs) to check they comply with the Act. Its focus is operational risk, incident response and the safeguarding of end users' funds. The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA) is Canada's anti-money laundering law, and FINTRAC supervises the businesses it covers, including money services businesses (MSBs).

The tests are different. You're a PSP because of the payment functions you perform. You're an MSB because of the money services you provide. One business can meet both tests, one test or neither. The Bank's own registration guide says applicants must register with FINTRAC if they are considered an MSB.

Which business models need both

The Bank's published case scenarios, read with the MSB services listed in the PCMLTFA, show how common models fall:

  • Money transfer and remittance apps. The Bank treats a money transfer operator as a PSP, and one that also offers a multi-currency wallet holds funds as well (remittance scenarios). Remitting or transmitting funds is also an MSB service. Expect to need both.
  • Crypto-backed payment cards. The Bank's crypto scenarios treat a crypto-backed prepaid card that earns interchange fees as a PSP. Dealing in virtual currencies is an MSB service. Expect to need both.
  • Crypto-only exchanges. The Bank says an exchange dealing only in cryptocurrency performs no retail payment activity. Dealing in virtual currencies still makes it an MSB. FINTRAC only. Our crypto registration guide covers that side.
  • Cash currency exchange counters. The Bank's example of a business exchanging currency for cash isn't a PSP, because no electronic transfer is involved. Foreign exchange dealing is an MSB service. FINTRAC only.
  • Payment gateways and marketplaces. These can be PSPs without moving money in the way a remitter does. Whether they're also MSBs depends on what they do with funds, so check the FINTRAC side separately.

How the Bank and FINTRAC share information

The link between the two registrations is written into both Acts:

  • The Bank's application asks whether you're registered with FINTRAC (RPAA paragraph 29(1)(l)). Its step-by-step guide asks for your FINTRAC registration number and issue date.
  • Once the Bank considers your application complete, it sends FINTRAC your name, address, structure, agents and description of activities (RPAA section 31).
  • Within 30 days, FINTRAC must tell the Bank if, in the past five years, you were found guilty of certain PCMLTFA offences or served with a decision or penalty for a serious or very serious violation, or if you aren't registered under the PCMLTFA (PCMLTFA section 53.6).
  • After registration, FINTRAC tells the Bank if it revokes your FINTRAC registration or penalizes you for a serious or very serious violation, and it may share other compliance information relevant to the Bank's role (sections 53.6 and 65.03).

On the Bank's side, these are grounds to refuse or revoke. The RPAA lets the Bank refuse an applicant that has such a conviction or penalty, or that isn't registered in accordance with section 11.1 of the PCMLTFA (section 48). The same matters are grounds to revoke an existing registration (section 52). The Bank's supervisory framework states that it can refuse or revoke registration based on information provided by FINTRAC.

Order of operations

For a business that needs both, a sensible sequence is:

  1. Map your activities against both tests. List each product and the payment functions and money services it involves.
  2. Put your FINTRAC registration in place first, or file both together. The Bank asks for your FINTRAC number, and FINTRAC reports to the Bank if you aren't registered.
  3. Deal with any open FINTRAC issues. A serious or very serious violation in the past five years is a refusal ground under the RPAA.
  4. Prepare the Bank application. Describe the same business in the same way in both, since the Bank passes your application details to FINTRAC.
  5. Keep both registrations current. Report changes to each regulator on its own timeline, and renew with FINTRAC every two years.

RPAA vs FINTRAC: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

What is the difference between a PSP and an MSB in Canada?

A payment service provider performs payment functions and registers with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. A money services business provides money services, such as remitting funds or dealing in virtual currencies, and registers with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. One business can be both.

Does RPAA registration replace FINTRAC registration?

No. They're separate registrations under separate laws. The Bank asks for your FINTRAC registration details in its application.

Can the Bank of Canada refuse my application because of FINTRAC?

Yes. The RPAA lets the Bank refuse an applicant that isn't registered under section 11.1 of the PCMLTFA, or that has certain PCMLTFA convictions or serious or very serious violations in the past five years.

Should I register with FINTRAC or the Bank of Canada first?

If you're an MSB, have the FINTRAC registration in place first or file both together, because the Bank asks for your FINTRAC number and FINTRAC reports to the Bank if you aren't registered.

Does a crypto exchange need both registrations?

Not always. In the Bank's example, an exchange dealing only in cryptocurrency isn't a PSP, but dealing in virtual currencies makes it an MSB. A crypto-backed payment card is a PSP in the Bank's example.

How to register an MSB in Canada

A money services business must register with FINTRAC before it starts operating, and renew every two years. FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee, and it says the majority of complete applications are processed within three months.

FINTRAC compliance program requirements

Every business FINTRAC regulates must establish and implement a compliance program. It has five parts: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan, and a review of the program at least every two years.
