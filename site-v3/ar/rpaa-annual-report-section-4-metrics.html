How to complete Section 4 of the RPAA annual report

Guide · Published 3 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

Section 4 of the RPAA annual report collects your ubiquity and interconnectedness metrics: end-user funds held, the monthly number and value of electronic funds transfers, and how many end users and other PSPs you served. Each figure is split by currency and between all end users and end users in Canada. This guide covers each question, the Bank of Canada's counting rules and the checks to run before the March 31, 2027 deadline.

النقاط الرئيسية

  • Count each completed transfer once, at its final value, before netting and excluding fees.
  • Every all-end-user figure must be equal to or greater than the matching figure for end users in Canada.
  • Put a number in every cell, using zero where nothing happened, and explain a zero year.
  • Bank staff said the metrics feed supervisory priorities and, expected from 2027, assessment fees.

What does Section 4 of the RPAA annual report ask for?

Section 4 asks for the information that paragraph 19(4)(a) of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations (RPAR) calls ubiquity and interconnectedness. A payment service provider (PSP) with a place of business in Canada reports its peak end-user funds held, 12 months of held-funds and transfer data, its number of end users and the number of other PSPs it served.

The Bank's step-by-step guide to the annual report (February 2026) sets out the questions. Question 4.1 asks which designated systems, such as Lynx, you join directly. Question 4.2 asks whether you have a place of business in Canada, which decides what follows:

  • 4.3: peak end-user funds held during the year, in Canadian dollars.
  • 4.4: monthly average end-of-day funds held, in Canadian dollars and by currency.
  • 4.5 and 4.6: monthly number and value of electronic funds transfers (EFTs), in total and by currency.
  • 4.7 and 4.8: end users served, and other PSPs served, with the number of those PSPs that have a place of business in Canada.
  • 4.9: estimated share of EFT value by payment method, totalling 100%.

The calculation rules sit in the Bank's policy Annual reporting of retail payment activity metrics (October 16, 2024). Numbering may change on the next form. Our RPAA annual report guide covers the deadline and other sections.

How do you count electronic funds transfers for the annual report?

For the RPAA annual report, count each completed transaction between a payer and a payee in which you performed at least one payment function, once, at its final value and excluding fees. Section 2 of the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA) defines an EFT as a placement, transfer or withdrawal of funds by electronic means, initiated by or on behalf of an individual or entity.

The metrics policy adds:

  • A transaction counts once, however many functions, steps or instructions it involves, and even if you handle both sides.
  • Transactions you perform for another PSP count too, so several PSPs in one chain each report the same transaction.
  • A batch counts as its underlying transactions, and settlement netting reduces neither the number nor the value.
  • Only completed transactions count, at the final amount where it differs from the authorized amount.
  • Refunds and unclaimed transactions are included where relevant to your operations.

In the policy's examples, eight card payments worth $1,000 that settle as a $50 net batch are 8 EFTs worth $1,000. A fuel purchase authorized at $100 that finalizes at $55 is 1 EFT worth $55.

Leave out excluded activity, such as merchant gift cards, ATM cash withdrawals and transactions among affiliates. A designated-system transfer is excluded only if every function happens inside that system. Include transfers by agents named in your registration.

Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 supervision update that some PSPs misread the EFT definition, without saying how. Test your count against each rule above.

How do all end users and end users in Canada differ in Section 4?

All end users means everyone you served, in Canada and elsewhere, so each all-end-user figure must be equal to or greater than the matching figure for end users in Canada. The Bank's February 2026 step-by-step guide states this rule for questions 4.3 to 4.9, because the all-end-user total already includes Canadian end users.

The RPAA defines an end user as a payer or payee using a payment service. For question 4.7, the metrics policy says to count distinct end users served during the year, so a customer who pays 50 times counts once. Payers and payees both count. Estimates from account or merchant numbers are allowed. Report zero if you dealt only with other PSPs.

Where you don't know an end user's location, the policy asks for a best-efforts estimate. Record the method and apply it to every metric.

For question 4.8, count the other PSPs you performed retail payment activities for, including banks, credit unions and Payments Canada, which the RPAA doesn't cover. Leave out a PSP that only buys services from you for its own operations.

How do you report monthly held funds and currency breakdowns?

The monthly average of held funds in Section 4 is the sum of each day's end-of-day balance of end-user funds divided by the number of days in that month. The Bank's metrics policy sets this method for question 4.4, reported in Canadian dollars and in each original currency, for all end users and for end users in Canada.

The policy doesn't treat weekends or holidays separately, so every day needs a balance. Question 4.3 asks instead for the single highest value held at any moment in the year, which can't be lower than any monthly average. Both usually come from the ledger behind your safeguarding records.

If you don't hold funds, the step-by-step guide says to enter zeros for questions 4.3 and 4.4 and explain in the open-text field. Explain any metric that totals zero for the year the same way, and report only the months you operated.

For currencies, report counts in total and per currency, and values in Canadian dollars combined, then each currency in its own units. Section 1.5 of the metrics policy lets you convert at the Bank's monthly exchange rates or at the rates you use in the normal course of business. Pick one source and document it.

What does a PSP without a place of business in Canada report?

A PSP without a place of business in Canada answers questions 4.10 to 4.16, which ask only about activity for end users in Canada. Paragraph 19(4)(b) of the RPAR limits its metrics to ubiquity and interconnectedness in Canada, and it counts only other PSPs that have a place of business in Canada.

The questions mirror 4.3 to 4.9, and the counting and currency rules are the same. The Bank treats a location here, including a home office, a Canadian incorporation, or staff or agents in Canada as a place of business, and those PSPs report all end users too. Our guide on who must register as a PSP explains that test. The main risk for a foreign PSP is putting worldwide totals in fields meant for Canada only.

Which Section 4 errors did the Bank see in the first cycle?

Bank staff said at the 2 November 2026 supervision update that Section 4 was where they saw particular data-quality problems in the first annual reports, which covered 2025. They named blank fields, all-end-user metrics lower than the Canadian figures, and misread guidance, including the EFT definition. As of 2 November 2026, the Bank was contacting those PSPs for clarification.

What changes for the March 2027 filing, and why do the metrics matter more?

For the report due March 31, 2027, covering 2026, Bank staff said the form will add validation rules for incomplete or inconsistent entries, clearer questions, better numbering and skip logic, improved summaries and bulk entry of metrics by copy and paste, including from Excel. It will open in PSP Connect well ahead of the deadline.

Validation can flag a Canadian figure above an all-end-user figure, but it can't see a transfer counted twice. Section 61 of the RPAA prohibits giving the Bank false or misleading information. Map your workbook to the new question numbers before you paste.

Staff said annual reports help the Bank understand the sector and target follow-ups, and "the information supports decisions about where we focus our supervisory attention." They also said the report will be used to set annual assessment fees, expected from 2027, with interim bills in spring 2027 based on 2026 metrics. The fee regulations still await Finance and Treasury Board, staff couldn't name the billing metrics, and they said timelines may change. As of 3 November 2026, treat Section 4 as data that may drive a bill.

How do you reconcile Section 4 before you submit?

Reconcile Section 4 by tying each figure to a source system, testing how the figures relate and recording every method and estimate. This checklist follows the Bank's metrics policy and step-by-step guide:

  1. Place of business. Check your answer to question 4.2.
  2. Scope. List each payment flow, remove excluded and affiliate activity, and add listed agents.
  3. No blanks. Put a number in every cell and explain any metric that totals zero for the year.
  4. Superset test. For every month, confirm all end users is at or above Canada, and total PSPs at or above Canadian PSPs.
  5. Held funds. Confirm the daily series covers every calendar day and no monthly average exceeds the peak.
  6. Transfers. Trace a sample from each flow, including refunds, and confirm each completed transaction appears once, at its final amount before netting.
  7. Currencies. Confirm currency rows convert to the Canadian dollar totals at one documented rate source.
  8. End users. Deduplicate across the year and record how you located users of unknown country.
  9. Trends. Investigate sharp monthly jumps or drops.
  10. Records. Keep the workbook, queries and sign-off. Under RPAR section 40, your records must be enough to show compliance.

Have someone who didn't prepare the figures run the checks. A documented method also speeds up your reply if the Bank follows up.

RPAA annual report Section 4: common questions

ما زلت غير متأكد؟ احجز استشارة أو راسلنا على info@amlcanada.ca.

What are ubiquity and interconnectedness metrics in the RPAA annual report?

They are the Section 4 figures on end-user funds held, monthly transfers, end users and other PSPs served, required by paragraph 19(4) of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations.

How do I count EFTs for the Bank of Canada annual report?

Count each completed transaction in which you performed a payment function once, at its final value, excluding fees. Netting doesn't reduce the number or value.

Can my all end users figure be lower than my Canadian end users figure?

No. The all-end-user figure includes end users in Canada, so it must be equal or greater. Bank staff said the reverse was a common error in the first annual reports.

What exchange rate should I use for the RPAA annual report?

The Bank of Canada's monthly exchange rates or your normal business rates. Use one source and record it.

Do I complete Section 4 if I don't hold end-user funds?

Yes. Enter zeros for the funds-held questions and explain in the open-text field. You still report transfers, end users and other PSPs served.
