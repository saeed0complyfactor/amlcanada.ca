Guides to FINTRAC and RPAA compliance.

Plain-English answers to the questions we hear most, checked against FINTRAC and the Bank of Canada.

All guides

Virtual currency

Ontario vs B.C. MSB registration: which province suits a crypto startup?

Today there's no MSB difference between the two provinces. A money services business in Ontario or British Columbia registers with FINTRAC and nobody else, because B.C.'s Money Services Businesses Act has been passed but isn't in force. That should change: B.C. plans to start provincial registration, and Ontario has no equivalent law.

Read the guide RPAA

RPAA incident reporting and the 48-hour rule

When a payment service provider becomes aware of an incident with a material impact, it must notify the Bank of Canada and the affected end users, payment service providers and clearing houses without delay. The Bank expects the first notice no later than 48 hours after the provider decides the incident is material.

Read the guide RPAA

RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration: do you need both?

Payment service providers register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. Money services businesses register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA. Many payment businesses are both, and the two registrations are linked: the Bank shares your application with FINTRAC and can refuse it based on what FINTRAC reports.

Read the guide RPAA

The RPAA annual report for payment service providers

Every payment service provider that performs retail payment activities in a calendar year must file an annual report with the Bank of Canada by March 31 of the following year. It covers your risk management and incident response framework, safeguarding, activity metrics and financial results.

Read the guide RPAA

Who must register as a PSP under the RPAA?

You must register with the Bank of Canada if you perform a payment function as a business in its own right, for electronic transfers in Canadian or foreign currency, with a link to Canada, and no exclusion applies. This guide shows how the Bank applies each part of that test to real business models.

Read the guide Apostille

Apostilling Canadian corporate documents for foreign banks and regulators

Foreign banks, payment partners and regulators often want Canadian corporate documents with an apostille. Some government certificates can go straight to the authority. Most corporate records must be notarized first.

Read the guide Apostille

How to get an apostille in Canada (2026 guide)

Since January 11, 2024, Canadian public documents going to Apostille Convention countries get an apostille. Who issues it depends on where the document was issued or notarized: Global Affairs Canada, or one of five provinces.

Read the guide FINTRAC

The travel rule and ministerial directives: what Canadian MSBs must do

Canada's travel rule requires money services businesses to send originator and beneficiary information with funds and virtual currency transfers, and to check it arrives with transfers they receive. Ministerial directives add stricter measures for transactions connected to Russia, Iran and North Korea.

Read the guide FINTRAC

FINTRAC administrative monetary penalties (AMPs) explained

FINTRAC can impose administrative monetary penalties for breaches of the anti-money laundering law. Amendments in force since March 26, 2026 raised the maximum amounts up to 40 times and added compliance agreements and compliance orders.

Read the guide MSB

MSB registration denied, revoked or expired: what happens and what to do

FINTRAC can deny an MSB application or revoke a registration for a short list of reasons set out in the Act. A registration that isn't renewed expires. Each outcome has its own deadlines, and operating without a valid registration is an offence.

Read the guide MSB

Can you use a virtual office for FINTRAC MSB registration?

FINTRAC's published guidance doesn't say whether a virtual office is acceptable. It does say what counts as a place of business in Canada, what the registration asks about your addresses, and that FINTRAC can check the information you give it.

Read the guide RPAA

RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds

Since September 8, 2025, a payment service provider that holds funds for its users must safeguard them, either in trust in a trust account or in a segregated account backed by insurance or a guarantee. It also needs a written safeguarding framework and an independent review at least every three years.

Read the guide Compliance programs

FINTRAC compliance program requirements

Every business FINTRAC regulates must establish and implement a compliance program. It has five parts: a compliance officer, written policies and procedures, a risk assessment, an ongoing training program and plan, and a review of the program at least every two years.

Read the guide After registration

The first 90 days of AML compliance after FINTRAC registration

Once a money services business in Canada is registered with FINTRAC, its obligations apply from the day it starts operating. This guide sets out a practical order for the first three months and the legal deadlines to put in your diary.

Read the guide MSB registration

FINTRAC MSB change of ownership: what to report and when

When a registered money services business changes hands, its registration stays with the company, but FINTRAC has to be told about the changes within 30 days.

Read the guide Acquisition

Ready-made FINTRAC MSB companies: what you get and what to question

A ready-made MSB is a Canadian company that's already registered with FINTRAC, sold through a share purchase. Here's what should come with one, and which sales claims don't hold up.

Read the guide MSB registration

MSB licence in Canada: what FINTRAC registration really is

People search for a Canadian "MSB licence". What FINTRAC actually gives a money services business is a registration, and the difference matters when you buy, bank or advertise one.

Read the guide Compliance officer

Outsourced or in-house compliance officer?

Every business FINTRAC regulates needs a compliance officer. You can appoint the owner or a manager, hire someone, or outsource the role, and FINTRAC's expectations are the same in each case.

Read the guide Acquisition

Buying an MSB in Canada: what to check

Buying a company that's already registered with FINTRAC as a money services business needs the same care as any acquisition, plus checks specific to FINTRAC and, for payment businesses, the Bank of Canada.

Read the guide Examination

Preparing for a FINTRAC examination

A FINTRAC examination usually starts with a call to your compliance officer and a letter asking for documents. Examinations can be done remotely or on site, and findings can lead to follow-up or a penalty.

Read the guide Effectiveness review

The two-year effectiveness review: a checklist

FINTRAC requires every reporting entity to review its compliance program at least every two years. The review should test how the program works in practice as well as what's written down.

Read the guide Virtual currency

Registering a crypto business with FINTRAC

Crypto exchanges and virtual currency dealers register with FINTRAC as money services businesses, whether they're based in Canada or serve Canadians from abroad.

Read the guide RPAA

What is the RPAA?

The Retail Payment Activities Act is the federal law under which the Bank of Canada supervises payment service providers. It sets rules on operational risk, incidents and the safeguarding of users' funds.

Read the guide Reporting

FINTRAC reporting for MSBs: which report, and when

Money services businesses report suspicious transactions, large cash, international transfers, large virtual currency receipts and listed person property to FINTRAC. Each report has its own trigger and deadline.

Read the guide Foreign MSB

Foreign MSB registration in Canada

A business with no office in Canada may still have to register with FINTRAC. It does if it directs and provides money services to people or entities in Canada.

Read the guide Costs

How much does MSB registration cost in Canada?

FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee to register a money services business. The costs come from criminal record checks, translations, documents and the compliance program you need to operate.

Read the guide MSB registration

How to register an MSB in Canada

A money services business must register with FINTRAC before it starts operating, and renew every two years. FINTRAC doesn't charge a fee, and it says the majority of complete applications are processed within three months.
