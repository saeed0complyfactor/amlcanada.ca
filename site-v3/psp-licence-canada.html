Is there a payment service provider licence in Canada?

Guide · Published 11 November 2026 · 6 min read · AML Canada

There is no payment service provider licence in Canada. A PSP covered by the Retail Payment Activities Act registers with the Bank of Canada, which lists it on a public registry and says it does not license or endorse PSPs. Depending on your business, FINTRAC registration, a Quebec licence or Payments Canada membership may also apply.

Key points

  • Canada has no PSP licence. In-scope PSPs register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA before they perform any retail payment activities.
  • Foreign PSPs that direct payment services at people in Canada and serve end users here must register too, and name an agent in Canada for notices.
  • A PSP that also remits or transmits funds usually needs FINTRAC MSB registration, and a funds transfer business in Quebec needs a Revenu Québec licence.
  • A Bank of Canada registration doesn't expire, but it can be revoked, and a change of control needs a new application first.

Is there a PSP licence in Canada?

No. The Bank of Canada's retail payments supervision FAQ (14 August 2026) answers the question "Do I need a licence to operate in Canada?" by saying PSPs must register with the Bank, which keeps a public registry "but does not provide PSPs with a licence". The registry page adds that the Bank "does not endorse or license registered PSPs".

Many people still search for a "PSP licence". In practice they usually mean a Bank of Canada registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (RPAA). It works the same way as the so-called MSB licence, which is a FINTRAC registration.

Registered PSPs have met the legal requirements to register, paid the required fees and cleared a national security screening coordinated by the Department of Finance, according to the registry page. They are then subject to the Bank's risk supervision and enforcement.

Who needs to register as a PSP with the Bank of Canada?

You must register if all four parts of the Bank's test apply. The test is set out in its supervisory policy Criteria for registering payment service providers (last updated 29 June 2026):

  1. You perform one or more of the five payment functions as a service that isn't incidental to another business activity.
  2. The function relates to an electronic funds transfer in a fiat currency or a prescribed unit.
  3. You have a place of business in Canada, or you both direct services at, and perform services for, individuals or entities in Canada.
  4. Neither you nor the activity is excluded.

The five payment functions in section 2 of the RPAA are providing or maintaining an account, holding funds, initiating a transfer at an end user's request, authorizing or transmitting transfer instructions, and providing clearing or settlement services. Section 9 excludes banks, authorized foreign banks, provincially regulated credit unions and caisses populaires, insurance companies and trust and loan companies, among others.

Section 23 of the RPAA says a PSP "must be registered with the Bank before it performs any retail payment activities". The Bank's FAQ says anyone applying after 8 September 2025 must receive a registration decision before operating. Our guide on who must register as a PSP works through the test with examples.

Which registrations, licences and memberships can apply to a PSP?

Four different registrations, licences or memberships can come up when people ask about a "PSP licence". They are issued by different bodies under different laws, and one business can need more than one. Only the first is required for every in-scope PSP.

Do foreign payment companies need a Canadian PSP registration?

Yes, if they serve the Canadian market. Section 5 of the RPAA applies the Act to a PSP without a place of business in Canada that directs retail payment activities at individuals or entities in Canada and performs them for end users in Canada. Section 29(1)(o) requires such an applicant to name an agent or mandatary in Canada authorized to accept notices and orders under the Act.

Working with a registered partner doesn't remove the duty. The Bank's FAQ says a foreign PSP doing business with a registered PSP is still "responsible for registering with the Bank". The exception in section 10 is narrow: it covers an agent or mandatary of a registered PSP acting within its authority and included on that PSP's list of agents.

Does a PSP also need FINTRAC MSB registration?

Often, yes. FINTRAC's money services businesses page treats remitting or transmitting funds as an MSB service, and says that invoice payment services and payment services for goods and services are engaged in remitting or transmitting funds or dealing in virtual currency. A payment business that does this needs FINTRAC registration before it starts operating in Canada.

The two registrations are linked. Section 29(1)(l) of the RPAA asks applicants to declare whether they are registered with FINTRAC, and section 31 requires the Bank to send FINTRAC the applicant's details once the application is complete. FINTRAC charges no registration fee, and it says that a provincial MSB registration or licence doesn't replace FINTRAC registration. See RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration for which business models need both.

How does Bank of Canada PSP registration work?

You apply online through PSP Connect, the Bank's web application for PSPs, and pay a one-time, non-refundable fee. Section 29(1) of the RPAA lists what the application must contain, including your names, address, structure, owners and directors, agents, a description of your retail payment activities, your risk management and incident response framework, how you safeguard end-user funds, and your material third-party service providers.

  • Fee. The Bank's registration application fee page set the fee at $2,500 as of 1 November 2024, indexed to the September CPI each year, with the new amount announced every 1 December. Its FAQ (14 August 2026) still describes a flat $2,500 fee. As of 11 November 2026, confirm the current amount on the Bank's site before you pay. The Bank evaluates an application only after the fee is paid in full.
  • Questions from the Bank. Section 29(3) gives you 30 days to answer the Bank's requests for additional information.
  • National security review. The Minister of Finance can review an application for national security reasons. The Bank's FAQ says the regulations prescribe 180 days for that review, which the Minister can extend, and section 37 bars the Bank from registering you while it is open.
  • Decision. Section 25 requires the Bank to register an applicant unless a refusal ground or a ministerial review applies, and to notify the applicant in writing.
  • Registry. Section 26 requires the Bank to publish each registered PSP's name, address and other prescribed information. The FAQ lists trade names, registration date, contact details, payment functions and agents.

The Bank's FAQ says a registration does not expire, but it can be revoked, for example if you stop performing retail payment activities or commit a violation. Under section 24, a registered PSP must file a new application before a planned acquisition of control or a prescribed change, and be registered on that basis.

What happens if you operate without registering?

The Bank's FAQ says failure to register is "a very serious violation" that may lead to a notice of violation and "a substantial administrative monetary penalty". Paragraph 101(1)(k) of the RPAA allows the regulations to set penalties of up to $10,000,000 for a violation. Section 93 requires the Bank to make public the nature of a violation, the name of the PSP and the amount of any penalty.

How should you describe your registration?

Say that the business is registered with the Bank of Canada as a payment service provider under the Retail Payment Activities Act, and point readers to its entry on the Bank's registry. Avoid "licensed by the Bank of Canada" or "approved by the Bank of Canada", since the Bank says it does not license or endorse registered PSPs. PSP licence vs registration: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Do I need a licence to process payments in Canada?

No licence exists for this. If your business performs payment functions that bring it within the Retail Payment Activities Act, you must register with the Bank of Canada before you perform any retail payment activities. The Bank says it does not provide PSPs with a licence.

How much does Bank of Canada PSP registration cost?

The Bank charges a one-time, non-refundable application fee. Its FAQ dated 14 August 2026 gives a flat $2,500 for all applicants, and its fee page says the amount is indexed to inflation each January. Confirm the current figure on the Bank's site before you apply.

Does a Bank of Canada PSP registration expire?

No. The Bank's FAQ says registration does not expire, but the Bank can revoke it. A registered PSP must also submit a new application before a planned acquisition of control or a prescribed change.

Is FINTRAC MSB registration the same as PSP registration?

No. They are separate registrations under separate laws. FINTRAC registers money services businesses under the PCMLTFA, and the Bank of Canada registers PSPs under the RPAA. Many payment businesses need both, and the Bank's application asks whether you are registered with FINTRAC.

Can a foreign PSP register without an office in Canada?

Yes. The RPAA covers foreign PSPs that direct retail payment activities at people in Canada and perform them for end users here. An applicant without a place of business in Canada must name an agent or mandatary in Canada to accept notices and orders.

RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration: do you need both?

Payment service providers register with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. Money services businesses register with FINTRAC under the PCMLTFA.
