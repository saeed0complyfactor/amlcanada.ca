KYC checklist for MSBs in Canada: what an onboarding file needs before review

Guide · Published 19 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

A KYC checklist for MSBs in Canada lists what a client onboarding file needs before the compliance officer can decide on it: verified identity, beneficial ownership, the politically exposed person and third party determinations, the sanctions check, the purpose of the relationship and a risk rating. Each item below is tied to its section of the Regulations or to FINTRAC guidance, as of 19 November 2026.

Key points

  • PCMLTFR s. 108 sets the identity record for each verification method, such as the document type, number and expiry date for photo ID.
  • A corporation's file needs its directors and everyone owning or controlling 25% or more (trustees, beneficiaries and settlors for a trust), the ownership and control structure and the confirming measures (PCMLTFR s. 138).
  • An MSB must take reasonable measures to make a PEP and HIO determination when it enters a business relationship (PCMLTFR s. 120(3)).
  • A high risk rating triggers the enhanced measures in PCMLTFR s. 157, so the file should show which ones were applied.

What should a KYC checklist for MSBs in Canada cover?

A KYC checklist for an MSB in Canada should cover eight items: how identity was verified, beneficial ownership for entities, the politically exposed person (PEP) and head of an international organization (HIO) determination, the third party determination, the sanctions and listed person check, the purpose of the relationship, the client risk rating, and enhanced measures for high risk.

The file starts with an identity trigger. Under PCMLTFR s. 95(1), an MSB must verify the identity of a person who asks it to initiate an electronic funds transfer of $1,000 or more, exchange $3,000 or more in foreign currency, or transfer or exchange $1,000 or more in virtual currency. Dealing in virtual currencies is an MSB service under PCMLTFA s. 5(h)(iv), so virtual currency dealers follow the same rules. FINTRAC's MSB identification guidance lists every trigger.

Some items attach to the business relationship. FINTRAC's guidance says an MSB enters one the second time it must verify a client's identity within five years, or when it signs a service agreement with an entity.

How must an MSB verify and record a client's identity?

An MSB verifies a person's identity using one of the methods in PCMLTFR s. 105(1) and keeps the record that s. 108 sets for that method. FINTRAC's methods guidance describes five methods for a person, including government-issued photo identification, the credit file method and the dual-process method.

For photo ID with a client who isn't present, FINTRAC says you need a process to authenticate the document and confirm the name and photo are the client's. Under s. 105(5), the document must be authentic, valid and current.

A corporation is verified from its certificate of incorporation or another current record showing its name, address and directors (s. 109(1)). For a public database, s. 109(5) requires a record of the registration number, record type and source. Under a service agreement, s. 109(4)(g) allows 30 days after the information record is created, and s. 37 lists the agreement records.

What beneficial ownership information does an entity file need?

For a corporation, PCMLTFR s. 138(1) requires the names of all directors, the names and addresses of everyone who owns or controls 25% or more of the shares, and information establishing ownership, control and structure. Trusts and other entities have their own lists in the same subsection.

Section 138(2) requires reasonable measures to confirm the information when first obtained. For a high-risk corporation under the Canada Business Corporations Act, you must also consult the information Corporations Canada publishes. If you can't obtain or confirm the information, s. 138(4) requires reasonable measures to verify the chief executive officer's identity, plus the special measures in s. 157.

FINTRAC's beneficial ownership guidance adds that where no one owns or controls 25%, you record the steps you took to reach that conclusion.

When does an MSB need PEP, HIO and third party determinations?

An MSB must take reasonable measures to determine whether a person it enters a business relationship with is a PEP, an HIO, a family member of one, or a close associate of a foreign PEP (PCMLTFR s. 120(3)). Section 120(1) adds certain transactions of $100,000 or more: sending or receiving an international electronic funds transfer, sending or receiving virtual currency, and transporting cash or negotiable instruments.

For a foreign PEP, s. 122.1(1) requires reasonable measures to establish the source of wealth, plus the special measures in s. 157. For a domestic PEP or HIO, those measures apply if you consider the risk high. Section 122.1(5) allows 30 days after the relationship starts. FINTRAC's PEP guidance for non-account-based sectors covers MSBs.

Under s. 136, when you create an information record, such as the one on an entity under a service agreement, you must take reasonable measures to find out whether the client acts for a third party. FINTRAC's third party guidance says asking the client is one such measure.

What must an MSB do about sanctions and listed persons?

The law sets prohibitions and disclosure duties. Under Criminal Code s. 83.08, no person in Canada may knowingly deal in property owned or controlled by or on behalf of a terrorist group, and s. 83.1 requires disclosure of such property without delay to the RCMP or CSIS.

When a disclosure is required under s. 83.1, the United Nations Act, the Special Economic Measures Act or the Sergei Magnitsky Law, PCMLTFA s. 7.1(1) also requires a report to FINTRAC. FINTRAC's listed person property guidance says your policies must explain your process to identify terrorist or sanctioned property, and it names the Canadian and UN lists to consult.

Suspected sanctions evasion goes on a suspicious transaction report, according to FINTRAC's sanctions evasion guidance. Our FINTRAC reporting requirements guide covers reports, and ministerial directives covers the Russia, Iran and North Korea directives.

How does the file show purpose, risk rating and enhanced measures?

Once a business relationship forms, PCMLTFR s. 145 requires a record of its purpose and intended nature. FINTRAC's MSB examples include foreign exchange for travel and funds transfers for family support. As a best practice, FINTRAC says the record should help you anticipate the client's transactions.

The client risk rating comes from your risk assessment under PCMLTFA s. 9.6(2) and PCMLTFR s. 156(1)(c)(i). FINTRAC's risk assessment guidance says to rate each relationship, or a logical group, and explain the rating. Building the method is covered in FINTRAC risk assessment for MSBs.

Where the risk is high, s. 9.6(3) and PCMLTFR s. 157 require written policies for enhanced measures to verify identity, keep client information current and monitor the relationship at a frequency that matches the risk. Monitoring after onboarding is in ongoing monitoring and transaction monitoring.

What to escalate to compliance and how the decision is recorded

When we act as your compliance officer, your operations point of contact posts the client file in a shared channel with the information we need. We run a customer risk assessment under your compliance framework and decide whether to approve, reject or request more information. Your team applies the decision and sends any request to the customer.

In our view, your procedures should name the files that go to compliance: a positive PEP or HIO determination, a possible listed person match, beneficial ownership you can't confirm, a suspected third party, or a high risk rating. We train the point of contact on what an escalated file needs to contain, so we have enough information to complete due diligence.

In our view, the review record should show the decision, the reason, the reviewer, the date and any information requested, next to the records the Regulations require. The full workflow is in how an outsourced compliance officer works, and the roles in the second line of defence. Our outsourced compliance officer service covers these reviews.

What is a KYC checklist for an MSB in Canada?

A list of what a client onboarding file should contain to meet the PCMLTFA, its Regulations and FINTRAC guidance: the identity record, beneficial ownership, PEP, HIO and third party determinations, the sanctions check, the purpose of the relationship, the risk rating and any enhanced measures.

When does an MSB enter a business relationship with a client?

FINTRAC's guidance says an MSB enters a business relationship the second time it must verify a client's identity within five years, or when it signs a service agreement with an entity.

What happens if an MSB can't confirm an entity's beneficial owners?

PCMLTFR s. 138(4) requires reasonable measures to verify the identity of the entity's chief executive officer, or the person in that role, and the special measures for high risk in s. 157.

Do MSBs have to check clients against sanctions lists?

The Criminal Code bars knowingly dealing in terrorist group property, and PCMLTFA s. 7.1 requires a report to FINTRAC when a disclosure is required. FINTRAC's guidance says your policies must explain how you identify terrorist or sanctioned property.
