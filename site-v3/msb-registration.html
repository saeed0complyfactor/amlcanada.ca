FINTRAC MSB registration in Canada

MSB registration with FINTRAC, from first assessment to decision.

Reviewed by Muhammad Saeed Abbasi, CAMS, ACMA, CGMA, MLRO and Managing Director, ComplyFactor Limited. Last checked 25 October 2026.

For money services businesses, foreign MSBs and virtual currency dealers. Fixed fee US$3,500.

Book a consultation Read on Fixed fee · US$3,500

Money services businesses (MSBs) must register with FINTRAC before they start operating, and so must foreign businesses that serve people in Canada.

FINTRAC, the Financial Transactions and Reports Analysis Centre of Canada, registers money services businesses under the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act (PCMLTFA). It's how foreign exchange dealers, money transfer businesses, virtual currency dealers and others become allowed to operate in Canada.

We work out whether and how registration applies to you, gather and check everything FINTRAC asks for, support you through submission and FINTRAC's questions, and prepare your compliance program at the same time.

Our fee is a fixed US$3,500. FINTRAC itself doesn't charge to register.

Who has to register

A business with a place of business in Canada must register with FINTRAC before it operates as a money services business, for example by dealing in foreign exchange, remitting or transmitting funds, or dealing in virtual currency. Registering or being licensed with a province doesn't replace FINTRAC registration. Whether your own activities are covered depends on how your business works, which is the first thing we confirm with you.

An MSB that holds funds for its users or transfers funds at their request may also need to register with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act. We cover that in RPAA registration.

Prefer a company that's already registered? Compare the two routes on our buy or register an MSB page.

Our guide on how to register an MSB in Canada lists every money service and walks through each step of the application.

Foreign MSB registration

A foreign MSB has no place of business in Canada but directs and provides money services to people or entities in Canada. It registers with FINTRAC before it starts and names a representative for service who resides in Canada; our foreign MSB guide covers the details.

What the application involves

FINTRAC asks for details of the business, its owners, senior management and compliance officer, its bank accounts, locations and agents, and its expected transaction volumes, with documents showing who owns and controls it. Criminal record checks are needed for the chief executive officer, the president, the directors and anyone who owns or controls 20% or more. A document that isn't in English or French needs a translation attested by a certified translator (PCMLTFA s.11.12(1.1)).

If FINTRAC asks for clarification, the answer is due within 30 days of the request, or FINTRAC can deny the application (PCMLTFA s.11.14). Registered businesses face the same 30-day limit, with revocation as the consequence (s.11.17).

How registration works with us

  1. Assessment. Which of your services are money services, whether you're an MSB or a foreign MSB, and what follows from that.
  2. Preparation. A checklist of everything FINTRAC needs, and help gathering and checking it. Criminal record checks from several countries often take longest, so we start them first.
  3. Submission. Your registration, submitted to FINTRAC with your approval.
  4. Follow-up. Answers to FINTRAC's questions, within its deadlines, until your registration is decided.
  5. Compliance program. Prepared at the same time as your registration, so you can operate once you're registered.

How long registration takes

FINTRAC says most complete applications are processed within three months. In our experience it has ranged from about 1 week to 7 months. The main factors are how complete and consistent the application is, how quickly criminal record checks arrive, and FINTRAC's workload at the time.

After you register

Registration is valid for two years and has to be renewed before it expires. Changes to your registration information must be reported to FINTRAC within 30 days.

After registration, an MSB also needs a compliance program, client identification, record keeping and reporting to FINTRAC. Since October 1, 2025, an MSB must also check that its agents and mandataries are eligible, including by reviewing criminal record checks, before engaging them.

Virtual currency dealers

Dealing in virtual currency is a money service, so crypto exchanges and dealers register with FINTRAC as MSBs, including foreign ones that serve people in Canada. Registered dealers also report virtual currency transactions of $10,000 or more within five working days. Provincial securities rules can also apply to some crypto trading platforms; we'll tell you if that's something you need separate advice on.

MSB registration: questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

How much does MSB registration cost?

FINTRAC doesn't charge a registration fee. Our fee to prepare and support your registration is a fixed US$3,500.

How long does FINTRAC registration take?

FINTRAC says most complete applications are processed within three months. In our experience it has ranged from about 1 week to 7 months, depending mostly on how complete the application is, how quickly criminal record checks arrive, and FINTRAC's workload.

Can a company outside Canada register as an MSB?

Yes. A foreign business that directs and provides money services to people in Canada registers as a foreign MSB and must name a representative for service who resides in Canada.

Do I need a compliance program before I register?

You need one to operate. FINTRAC asks for your compliance officer when you register, and your obligations start as soon as you begin. We prepare your program at the same time as your registration. Program documents are a separate fixed fee of US$3,500.

How often do I renew?

Every two years. Changes to your registration information have to be reported within 30 days.

Do crypto businesses register with FINTRAC?

Yes. Dealing in virtual currency is a money service, so crypto dealers and exchanges register as MSBs.

Guides on this

FINTRAC

FINTRAC revoked your MSB registration: a response plan

A revoked registration means the business can't offer the money services it was registered for. You have 30 days from receiving the decision to ask FINTRAC's Director to review it. Stop the registered services straight away, because the Act doesn't pause a revocation while a review runs.

Read the guide Crypto

Crypto custody in Canada: the rules and custody models compared

Canada has no single crypto custody licence. A platform that holds client crypto is expected to register with securities regulators. Under CSA pre-registration undertakings, which broadly match the terms registered platforms work under, client crypto is held in trust, mostly with an acceptable third-party custodian. Exchanging or transferring crypto for clients means FINTRAC registration. The Bank of Canada comes in where fiat payments are involved and, once the Stablecoin Act is in force, for stablecoin issuers.

Read the guide Virtual currency

Ontario vs B.C. MSB registration: which province suits a crypto startup?

Today there's no MSB difference between the two provinces. A money services business in Ontario or British Columbia registers with FINTRAC and nobody else, because B.C.'s Money Services Businesses Act has been passed but isn't in force. That should change: B.C. plans to start provincial registration, and Ontario has no equivalent law.
