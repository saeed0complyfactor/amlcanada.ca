Preparing for a FINTRAC examination

Guide · Updated 11 October 2026 · 3 min read · AML Canada

FINTRAC has been in touch, or soon will be. How examinations work, what's requested, and what to do first.

Key points

  • The examination starts on the date of FINTRAC's notification call.
  • The notification letter requests your program documents and records.
  • Reports filed after the notification date count as late.
  • Findings can lead to follow-up or an administrative monetary penalty.

On this page

  1. How a FINTRAC examination works
  2. Why the notification date matters
  3. What to do first
  4. After the examination
  5. Preparing before FINTRAC calls

How a FINTRAC examination works

FINTRAC's assessment manual describes three phases: planning and scoping, examination and assessment, and drawing conclusions and closing out. Examinations can be done remotely (a desk examination) or at your place of business (an on-site examination).

It usually starts with a call to your compliance officer, confirmed in a notification letter. The letter is FINTRAC's formal request for information: your compliance program documents and, where relevant, lists of transactions and records. FINTRAC treats the notification call as the start of the examination.

Why the notification date matters

Anything not reported or corrected before the notification date can count against you. A report filed after that date, for example, is treated as not having been filed when it was required. The time to fix your reporting is before FINTRAC calls, which is why a regular effectiveness review matters.

What to do first

  1. Read the letter carefully and note every deadline. Missing them is itself a risk.
  2. Gather your compliance program: policies and procedures, risk assessment, training program and records, and your last effectiveness review and its report to a senior officer.
  3. Pull the records requested, completely and in the format asked for.
  4. Brief the people involved, especially your compliance officer and anyone FINTRAC may interview.
  5. Be accurate. Don't create documents after the fact to fill gaps; explain what exists and what you're fixing.

After the examination

FINTRAC may send a findings letter to your compliance officer, covering the findings discussed at the exit meeting and listing deficient records. The outcome can be no further action, follow-up, a recommended enforcement action, or a notice of violation with a penalty. If an action plan is requested, it's typically due within 30 calendar days.

Administrative monetary penalties aren't automatic. FINTRAC considers the nature and extent of the problem, its root cause, mitigating and aggravating factors, and your compliance history. If you receive a notice of violation, you can make written representations within 30 days. FINTRAC publishes certain penalties on its website.

Preparing before FINTRAC calls

  • Keep your program current and approved by a senior officer.
  • Make sure every reportable transaction has been reported, and late reports are dealt with.
  • Keep training records and your last effectiveness review ready to send.
  • Close the findings from your last review or examination.

An outsourced compliance officer can keep this in order between examinations.

Questions

How much notice does FINTRAC give before an examination?

FINTRAC's guidance says the notification letter usually arrives 30 to 45 days before the examination date, sometimes longer for larger businesses.

Can FINTRAC examinations be done remotely?

Yes. Examinations can be desk examinations done remotely, or on-site at your place of business.

Does every deficiency lead to a penalty?

No. FINTRAC says penalties aren't an automatic response. It considers the nature, extent and root cause of the problem and your compliance history.
