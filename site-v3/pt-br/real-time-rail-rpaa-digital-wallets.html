Real-Time Rail access for PSPs and the RPAA digital wallet amendment

Guide · Published 3 November 2026 · 7 min read · AML Canada

Real-Time Rail access is now open to payment service providers (PSPs) registered under the RPAA, through Payments Canada membership, and Payments Canada targets the system's launch for Q4 2026. Separately, Bill C-15 adds a payment function for tokenized payment instruments and private keys, which, as of 3 November 2026, isn't in force.

Pontos principais

  • Since September 2025, the Canadian Payments Act lets RPAA-registered PSPs apply to become members of Payments Canada.
  • RTR participation needs RPAA registration, Payments Canada membership and a settlement route, in that order.
  • Payments Canada targets an RTR launch in Q4 2026, with onboarding phases running to Q3 2027.
  • The new tokenized-instrument and private-key payment function awaits an order in council, and the Bank's guidance on it is still pending.

Which two changes affect PSPs, and where do they stand?

The first change lets registered PSPs connect to Canada's national payment systems, including the Real-Time Rail (RTR). The second widens the RPAA's list of payment functions to cover wallet-style services. As of 3 November 2026, the first is in force, and the second is law but not in force.

Can non-bank PSPs join Payments Canada?

Yes. Paragraph 4(2)(i) of the Canadian Payments Act has entitled a payment service provider, as defined in the RPAA, that performs retail payment activities to Payments Canada membership since September 25, 2025, if it meets the requirements in the regulations and by-laws.

The paragraph was added by section 220 of the Fall Economic Statement Implementation Act, 2023 (S.C. 2024, c. 15), which was Bill C-59 and received Royal Assent on June 20, 2024. An order in council set the start date (section 230), and the Justice Laws consolidation shows the paragraph from September 25, 2025. Payments Canada announced on September 29, 2025 that the changes had been implemented.

Payments Canada's membership page says membership alone doesn't guarantee access to its payment systems. On October 1, 2026, Payments Canada said 22 new members had joined in the first year of expanded eligibility, including PSPs and credit union locals. Bank staff said at the Bank's 2 November 2026 supervision update that Payments Canada had begun welcoming a first wave of non-bank PSPs as participants and was still assessing and onboarding more.

What does a PSP need to participate in the Real-Time Rail?

Payments Canada's eligibility checklist lists four conditions. A PSP must be registered with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA, meet the PSP criteria, be a member of Payments Canada, and have a Bank of Canada settlement account or an agreement with a settlement agent. RPAA registration is the prerequisite for the whole path, so a business still waiting on the Bank's decision should plan around that wait.

Payments Canada describes the path in four stages:

  1. Prerequisite. RPAA registration.
  2. Membership. An application, a review by Payments Canada and approval by the Board, which typically meets in March, May, September and December.
  3. RTR participation. A separate participant application. Payments Canada gives two to three weeks from submission for conditional approval by its President, subject to onboarding and testing.
  4. Go-live readiness. Onboarding, testing and certification in the test environment, an attestation for final approval, then production testing and rollout.

A PSP can connect directly to exchange, clear and settle payments, or offer an RTR product through a participant. A direct participant can also sell connectivity to others. Payments Canada's RTR FAQ adds that each RTR participant must belong to an external complaints body and be registered with FINTRAC. Our guide to RPAA vs FINTRAC MSB registration explains the FINTRAC side.

For direct settlement, the Bank of Canada's settlement account policies (last updated July 31, 2026) require an applicant to be a member of Payments Canada, or eligible to become one, and to meet Payments Canada's operational, technical and security requirements. The applicant then emails the Bank for a form.

When does the Real-Time Rail launch?

Payments Canada's testing and launch page, checked 3 November 2026, targets a Q4 2026 launch with the first participants connecting directly to the RTR Exchange. Its target timeline then runs through 2027:

  • Q1 2027: the first participants migrating Interac e-Transfer clearing and settlement.
  • Q2 2027: more e-Transfer migration participants.
  • Q3 2027: all participants in the initial launch phases at full transaction volumes.

Canadian Payments Association By-law No. 10, the RTR By-law, was registered as SOR/2026-133 on June 18, 2026, and Payments Canada said the By-law and the RTR Rules took effect on August 24, 2026. Bank staff said at the 2 November session that the RTR is "expected to launch soon with a phase rollout continuing through 2027." Payments Canada hadn't announced the launch as of 3 November 2026.

In its July 2026 quarterly update, Payments Canada said there is no forced deadline for new participants to join. Each one goes live when it's ready, in the launch sequence.

What is the RPAA digital wallet amendment?

Section 604 of the Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1 (S.C. 2026, c. 3) adds a sixth payment function to section 2 of the RPAA: "the transmission or maintenance of an end user's encrypted or tokenized payment instrument or an end user's private key, whether or not the private key is encrypted or tokenized." The Act was Bill C-15 in the 45th Parliament and received Royal Assent on March 26, 2026.

The amendment itself doesn't use the words "digital wallet." The Library of Parliament's legislative summary of Bill C-15 (January 19, 2026) reads it as aimed at digital wallet providers and notes that this could include stablecoins. Bank staff at the 2 November 2026 update described it the same way. They said the fall 2025 budget clarified the scope of digital wallets, which have been "separated out into a distinct payment function."

The change sits in Division 45 of the Act, which enacts the Stablecoin Act. Budget 2025 said related RPAA amendments would let the government regulate PSPs that perform payment functions using prescribed stablecoins. Section 605 makes a second RPAA change: it adds any prescribed individual or entity to those a PSP must notify of a material incident under subsection 18(1), for incidents involving a unit that meets prescribed criteria.

Is the RPAA digital wallet amendment in force?

No. Section 606 of S.C. 2026, c. 3 says sections 601 to 605 come into force on a day or days fixed by order of the Governor in Council. The consolidated RPAA on the Justice Laws website, current to October 19, 2026, still lists sections 604 and 605 under "Amendments not in force." Until an order is made, the five existing payment functions remain the full list.

The Bank of Canada hasn't yet published guidance on the new function. Its registration criteria, last updated June 29, 2026, still explain the five existing functions. Bank staff said at the 2 November session that the Bank is finalizing updates to its guidance materials so they reflect the fall 2025 budget changes before those changes come into force. They added that the Department of Finance leads on the legislation and decides the timing. No in-force date had been announced as of 3 November 2026.

Which wallet businesses could the new payment function reach?

Any business that stores or passes on an end user's tokenized card, encrypted credential or private key should test its model against paragraph (f). Many wallet providers are already PSPs. The Bank's current criteria treat storing an end user's details for future transfers as providing or maintaining an account, and they treat encrypting or tokenizing a payment instruction as part of transmitting it. Our guide to who must register as a PSP covers the five existing functions and the four-part test.

Two limits still apply after the change. A payment function only makes an activity a retail payment activity when it relates to an electronic funds transfer in Canadian dollars, a foreign currency or a unit that meets prescribed criteria. And a business whose payment functions are incidental to another business still isn't a PSP. The Bank's updated guidance should show how far paragraph (f) reaches beyond today's functions, so record your analysis now and revisit it then.

What should PSPs do now?

Map your wallet features, plan any RPAA notices, choose an RTR route, confirm the RTR prerequisites and update your framework. None of these steps has to wait for either date:

  • Map wallet features. List each feature that transmits or maintains a token, encrypted credential or private key for an end user, and the existing payment function that covers it today.
  • Plan any filings. Before you perform a new retail payment activity, or make a change that could materially affect operational risk or safeguarding, RPAA section 22 requires notice to the Bank. Under section 20 of the Retail Payment Activities Regulations, the notice must reach the Bank at least five business days ahead.
  • Pick an RTR route. Decide whether to connect directly, through a third-party provider or through a participant, and whether to settle through your own Bank of Canada account or a settlement agent.
  • Check the RTR prerequisites. Confirm your RPAA registration, FINTRAC registration and external complaints body membership before you apply.
  • Update your framework. A 24/7 rail changes your availability targets, fraud monitoring and incident response, and any new connectivity provider is a third party you must assess. Our guide to RPAA compliance frameworks covers those requirements.

If you aren't registered yet, RTR access starts with the RPAA application. Our RPAA registration service scopes your payment functions, including wallet features, before preparing it.

Real-Time Rail and digital wallets: common questions

Ainda com dúvidas? Agende uma consulta ou escreva para info@amlcanada.ca.

Can PSPs join the Real-Time Rail?

Yes, a non-bank PSP can if it's registered with the Bank of Canada under the RPAA. It must then become a Payments Canada member, apply separately for RTR participation and arrange settlement through its own Bank of Canada account or a settlement agent.

When is the Real-Time Rail launching?

Payments Canada's testing and launch page, checked 3 November 2026, targets a Q4 2026 launch. Participants then join in phases, with all initial-phase participants at full volumes targeted for Q3 2027.

Is the RPAA digital wallet amendment in force?

No. Section 604 of the Budget 2025 Implementation Act, No. 1 (S.C. 2026, c. 3) takes effect only once the Governor in Council makes an order, and none had been made as of 3 November 2026.

Do digital wallet providers need to register under the RPAA?

Many already do, because storing an end user's details for future transfers counts as maintaining an account. Once in force, the new payment function will expressly cover transmitting or maintaining tokenized payment instruments and private keys.

As estruturas de compliance da RPAA de que um PSP precisa

Todo prestador de serviços de pagamento precisa de uma estrutura escrita de gestão de riscos e de resposta a incidentes, e o que mantém fundos de usuários finais também precisa de uma estrutura de proteção de fundos. RPAA vs. registro de MSB no FINTRAC: você precisa dos dois?

Prestadores de serviços de pagamento se registram no Banco do Canadá nos termos da RPAA. Empresas de serviços monetários se registram no FINTRAC nos termos da PCMLTFA. Muitas empresas de pagamentos são as duas coisas, e os dois registros estão ligados: o Banco compartilha seu pedido com o FINTRAC e pode recusá-lo com base no que o FINTRAC informar.
