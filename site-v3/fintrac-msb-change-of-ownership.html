FINTRAC MSB change of ownership: what to report and when

Guide · Updated 25 October 2026 · 2 min read · AML Canada

When a registered money services business changes hands, its registration stays with the company, but FINTRAC has to be told about the changes within 30 days.

Key points

  • Report changes to your registration information within 30 days.
  • Use FINTRAC's change form; FINTRAC may follow up with questions.
  • New owners and managers must not be ineligible under s.11.11.
  • A registered PSP is re-registered by the Bank of Canada before closing.

The 30-day rule

A registered MSB must keep its registration information up to date. When it changes, FINTRAC must be told within 30 days, using its change form: "Request to update registration information for a money services business or foreign money services business". See FINTRAC, Update, renew or cancel your registration.

A sale usually changes several things at once: the owners, the directors and senior officers, often the compliance officer, and sometimes the address, agents or services. Report them together if you can, and keep a record of what was sent and when.

Eligibility of the new people

Under section 11.11 of the PCMLTFA, some persons and entities can't be registered, for example a corporation whose director, CEO, president or 20%+ owner has been convicted of certain offences. If a change of ownership makes the business ineligible, its registration is revoked. FINTRAC asks for criminal record checks for the CEO, the president, each director and anyone owning or controlling 20% or more, with certified translations for any not in English or French. Start these for the incoming people before closing; checks from abroad take longest.

If FINTRAC asks questions

FINTRAC may send a clarification request about the changes. You have 30 days to respond. If a business doesn't answer, or doesn't report changes in time, its registration can be revoked or denied. For a foreign MSB, FINTRAC says registration will be revoked or denied if a change to the representative for service's name or address isn't reported within 30 days.

If the company is also a registered PSP

If the company is registered with the Bank of Canada under the Retail Payment Activities Act, a planned acquisition of control is different: the company must apply again and be registered before the acquisition closes (RPAA s.24). Allow time for this, because it can include a national security review. See the Bank of Canada's guidance.

A workable order of events

  1. Before signing: due diligence, criminal record checks for the incoming people, and any Bank of Canada re-registration.
  2. Closing: share transfer, new directors and officers appointed, corporate registers updated.
  3. Within 30 days: FINTRAC change form covering owners, directors, officers, compliance officer and any other changed details.
  4. Straight after: compliance program, risk assessment and policies updated for the new owners, and staff training if people changed.
  5. Diary the registration renewal date. Registration must be renewed before it expires.

Change of ownership: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

How long do I have to tell FINTRAC about a change of ownership?

30 days from the change, using FINTRAC's change form for MSB registration information.

Does the MSB need to register again after a share sale?

No. The registration stays with the company. The changes are reported to FINTRAC within 30 days. A company that buys the business's assets instead needs its own registration.

What happens if we report late?

FINTRAC says failing to keep registration information up to date can lead to administrative monetary penalties or other consequences, and registration can be revoked or denied.

What if the company is registered with the Bank of Canada?

Then an acquisition of control needs re-registration by the Bank of Canada before closing, under RPAA section 24.
