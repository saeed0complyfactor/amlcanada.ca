RPAA frameworks built around your payment flows.

Risk management, incident response and safeguarding frameworks for payment service providers. Fixed fee US$3,500.

Senior officer and governance

Clear roles and responsibilities, with a senior officer accountable for the frameworks, and enough resources to run them. We set out who owns each framework, how it's approved and reviewed, and how issues reach senior management.

Risk framework and registers

The Bank expects PSPs to identify their operational risks, protect against them, detect problems, and respond and recover, with periodic review and testing. We build your risk framework and the registers behind it: the risks in your payment activities, the controls that address them and who is responsible.

Incident response

When something goes wrong, your team needs to know what to do. The framework covers how incidents are identified, escalated, contained and recovered from. When you determine that an incident has a material impact, you notify the Bank and any affected end users, PSPs and clearing houses without delay, and no later than 48 hours after that determination.

Third parties and agents

Many PSPs depend on processors, banks, cloud providers and others, and some act through agents or mandataries. The framework covers how you assess and manage the risks they bring. Foreign PSPs also identify the agents or mandataries acting for them in Canada.

Safeguarding end-user funds

If you hold end-user funds, you must safeguard them, either in a trust account, or in a segregated account protected by insurance or a guarantee. Your written safeguarding framework explains how end users get timely access to their funds and how funds would be paid out if your business became insolvent. Our guide to RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds covers the requirements in detail.

Cyber, IT and continuity

Protecting the integrity, confidentiality and availability of your systems and data, detecting cyber threats, and keeping payments running, or restoring them quickly, when systems or providers fail.

Annual report and change notices

PSPs file an annual report with the Bank no later than March 31 of the following year. The Bank lists its contents as up-to-date information on risk management and incident response, safeguarding of end-user funds where that applies, and certain financial and activity metrics.

Changes to registration information are generally notified within 30 days after they happen, and certain changes at least 30 or 60 days before they take effect (Retail Payment Activities Regulations, ss.35 and 36). A significant change or new activity needs notice at least five business days before. An acquisition of control needs a new application, approved by the Bank before the acquisition closes (RPAA s.24). The framework sets out how you track, prepare and approve each of these.

Sources: Bank of Canada, Supervisory framework; Retail payments; Reminder: PSP reporting obligations under the RPAA; Acquisitions of control and prescribed changes; Retail Payment Activities Regulations. Checked 11 October 2026. General information, not legal advice.

RPAA compliance program: questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

What does the RPAA require from payment service providers?

Risk management and incident response frameworks, safeguarding of end-user funds where you hold them, incident notices within 48 hours of determining an incident is material, change notices, and an annual report by March 31.

How much does an RPAA compliance program cost?

A fixed US$3,500 for the program documents covering all seven areas.

Is this the same as our FINTRAC AML program?

No. The RPAA program covers operational risk and safeguarding under the Bank of Canada. A FINTRAC compliance program covers money laundering, terrorist financing and sanctions evasion. Many payment businesses need both.

Do you help us put the frameworks in place?

Yes. We write them around how you operate and walk your team through them. Ongoing support can be covered by outsourced compliance officer hours.

Guides on this

RPAA

RPAA incident reporting and the 48-hour rule

When a payment service provider becomes aware of an incident with a material impact, it must notify the Bank of Canada and the affected end users, payment service providers and clearing houses without delay. The Bank expects the first notice no later than 48 hours after the provider decides the incident is material.

Read the guide RPAA

The RPAA annual report for payment service providers

Every payment service provider that performs retail payment activities in a calendar year must file an annual report with the Bank of Canada by March 31 of the following year. It covers your risk management and incident response framework, safeguarding, activity metrics and financial results.

Read the guide RPAA

RPAA safeguarding of end-user funds

Since September 8, 2025, a payment service provider that holds funds for its users must safeguard them, either in trust in a trust account or in a segregated account backed by insurance or a guarantee. It also needs a written safeguarding framework and an independent review at least every three years.
