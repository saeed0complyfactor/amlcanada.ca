Can you use a virtual office for FINTRAC MSB registration?

Guide · Published 21 October 2026 · 4 min read · AML Canada

FINTRAC's published guidance doesn't say whether a virtual office is acceptable. It does say what counts as a place of business in Canada, what the registration asks about your addresses, and that FINTRAC can check the information you give it.

Key points

  • FINTRAC's guidance doesn't address virtual offices, so no address type is pre-approved or ruled out.
  • A physical location, Canadian incorporation, or staff, agents or branches in Canada make you a domestic MSB.
  • Your place of business address is published on FINTRAC's public MSB registry.
  • A foreign MSB needs a representative for service who resides in Canada. An address can't fill that role.

The short answer

We checked FINTRAC's pages on registering, on who is a money services business (MSB), on updating a registration and on the public registry. None of them mentions virtual offices, mail-forwarding addresses or shared workspaces. FINTRAC doesn't publish a list of acceptable address types, and it doesn't certify addresses.

So the useful question is narrower: does the address you give FINTRAC describe how your business really operates? The rest of this guide sets out what the law and FINTRAC's registration page ask for, so you can answer that before you file. If you haven't started the application yet, our guide on how to register an MSB covers the full process.

Your address decides which kind of MSB you are

The Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act draws one line. A business with a place of business in Canada that provides money services is an MSB under paragraph 5(h). A business without one, that directs money services at people in Canada and provides them to clients here, is a foreign MSB under paragraph 5(h.1).

FINTRAC's MSB page says you have a place of business in Canada if any of these apply:

  • you are incorporated in Canada;
  • you have a physical location in Canada; or
  • you have employees, agents or branches in Canada.

Two things follow. A company incorporated in Canada is a domestic MSB wherever its staff work. And a foreign business that sets up a physical location here stops being a foreign MSB. FINTRAC's guidance doesn't say whether a rented address with no staff on site counts as a physical location. If you're a foreign business, settle that point before you list a Canadian address anywhere. Our foreign MSB guide explains the foreign route in full.

What the registration asks about addresses

Schedule 1 to the Registration Regulations lists the information an application must contain. The address-related items are:

ItemPublic identifying information?
Address of place of businessYes
Mailing address of the place of business, if differentNo
Telephone number and websiteYes
Whether any money service is provided in a dwelling-houseNo
Address and telephone number of every agent, mandatary and branchYes
For a foreign MSB, contact details of the representative for service in CanadaNo

The Regulations make the information in Parts A and C of Schedule 1 public. That includes the place of business address, telephone number, website and the agent and branch details. FINTRAC's registry shows a business address and website for each listing. FINTRAC's registration page also asks for "detailed information about every location". If you're a corporation, your certificate of incorporation or equivalent record must show the corporation's name, address and directors, so that address should match what you file.

FINTRAC can check what you tell it

Subsection 54.1(4) of the Act lets FINTRAC verify the information in any registration application. FINTRAC says it sends a clarification request when information is inconsistent or can't be adequately verified, usually by email to the contact person or the representative for service. You have 30 days to answer. If you don't, FINTRAC may deny the application, or revoke the registration if you're already registered.

Consistency matters more than the type of address. Your incorporation records, bank account details, website, contact details and the address you list should tell the same story. Once you're registered, a change of address must be reported to FINTRAC within 30 days. Our guide to denied, revoked and expired registrations covers what happens when these steps are missed.

Foreign MSBs: the representative for service

A foreign MSB must name a representative for service. Under paragraph 11.12(1)(c.1) of the Act, that's a person who resides in Canada and is authorized to accept notices from FINTRAC on the business's behalf. FINTRAC needs their name, address for service and contact details.

The representative is an individual, so a business address can't serve as one. The representative also doesn't take on your obligations. FINTRAC states that the foreign MSB, and not its representative, is responsible for meeting the requirements of the Act.

If the representative's name or address for service changes and you don't tell FINTRAC within 30 days, FINTRAC must deny your application or revoke your registration (subsection 11.13(2)). Here the Act gives FINTRAC no discretion.

Make sure someone reads the mail

The Administrative Monetary Penalties Regulations say how FINTRAC can serve documents such as a notice of violation. For an entity, that includes leaving the document at its head office or place of business with a person who appears to be in charge, or sending it by registered mail or courier to the head office or place of business. A document sent by registered mail or courier is deemed served on the tenth day after the date on the postal or courier receipt.

Deadlines in the Act run from service. A notice of violation, for example, gives you 30 days to pay or make representations. If mail sent to your listed address isn't collected and read, those days can pass before anyone in the business knows. Whatever address you use, decide in writing who checks the mail and how quickly it reaches your compliance officer.

Before you list an address

  • Confirm whether you're a domestic or foreign MSB, using FINTRAC's place of business criteria.
  • Make sure the address matches your incorporation records, bank details and website.
  • Remember the place of business address will be public on the registry.
  • If services are provided from a home, say so: the form asks.
  • For a foreign MSB, appoint a representative for service who resides in Canada, and diarize the 30-day rule for changes.
  • Set up mail handling so FINTRAC notices reach your compliance officer quickly.

Virtual office and MSB registration: common questions

Still not sure? Book a consultation or write to info@amlcanada.ca.

Does FINTRAC accept a virtual office for MSB registration?

FINTRAC's published guidance doesn't address virtual offices. It asks for the address of your place of business, can verify what you provide and sends clarification requests when information is inconsistent or can't be verified. The address should reflect how your business actually operates.

Is my MSB's address made public?

Yes. The place of business address is part of the identifying information FINTRAC publishes on its MSB registry, along with agent and branch details.

Can a Canadian business address act as a foreign MSB's representative for service?

No. The representative for service is a person who resides in Canada and is authorized to accept FINTRAC's notices. A business address is a separate thing.

Does a Canadian address turn a foreign MSB into a domestic MSB?

FINTRAC says a physical location in Canada is a place of business, and a business with a place of business in Canada is a domestic MSB. FINTRAC doesn't say whether an address without staff counts, so get this confirmed before you list one.

Can I register an MSB that operates from home?

The registration asks whether any money service is provided in a dwelling-house, so it's something you disclose. Your address must still match your other records.
